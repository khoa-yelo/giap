    ADR-0058: The tolerant matcher may consume an annotated stop codon (option B of M7-16)¶

    • Status: accepted-by-khoa (2026-10-24, in session: "B"). Read-through residue in makedb patches = X (the proposed default, taken with B). Item 6 accepted-by-khoa (2026-10-24, in session: "Yes to 2"; built in M7-16 PR 16c, after 16b). Amendment 1 (Khoa, 2026-10-24: "sure"): placement-kind labels, under Consequences › Interfaces.
    • Date: 2026-10-24
    • Spec sections: §7.2.1 (six-frame index, decoys), §7.2.4 (route 3, the cost table, G4 completeness), §7.2.5 (placement FDR, G3), §7.2.6 (locus classes), §7.5.1, §7.5.3 G8 stop_codon, §7.6.3, §7.9.2 (makedb --patch), §11.6 TE1/TE2; ADR-0013 (cterm_extension, readthrough_candidate)
    • Needs Khoa: yes. It changes Module 2's search space and therefore what the placement FDR controls. Answered for items 1–5 ("B") and item 6 ("Yes to 2"), both 2026-10-24.

    Context¶

    Three accepted parts of the design assume a peptide can be placed across a genome stop codon: §7.5.3 G8 stop_codon ("the REF residue is a stop (e.g., Sec read through UGA)") and §7.5.1 ("Sec sites … appear as ALT calls"); ADR-0013 (cterm_extension evidence is "a peptide spanning the annotated stop", the readthrough_candidate label, and a patch row with a new end); and TE2's stop_codon exception (§11.6).

    The merged matcher cannot do any of this. The index writes * at every stop (§7.2.1). The exact automaton never matches across one. The M3-06 DP says "No operation may consume a target *" (tolerant_dp._verify_core gates every move on t1 < 19), and the frameshift route and the M7-01 re-alignment (variants.align._residues raises on a genome *) do the same. So in v1 as built, stop_codon and cterm_extension calls are unreachable (M7-05 rule 6, M7-09 rule 4, M7-11b), and M7-18 lead decision 1 handed the Sec/Pyl ALT rule to this ADR.

    M7-16 listed three options (A leave it, B annotated-stop substitution, C any-stop substitution). Khoa chose B.

    What B reaches, checked on the Nissle recording (tests/data/recorded/bakta/1.12.2-full/nissle, Bakta 1.12.2, full DB; all four replicons circular; the review's mass numbers, job 1889319, came from a local run with linear replicons, local/recordings/bakta_full/nissle1917, and job 1889965 reproduces them on this recording). There are 4,692 CDS and sORF features. 4,689 end in a stop (TAA 2,943, TGA 1,401, TAG 345); the other 3 are partial genes at plasmid edges with no stop as their last codon (JMJJOG_04967, JMJJOG_04968, JMJJOG_04975), counted as not_a_stop (corrected 2026-10-24 by the 16a implementation). No two features share a stop codon, and none crosses an origin. The 4 TE1 misses (M3-22) are the selenocysteine peptides of the two fdnG copies (JMJJOG_02511 −, JMJJOG_04733 +): VUHGPTVASLAPTFGR, VUHGPTVASLAPTFGRGAMTNHWVDIK, SLGMLAVDNQARVUHGPTVASLAPTFGR and ALGMLAVDNQARVUHGPTVASLAPTFGR. Bakta annotates both copies as full-length selenoproteins (1,015 and 1,016 aa), with U at residue 196 over an internal TGA: [2578627, 2578630) − and [4825395, 4825398) +. That TGA is not the last codon of the feature. So the option-B rule as worded does not reach the fdnG peptides. It reaches C-terminal read-through (cterm_extension) and any residue read at an annotated terminal stop. Item 6 states what reaching fdnG would take.

    Decision¶

    1. Consumable stops. The tolerant DP (route 3, M3-06/M3-07) may consume a target * only at the annotated stop codon of a CDS or sORF. That is the last codon of the feature (Gene.interval, feature_type in {cds, sorf}), on the feature's strand, in the feature's frame. It may consume it only by a substitution (one query residue against the *), at cost 1, recorded as diff_types substitution with REF *. The rule does not depend on the codon (TAA, TAG and TGA alike). A match is never possible: no query code equals a stop. Swaps, blocks and near-blocks never consume a stop, consumable or not. Every other * and every X stays unconsumable. Pseudogenes are excluded. So is a feature whose last codon is not a stop (for example a partial gene at a contig edge); it is counted in an event. On circular replicons the codon is marked at every copy in the origin-extended frames, including a codon that crosses the origin.
    2. Decoys. Each decoy search carries the same rule at the mirrored positions. For a reverse decoy frame of n residues, target position p becomes n − 1 − p (mirror_aa). For a shuffle decoy it stays p, because stops keep their positions. Both decoys keep a * at the mirrored cell, so a target and its decoy expose the same number of consumable cells per frame. Decoy hits stay counted only, never placed.
    3. Routes. The exact route is unchanged. The frameshift route (M3-08) never consumes a stop in either side. Its precedence check ("a non-frameshift alignment at cost ≤ the split's overlaps the split locus") does see consumable-stop alignments, so a split never coexists with a cheaper or equal read-through alignment of the same locus. M7-01 re-alignment uses the same set and rule. The recomputed cost must still equal n_diff.
    4. Variants. A substitution op with REF * is an ALT site with flag stop_codon (G8; M7-05 rule 6 as written). The snv/multi_nt class comes from the codon (for example W over TGA → snv). U over TGA and O over TAG are the standard decodings (Sec, Pyl). Per §7.5.1 they are ALT calls with stop_codon, zero nucleotide changes, neither snv nor multi_nt, no VCF record (ALT codon = REF codon) and flag vcf_unrepresentable. This replaces the M7-18 rare skip for exactly those two cases. Every other U/O, and every B/J/X/Z, is skipped as M7-18 does today.
    5. makedb --patch residue: X. A patch CDS with Note=cterm_extension translates the annotated stop codon of its locus_tag gene as X. Search engines treat X as any residue, which covers Sec (U), Trp (W) and Gln/Tyr read-through. Any other internal stop in any patch CDS still raises, as M3-23 does.
    6. Accepted by Khoa (2026-10-24), built in 16c. The consumable set also includes annotated recoded codons: an internal in-frame codon of a CDS whose Bakta protein has U over TGA or O over TAG at that residue. That is 3 sites in Nissle (fdnG ×2, fdhF). Decoys would be mirrored as in item 2. This is what makes the fdnG TE1 misses placeable. Until 16c merges, the code builds only item 1's set.

    Evidence¶

    • Search-space growth (an approximation, not a bound). A window can consume a marked stop only if it contains it: L windows per stop, with the other L − 1 residues within m − 1 differences. The added chance-hit mass is approximately G·L·Σ_{j<m} C(L−1, j)(1−s)^j s^(L−1−j), against E(L, m) of §7.2.5. In closed form the ratio is approximately G/(N(1 − s)) at m = 1 and 2G/(N(1 − s)) at m = 2, independent of L. It scales with the gene density G/N. In the Appendix B setting (N = 10⁷ six-frame positions, s = 0.069, G = 5,000 consumable stops, so G/N = 5 × 10⁻⁴) that is about 0.054 % and 0.107 %:
    L, m E(L, m) added by B (approx.) ratio (approx.)
    10, 1 3.3 × 10⁻³ 1.8 × 10⁻⁶ 0.05 %
    11, 1 2.5 × 10⁻⁴ 1.4 × 10⁻⁷ 0.05 %
    12, 2 1.4 × 10⁻³ 1.5 × 10⁻⁶ 0.11 %
    16, 2 5.8 × 10⁻⁸ 6.2 × 10⁻¹¹ 0.11 %

    The formula counts all N positions, stops included, and spreads chance hits evenly; against the realised searchable mass both sides lose stop-containing windows. Measured realised shares (exact expected chance mass, statistician, Slurm jobs 1889319 and 1889320, local/stat/m7-16-review.md): 0.04–0.08 % of the target chance mass on Nissle (i.i.d. and tryptic laws, m = 1 and 2), and 0.05–0.20 % on three giapsim 1-Mb genomes (the top of the range under the tryptic law at m = 2). On another genome the share is approximately, not at most, the formula's value for its G/N.

    Option C, with about 5 × 10⁵ stops in 10⁷ positions, would add about 100× this, and that mass would sit at every stop of every frame. - Symmetry. By construction (item 2), each decoy frame has exactly the target's number of consumable cells. Each such cell holds * and is mirrored one to one. The tests in M7-16 make this a property test. The reverse decoy is exact under any i.i.d. peptide law (measured D_rt/T_rt = 1 to 1e-16). The local context is mirrored, not kept (gene | * | flank becomes flank | * | gene), so under the tryptic law the per-genome read-through D_rt/T_rt was 0.52–1.89 (giapsim) and 0.81–1.15 (Nissle) for reverse, and 0.58–1.74 and 1.14–1.42 for shuffle. The measured net effect on the total, (D_rt − T_rt)/T_regular, was −9.4 × 10⁻⁴ in the worst giapsim replicate. D_rt/T_rt is not an FDR for the read-through subset and is never reported as one. - Calibration. Before/after numbers come from the re-runs that M7-16 requires (fast + full, TE1/TE2/TE8). Only the exercised suites are evidence for this rule: the run_chain suites test_placement_fdr_sim.py, test_denovo_precision_sim.py and attack 5, plus the M7-16 stressed arm (test_readthrough_stressed.py) and the statistician's attack (attacks/test_attack_readthrough_decoys.py). For each exercised suite this section records the per-replicate number of accepted target rows that consumed a cell. The regression-only suites (test_tolerant_recall.py, test_placement_fdr_genome.py, test_placement_fdr_lists.py, attacks/test_attack_placement_*) are byte-identical by construction (cells=None or no matcher) and are not cited as evidence. This section is filled in with the Slurm job IDs when 16a merges.

    Consequences¶

    • Module 2 search space. Route 3 grows by at most one consumable cell per annotated gene and search (≈ 4,700 on Nissle), adding approximately G/(N(1 − s)) (m = 1) to 2G/(N(1 − s)) (m = 2) of the chance-hit mass; measured 0.04–0.08 % on Nissle and up to 0.20 % on giapsim (above). Peptides of 7–9 aa never reach route 3 (min_len 10), so a stop-spanning peptide shorter than 10 aa is never placed. At 10–11 aa (k = 1) the stop is the only allowed difference. With no annotated genes the matcher is byte-identical to main.
    • Placement FDR (G3). The estimator, the strata, π̂ (m ≥ 1 → 1) and the ranking score E are unchanged. Read-through placements are tolerant hits at m ≥ 1, so same_db exact skipping never applies to them. Validity rests on item 2. No tolerance, bound, α or R changes (R3).
    • Re-calibration required. The M3-21 placement suites must re-run, fast and full, with no tolerance change. A failure stops the merge and goes to a new ADR, never to a tolerance change. Regression-only (byte-identical by construction): test_tolerant_recall.py, test_placement_fdr_genome.py, test_placement_fdr_lists.py, attacks/test_attack_placement_*. Exercised: test_placement_fdr_sim.py, test_denovo_precision_sim.py and attack 5. Even the exercised suites see few cells at 100 kb to 1 Mb, so M7-16 adds a required stressed arm (dense short annotated genes, ≥ 100 expected false target read-through rows per replicate, reverse and shuffle arms, a negative control without decoy cells that must fail), a decoy-balance check (se_multiplier, one-sided, may be vacuous) and the statistician's exact-mass attack, which 16a's merge waits for. TE8 (Nissle pooled FDR ≤ 0.01) re-runs weekly as usual.
    • TE1. Unchanged under items 1–5. The fdnG Sec site is internal, so the 4 misses stay unmapped. Under item 6 (16c) they become tolerant placements (cost 1) flagged sec_site. They are still not exact, so they stay TE1 exceptions, now labelled as Sec.
    • TE2 and G8. The stop_codon flag becomes reachable. TE2's second exception is exercised only when an annotated stop or (item 6) a recoded codon carries a placed peptide. On Nissle same-strain peptides, items 1–5 add no site. Item 6 would add two candidate U sites at the fdnG codons, one unique peptide each (the 16-mer and 27-mer are shared between the copies), and no high site.
    • cterm_extension. It is reachable through route 3: a peptide in frame with a CDS/sORF that crosses its annotated stop into the 3′ flank (M3-14 _cterm, unchanged). M7-09's class rule becomes live (a spanning peptide plus an in-frame continuation; readthrough_candidate). Under M7-11 decision 2 the class stays unestimated, so it is called only with ≥ 2 distinct peptides. M7-07 writes its patch row, and makedb --patch translates the old stop as X.
    • Interfaces. sixframe_index gains the genes input and writes index format 2, which carries the consumable cells for the target and each decoy type. No config key, no new column and no new diff type. Amendment 1 (Khoa, 2026-10-24) adds labels, not method:
    • the placement flag readthrough (16b), and sec_site/pyl_site (16c), in the §9.4 vocabulary through the schema;
    • the GFF3 attributes readthrough_codon and recoded_codon;
    • an optional sixframe input to loci;
    • placement-kind count rows in detection_summary.tsv (section qc).

    The other flags (stop_codon, vcf_unrepresentable, readthrough_candidate) already exist. See the M7-16 spec, "Amendment: 16b reporting and the 16c labels". - Backlog. "M7-16: decide stop_codon and mass_equivalent for blocks and swaps whose REF contains *" closes: no block or swap ever consumes a stop.

    Alternatives¶

    • A. Leave it. No cost. But G8 stop_codon, ADR-0013's evidence and patch row, and TE2's second exception stay dormant or vacuous, and PROGRESS.md must keep saying so. Rejected by Khoa.
    • C. Any-stop substitution. About 150 lines, but every stop in every frame becomes a cost-1 chance-hit site: roughly 100× the added mass above, concentrated next to stops. The §7.2.5 / Appendix B.2 chance-hit table and the placement-FDR calibration would need re-derivation. Every pseudogene and frame-shifted ORF would also become a "read-through". Rejected.
