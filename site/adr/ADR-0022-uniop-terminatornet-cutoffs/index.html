    ADR-0022: UniOP and TerminatorNet cut-offs and the distance-model refit on RegulonDB 14.5.0 K-12¶

    • Status: accepted-by-khoa (2026-10-23, max-F1; M6-12 lead decision 10; 2026-10-24, UniOP pair_threshold 0.5)
    • Date: 2026-10-23 (run 2026-10-24 01:05 UTC, Slurm 1879748)
    • Spec sections: §15.4 steps 3–5, §7.1.3, §7.1.5, §7.4.5, §7.4.6, §7.4.7; spec docs/specs/M6-12-uniop-terminator-cutoffs.md with lead decisions 1–10; methods docs/methods/operons.md › "K-12 cut-off calibration (M6-12)"
    • Needs Khoa: yes (a protected file, src/giap/data/calibration.yaml, and two defaults change). Khoa decided the cut-off rule on 2026-10-23: max-F1 as §15.4 specifies, no one-SE rule.

    Context¶

    §15.4 asks for three numbers from RegulonDB on K-12 MG1655, recorded in calibration.yaml with the RegulonDB version, date, AUROC, precision and recall: 1. the UniOP AUROC and pair cut-off: "the smallest threshold with precision ≥ 0.90 (if none reaches 0.90, the threshold with maximum F1)"; 2. the distance-model refit, p(operon | d) = 1/(1 + exp((d − d₀)/s)) (§7.1.3, placeholder 50 / 20); 3. the TerminatorNet confidence cut-off, by the same rule, with precision against RegulonDB terminators within ±50 bp on the same strand.

    Release of record: RegulonDB 14.5.0 (2026-01-28), as Khoa decided on 2026-10-21 (§15.3 says "v12"). Until now calibration.yaml held the placeholders (d0: 50, s: 20, terminatornet_yes: 0.5, both calibrated: false), every run carried the flags uncalibrated:operon_distance and uncalibrated:terminatornet_yes, and annotation.uniop.pair_threshold was the uncalibrated 0.5.

    The cut-off rule follows lead decision 1 (the stable reading of "smallest t with precision ≥ 0.90": every candidate at or above t* has precision ≥ 0.90), with max-F1 as the fallback, ties to the smallest t. Khoa's decision 10 keeps max-F1 exactly as §15.4 specifies; the one-SE rule the statistician raised for TerminatorNet's flat F1 curve (methods R3) is not adopted, and the flatness is reported below as context only.

    • The precision CI holds t* fixed: it is a CI for the precision of the threshold 0.529415, not for the precision achieved by the rule. The rule chose t* because the observed precision there is ≥ 0.90, so the point estimate is optimistic; the cross-validated precision of the rule (0.9001) puts the optimism at about 0.002, against a CI half-width of 0.017.
    • t* itself is known only to about ±0.08 (the re-selection quantiles); the precision of the cut-off is what is stable.
    • Precision 0.90 is cumulative, not per-pair: it is the share of operon pairs among all pairs with p ≥ t*. Pairs just above the cut-off (p in [0.529, 0.65), 122 pairs) are operon pairs 49 % of the time.
    • pair_threshold is K-12-tuned. Precision depends on prevalence (K-12 labelled π = 0.632): at the same TPR / FPR, a genome with π = 0.5 would see about 0.84 and one with π = 0.4 about 0.78. AUROC is prevalence-free but not genome-free.
    • UniOP's training data: unknown here (not verified in M6-12). If UniOP's development used K-12 operons from RegulonDB, this AUROC is partly in-sample for the tool.
    • Khoa's decision, 2026-10-24: UniOP uses 0.5. On the K-12 curve 0.5 gives precision 0.896, recall 0.853 and F1 0.8739 (TP 1,476, FP 172), against 0.902 / 0.848 / 0.8740 at the precision_0.90 threshold 0.529415: the same operating point, so the round value is kept and rule is recorded as khoa_fixed_0.5. Khoa also asked whether TerminatorNet could use 0.5; it cannot without a large change. At 0.5 every eligible candidate is called (recall 1.0), FP rises from 563 to 2,154 and precision falls from 0.226 to 0.120, close to the 0.091 coincidence rate, so TerminatorNet keeps the max-F1 value.
    • Context for decision 10, not the recorded value. The max-F1 threshold on the UniOP curve is 0.25114 (F1 0.8743, precision 0.848, TP 1,562, FP 281), against F1 0.8740 at 0.529415. §15.4 applies max-F1 only when no threshold reaches precision 0.90; UniOP reaches it, so the recorded rule is precision_0.90. F1 is flat here too: every candidate from 0.069 to 0.707 is within 1 % of the maximum.

    • RegulonDB is incomplete, so this precision is a lower bound, except for coincidental matches: a false call can land within 50 bp of a listed terminator by chance, because both concentrate just past stop codons. The offset-preserving placement null puts that chance at c = 0.091, and the coincidence-corrected bound is 0.148 (0.115 at the window-bootstrap 97.5 % lower limit). The true precision of terminatornet_yes on K-12 lies above about 0.115 with 97.5 % confidence; RegulonDB cannot say how far above.
    • The 0.90 target is unreachable against RegulonDB: the best precision at any candidate is 0.229. terminatornet_yes is a max-F1 cut-off, not a 90 % cut-off, and carries no precision guarantee on any genome.
    • Recall is low by construction: RegulonDB includes Rho-dependent terminators, which TerminatorNet does not model.
    • Flat F1 (context only; Khoa decided max-F1, 2026-10-23). F1 rises from 0.214 at 0.5 to 0.310 at 0.9 and is flat above: 0.314 at 0.99, 0.313 at 0.999, 0.315 at 0.99999, maximum 0.322 at 0.9999999999961204. The 45 candidates within 1 % of the maximum all lie in [0.9999999999689556, 0.9999999999999982]; the 210 within 5 % lie in [0.8814, 1.0]. The argmax therefore sits on saturated network outputs, and its decimals beyond about the 4th are noise; on another genome or TerminatorNet build the float pattern near 1.0 can differ. The value is recorded exactly, which is correct for K-12. A one-SE rule was considered and not adopted (decision 10).
    • The rule's F1 is call-level (recall = TP over all matched eligible calls), as specified; the reported recall is truth-level.

    Consequences¶

    • One genome, one truth set: every CI here is over resampled directons (or windows) of K-12 and describes K-12's uncertainty, not genome-to-genome transfer. d₀ and s transfer worse than AUROC (β₀ absorbs K-12's prevalence and intergenic statistics).
    • Label noise in RegulonDB biases AUROC and precision towards the base rate (conservative); study bias towards well-studied operons may do the opposite.
    • A later RegulonDB release reruns the driver into regulondb/<release>/calibration/ and needs a new ADR.
    • PR-C needs test changes beyond C0 (lead work, role guard). With the proposed values, the following tests fail; none is a statistical failure, all pin placeholders or the two-key set:
    • tests/unit/test_operons.py::test_calibration_yaml_entries converts every non-calibrated, non-provenance field to float, so the spec's string fields regulondb_version, date and rule raise ValueError (spec § "What changes in protected files" vs C0's test). Either the test skips string metadata or the yaml folds them into provenance.
    • tests/unit/manifest/test_content_blocks.py::test_calibration_lists_both_bundled_keys_and_flags_them and tests/integration/test_mvp03c_manifest_content.py::test_calibration_block[local|nextflow] pin the key set to {operon_distance, terminatornet_yes} and (the first) require both uncalibrated:* flags.
    • tests/unit/test_operon_steps.py (4 tests: ..._falls_back[empty|header_only], ..._without_uniop_pred_falls_back, ..._uses_uniop_and_distance_model_for_wrap) pin the distance-model probabilities of d₀ = 50, s = 20.
    • With the terminatornet_yes mirror at 0.9999999999961204, tests that read the default implicitly with toy confidences such as 0.9: tests/unit/operons/test_flags.py (10 tests), tests/unit/novel/test_novel_evidence.py (2) and tests/unit/novel/test_novel_evidence_review_180.py (1). The spec's last acceptance criterion asks PR-C to list these; they should pass explicit thresholds.
