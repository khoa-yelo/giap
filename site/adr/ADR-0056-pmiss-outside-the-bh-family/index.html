    ADR-0056: P(miss) is reported without a q and stays out of the discordance BH family¶

    • Status: accepted-by-khoa (2026-10-24, in session: "Accept it", after the P(miss) interpretation was explained)
    • Date: 2026-10-24
    • Spec sections: §7.4.3 "One detected, one not" and "Multiple testing", §8.1 row "Operon discordance", §8.5, §9.3 pairs.tsv
    • Needs Khoa: yes. It departs from §7.4.3's "BH across all tested OP pairs (both kinds together)" and from the §8.1 row, which names the detection-limit model as part of the discordance family.

    Context¶

    §7.4.3 tests two kinds of OP pair. A pair with both genes detected gets a two-sided Student-t p-value against the within-operon Δ distribution. A pair with exactly one gene detected gets p = P(miss): the probability, under M6-02's detection-limit model, that the undetected partner u would be missed, given the detected partner's abundance a_v and the empirical within-operon ratios. The spec then puts both kinds into one BH family at q ≤ 0.1.

    P(miss) is a model probability of an event that has already happened. It is computed only on the pairs where u was missed, so conditional on entering the family it is not super-uniform under any null. M6-index lead decision 1 and M6-02 lead decision 4 therefore reported P(miss) with no q and kept it out of the BH family, pending M6-09 attack C and an ADR. M6-05H (PR #311) implements that: a one_detected_pmiss row has p_value = P(miss), method = detection_limit, null_model = p_detect_model and q_value NA, and the BH family holds only the both_detected_t rows.

    M6-09 recorded attack C with the oracle P(miss) (the generating model). This ADR adds attack C through the step, with the M6-02 model fitted (tests/calibration/attacks/test_attack_operons_pmiss.py).

    Decision¶

    1. P(miss) stays in pairs.tsv p_value for test = one_detected_pmiss rows, as §7.4.3 and §9.3 name it, with method = detection_limit and null_model = p_detect_model. It gets no q and never enters the BH family; operon_flags.tsv makes no discordance call from it.
    2. The §8.1 discordance family is every both_detected_t row of the run, one BH family at stats.alpha.operon_flags.
    3. The report and the methods note describe P(miss) as "the model's probability that this partner would be missed if it were co-expressed", never as a significance level, and say that it is anti-conservative when detectability varies between proteins in ways the model does not see.
    4. A scored one_detected_pmiss row carries ci_unestimated, as the both_detected_t rows do. Its effect and CI stay NA (M6-05 item 4), and §8's "effect sizes with 95 % CIs accompany every p or q" is then met by the flag rather than silently.
    5. Pooling is revisited only with a method that makes the one-detected arm a valid p-value under protein-level detectability heterogeneity. For example, a model with a per-protein random effect, or a replicate-model P(miss) where runs identify that effect. Any such method must pass the gene_effect arms of attack C with the pooled family's any-call share within the §8.4 bound.

    Evidence (attack C through the step; fitted M6-02 model)¶

    Setup: operon_null(1800) genomes (TU blocks and the OPERON_T within-TU walk). Detection is redrawn at peptide level, with logit = −11.5 + 0.5 a − 0.03 L + 0.8 GRAVY + o_g, and a gene is detected at ≥ 1 seen peptide (k_min = 1). C11 holds the seen peptides, and P(miss) comes from giap.steps.operons.detection_limit → discordance. Arms: - null: o_g = 0; - gene_effect: o_g ~ N(0, 1), a per-protein detectability the model does not see; - gene_effect_05: o_g ~ N(0, 0.5²); - block: o_g = −1.5 on the ADR-0018 prophage block of operon_attack_detection.

    Full tier: Slurm 1884524, R = 300 genomes per arm, 2,331 s on rye-01. It ran at commit 65d9fedd on branch stat/m6-05h-attack-c: #311 round 1 (fb1410cf, u scored with u's TU model) merged into #310 (bbd6d4a4). The fast tier (R = 20) was rerun on #311's review round (4551ab61: v's TU, the k_min event) and reproduces the round-1 fast numbers to every printed digit. - On these simulations OP pairs never cross a TU boundary (pure UniOP classes), so the v-TU fix cannot move them. - At k_min = 1, "fewer than k_min observable peptides seen" is the simulated non-detection, so the event fix cannot move them either. - On real data, OP pairs that cross a C5 TU boundary can move.

    Rates are at α = 0.01 / 0.05 / 0.10. The step's P(miss) matches an independent recomputation to ≤ 6·10⁻¹⁶ on every row.

    • Pooled sizes: 65,738 to 79,281 one-detected rows and 260,456 to 347,647 at-risk orientations per arm.
    • In every arm the oracle (the true model with its offset, Δ from the generating t) is calibrated: recalibration slope 0.96 to 0.99, intercept within ±0.03.
    • The step's ratio sample (both-detected Δ only) costs nothing: with the true model it gives a mean P(miss) equal to the oracle's to 3 decimals. The departures are all in the fitted model.
    • The any-call bound for R = 300 is 0.1 + 2 SE + 1/R ≈ 0.138. Flags per genome at P(miss) ≤ 0.05 are 6.4 (null), 14.8 (SD 0.5), 52.3 (SD 1) and 8.1 (block).

    Reading: - Correct model: P(miss) is a calibrated probability. As a discrete p-value of the event "u missed", it is conservative over the at-risk pairs. The share of flagged one-detected pairs is below α, and pooling would not break the family on this arm. - Protein-level detectability breaks it. With o_g ~ N(0, 1) the fitted P(miss) is roughly half the true miss rate. The conditional flag share is several times α, the per-pair rate of the pooled mixture exceeds α, and the pooled BH family makes a false call in most genomes; the shipped t family does not. - The mechanism is the conditional (Huggins) likelihood. It assumes that detectability is a function of the three features alone. Genes that were detected have high o_g, so the model over-predicts detection for the genes that were not. - Real proteomes have such heterogeneity: ionisation, digestion, membrane location and peptide sharing all vary beyond length and GRAVY. - Spatially clustered detectability (block) biases P(miss) downward more mildly (intercept +0.24). It already takes the pooled family's any-call share to 0.140, just over the bound. - With SD 0.5 the pooled family stays inside its any-call bound, but the per-pair mixture rate is already above α at 0.05 and 0.10.

    The oracle arm of M6-09 (generating model, gene-level detection) stays as recorded there: 0 / 0.0127 / 0.0832 on operon_null and 0.0176 / 0.0649 / 0.1189 on the detection attack.

    Options considered¶

    • (a) Pool, as §7.4.3 says. This is safe only when M6-02 is correctly specified. Under a per-protein detectability SD of 1 logit, the pooled family's FWER/FDR under the complete null is far above 0.1. Rejected.
    • (b) A separate BH family over the P(miss) rows. Conditional on entering the family, P(miss) is not super-uniform, so BH over the one-detected rows alone is not valid. The valid discrete family would have to count every at-risk pair, including those where u was detected (p = 1). It would still inherit (a)'s misspecification failure. Rejected.
    • (c) No q, outside the family, labelled a probability (this ADR). No claim is made that can fail, and the number is still reported for ranking and inspection.

    Consequences¶

    • The §8.1 discordance family is narrower than the spec's. The M6 discordance claim is about both-detected pairs only (and is itself pending ADR-0055).
    • One-detected pairs are never flagged as discordant. A user who thresholds p_value on one_detected_pmiss rows gets the false-flag shares in the table above. The report must say so.
    • docs/methods/operons.md "One detected (M6-05H)" and "Family" cite this ADR in place of "needs an ADR (pending M6-09 attack C)". The two Superseded notes in docs/specs/M6-02 and M6-05 point here.
    • When the #311 review round lands (u scored with v's TU model; the one-detected event = fewer than k_min observable peptides seen), attack C through the step is rerun and the table updated. On real data, pairs that cross a TU boundary can move.
