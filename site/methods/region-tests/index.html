    Region tests: O − E and Hodges–Lehmann shift under circular gene-index shifts¶

    Implements giap.context.regions (GIAP_SPEC.md §7.3.5(b), §8.1, Appendix B.4; issue M4-17, docs/specs/M4-17-region-tests.md, ADR-0018 and lead decisions 6–10) and the regions_stats.tsv rows of giap.context.regions_table (issue M4-18, docs/specs/M4-18-regions-stats-table.md, sections "Replicon-level effects" and "Descriptive rows"). Reuses giap.stats.permutation (M4-03), giap.stats.bootstrap and giap.stats.robust (M4-04) and giap.stats.multitest.bh (M4-01). Statistician sign-off: see the end of this note.

    Index¶

    A replicon's index is its eligible genes — not partial and with a p_detect — sorted by start (the origin-crossing gene, stored unwrapped, comes last), numbered 0 … N − 1. Partial genes occur only at linear contig ends and are never indexed, so every shifted set has the same number of eligible genes as the region (lead decision 5). A region's index set I = the indices of its eligible members on that replicon. Members on two replicons (an AMR gene set spanning chromosome and plasmid) give one test per replicon, each on its own shifts, flagged split_contigs.

    Statistics¶

    • O − E S(I) = Σ_{i∈I} (d_i − p_i); observed = Σ d_i, expected = Σ p_i, effect = S (and its CI) on the O − E scale. The test statistic is the studentised sum T(I) = S(I)/√Σ_{i∈I} p_i(1 − p_i) (ADR-0018 decision 2), recomputed at every shift, so a region whose p_detect sits nearer 0.5 than the replicon's is not anti-conservative (Σ p(1 − p) = 0 gives T = 0 for S = 0, ±∞ otherwise). With one p on the whole replicon T and S give the same p.
    • Residualised abundance (ADR-0018 decision 1, lead decision 10): per replicon, OLS with an intercept of log2 abundance on log(length_aa), log1p(n_observable_peptides) and GRAVY (the §9.3 genes.tsv covariates), fitted on the replicon's indexed genes that are detected with a finite abundance; a non-finite transformed covariate takes that column's median over the fitted genes (minimum-norm solution for rank-deficient designs, so constant covariates only centre a). The residuals ã are fitted once per replicon, so every shift sees the same values.
    • HL shift: the median of all pairwise differences ã_i − ã_j between the region's detected genes with a finite residualised log2 abundance and the replicon's other such genes (hodges_lehmann; mean of the two middle values for an even count). NaN when either side is empty. Detected genes without abundance (policy exclude) drop out on both sides. Comparing within the replicon only keeps a high-copy plasmid's dosage out of the shift (attack 4). Effect, CI and p are on the residual scale: log2 units relative to the covariate expectation, labelled "detectability-adjusted" in the report.
    • HL test statistic (ADR-0018 amendments 1–2, lead decisions 11–16): the shift p comes from the studentised T = HL/√V, recomputed at every shift (studentised_hl_null). V is label-free (amendment 2): V = Var(mean_in − mean_out) = S_in,in/m² + S_out,out/m′² − 2 S_in,out/(m m′), S_A,B the sum of γ(d(i, j)) over detected genes with finite ã in A × B, m, m′ their counts inside / outside the set (hl_variance). γ(h), h = 0 … 30 (LAG_MAX), is the replicon's autocovariance of ã at gene-index lag h (centred on the mean of ã, each lag divided by its number of detected pairs), estimated once per replicon and set to 0 from the first h ≥ 1 with γ(h) ≤ 0 (lag_autocovariance). Lags and d are circular on circular replicons and plain |i − j| (pairs inside the sequence) on linear ones (decision 16). Implementation: c_i = Σ_j ok_j γ(d(i, j)) once per replicon (≤ 61 offsets), then per shift S_in,all = Σ_{i∈set} c_i, S_in,in by pairwise distances inside the set, S_in,out = S_in,all − S_in,in, S_out,out = S_all − 2 S_in,all + S_in,in. Guard (decision 12): when γ(0) ≤ (10⁻⁷ · max |raw log2 abundance|)², i.e. (numerically) constant residuals, V counts as 0 and T = 0 at every shift. Otherwise, a finite V ≤ 0 at the observed or any null shift (the truncated γ need not be positive definite) makes the region's HL p NA with flag hl_variance_nonpositive (fail closed, decision 16). TU labels do not enter V; a missing tu_id on a tested replicon raises ValueError (decision 13; C6 makes it non-null). The reported effect and CI stay HL. Why: a region's detected-gene count can differ strongly from that at shifted positions (≈ 7 vs ≈ 22 of 40 short prophage genes), so raw HL has the wrong spread under the shift null (limitation (iii)); label-free V is immune to TU-label errors (attack A-TU).

    Null: circular gene-index shifts¶

    I ↦ (I + k) mod N for k = 1 … N − 1 (circular_shifts); on linear contigs only shifts that do not wrap. Exhaustive while N ≤ context.exhaustive_shift_max_genes, otherwise stats.permutations shifts drawn without replacement (choose_shifts). Both statistics are recomputed at each shift; HL shifts with an empty side are dropped, so HL n_perm counts valid shifts.

    What the null preserves: region size, the spacing of its members (a block stays a block, a scattered AMR set keeps its gaps), and the replicon's autocorrelation of d − p and abundance; the region is moved, the genome is not rearranged. Under H₀ "the region's position is exchangeable with every other position on the replicon", the observed set is one member of the shift orbit, so the +1-rule p is exact. The circular null is not perfect on simulated data (M4-08's AR(1) restarts per replicon and does not wrap at the origin, one uncorrelated join) — negligible for level.

    p = perm_p(S_obs, null, "two-sided"): (1 + #{|S_k − c| ≥ |S_obs − c|})/(1 + K), c the pooled mean of the observed and null statistics (exact under exchangeability; ties within 1e-12 count). K = 0 (a linear contig with no non-wrapping shift) → p NA. p is discrete with floor 1/(1 + K): rows with K < 19 (no p can reach 0.05) are flagged few_shifts.

    Exact HL at every shift¶

    Recomputing HL by enumeration (m·(M − m) differences at each of up to N − 1 shifts) is too slow at 4,500 genes. hl_null sorts the replicon's M observed values once; for each shift the k-th smallest inside-minus-outside difference is found by bisection on its value, counting pairs x_i − z_j ≤ t by binary search in the sorted values and subtracting the inside-inside pairs, for all shifts at once; once ≤ 64 candidates remain they are enumerated. Results equal hodges_lehmann to ~1e-14 (the property suite compares with a naive per-shift loop).

    CIs: TU-block bootstrap¶

    tu_block_bootstrap, B = stats.bootstrap, 95 % percentile. O − E resamples the region's TUs, counting only region members within each TU (a region inside one TU has a degenerate CI). HL resamples inside and outside TUs as two strata (on the residualised abundances; the fit is not repeated per resample). A missing tu_id raises ValueError before any test (decision 13), so every block is a real TU.

    Multiple testing and flags¶

    BH within each (region_type, stat) family (lead decision 5, ADR-0018 decision 3; detection and abundance answer different questions; at least as strict as "within type" for each statistic). Flags: small_region (< 3 eligible genes; tests still run when K > 0; with 0 eligible genes all statistics are NA and n_perm = 0), few_shifts, split_contigs, hl_variance_nonpositive (HL row only; p NA, see above).

    Performance¶

    4,500-gene chromosome, 30 regions, exhaustive shifts, B = 1,000: ~35 s on one core (benchmarks/test_region_tests_bench.py; budget 60 s); the TU-block bootstrap of HL dominates.

    Limitations (statistician)¶

    • Exactness needs exchangeable positions. The shift p is an exact randomization p only when the region's position is independent of the per-gene data. Two violations matter: (i) detectability confounded with membership (§8.5 attack 2): the unstandardised O − E sum has variance Σ p(1 − p), so a region whose p_detect sits nearer 0.5 than the replicon's is anti-conservative (synthetic, R = 4,000: replicon p ~ Beta(4, 1.5), 40-gene region p ~ Beta(2, 2) gives 0.016 / 0.074 / 0.131 at α = 0.01 / 0.05 / 0.10; replicon Beta(0.3, 0.3), region p = 0.5 gives 0.074 / 0.212 / 0.303). The studentised shift statistic Σ(d − p)/√Σ p(1 − p), recomputed at each shift, restores level (0.010 / 0.048 / 0.103 and 0.010 / 0.046 / 0.097); adopted (ADR-0018). The giapsim attack-2 layout (short prophage genes, low p) is the conservative direction (0.004 / 0.019 / 0.047 with p_detect_true) and cannot detect this. (ii) detection-selection bias of HL: HL uses detected genes only. When detection depends on abundance (giapsim detection = "abundance", β_abundance 0.5–1) and the region's genes are short, only its abundant genes are detected: prophage HL rejects 0.089 / 0.21–0.25 / 0.30–0.35 (R = 2,000, mean HL +0.7 to +0.8 log2; control layout without short genes 0.004–0.009 / 0.036–0.048 / 0.080–0.100). Residualising log2 abundance on detectability covariates among the replicon's detected genes before HL (prototype: quadratic in log length) gives 0.017 / 0.067 / 0.103 (R = 300); adopted as the linear fit above (ADR-0018, lead decision 10). Residualising removes the part of a real region shift that the covariates explain (e.g. a prophage that is both short and silent). Correction (review round 2): the R = 300 prototype figure was noise; residualisation removes the location bias but not the failure. The remaining failure is a third violation: (iii) detected-count heterogeneity of HL: only ~6–7 of the prophage's 40 genes are detected (short genes), against ~22 at a shifted position, so the observed HL has a wider sampling distribution than the shift null (same mechanism as (i) for O − E). It fails even when detection does not depend on abundance (A2-HL-MAR below) and even with the exact oracle E[a | x, detected].
    • Replication dosage. Real replicons have an ori → ter gene-dosage gradient (up to ~1–2 log2 in fast growth) that giapsim does not model; circular shifts move the region along it, so HL partly measures position. Report HL as "relative to random positions on this replicon", not as a region-specific regulatory effect.
    • Plasmid copy number cancels exactly (within-replicon comparisons): results are bitwise identical for plasmid_copy_log2 0 and log2 20 on the same seed (3 seeds checked), so attack 4 holds by construction and is best tested as an invariance, not a calibration stream.
    • TU labels enter only the CIs (amendment 2). V is built from gene-index lags, so TU-label errors cannot move the HL p (attack A-TU at level). The TU-block bootstrap still uses the labels, so labels that fragment a region's operons narrow that region's CI; the p is unaffected.
    • V ≤ 0 (decision 16). The truncated γ is not always positive definite. In 60,000+ chromosome HL rows (ρ up to 0.95, ori–ter cosine up to 2 log2) it never happened; on the 60-gene plasmid at ρ ≥ 0.9 it hits 0.15–0.35 % of rows (lags up to 30 cover half the replicon). Those rows lose their HL p, but level is unaffected: under exhaustive shifts the flag is a function of the whole shift orbit, so the same rows would be NA whichever orbit member was observed.

    Replicon-level effects (plasmids, M4-18)¶

    Plasmids are not tested (§7.3.5(b), §8.5 attack 4). A plasmid region is the whole replicon, so no circular shift moves it, and its contrast with the chromosome mixes gene dosage (copy number) with regulation: nothing in proteomics data separates the two. Each plasmid_* region gets two rows with p_value = q_value = NA, null_model = none, n_perm = 0, method = replicon_contrast, label = dosage_plus_regulation:

    • o_minus_e: the plasmid's own O − E over its eligible genes (E = Σ p_detect, which already carries the detectability model; lead decision 1). No chromosome-rate row.
    • hl_shift: HL of the plasmid's raw log2 abundance against the reference replicons (lead decision 2). Not M4-17's per-replicon residuals: those centre every replicon and give HL ≈ 0 whatever the dosage. Only detected, eligible genes with an observed value enter; imputed genes are excluded on both sides, because the genome-wide median fill (docs/methods/abundance.md) places imputed plasmid genes at the chromosome median and pulls the contrast towards 0.
    • Reference (lead decision 3): the kind = chromosome replicons; without one (draft genomes) every replicon that is neither kind = plasmid nor a plasmid region's contig, flag no_reference_replicon (HL NA with the same flag if that set has no usable gene).
    • CIs: 95 % TU-block bootstrap; for HL with strata plasmid / reference, so every replicate has both sides. Each plasmid draws from its own child stream keyed by its region_id (lead decision 7), so adding a plasmid changes no other row.
    • Fewer than SMALL_REGION (3) eligible genes: every statistic NA, flag small_region.

    Depth offset. With --replicon-depth (§5.3), an extra hl_shift row (label = dosage_adjusted, flag depth_adjusted) reports HL − log2(plasmid depth / reference depth), the reference depth being the length-weighted mean over the same reference replicons. Depth is treated as known: the CI is the HL CI shifted by the same constant, so it omits the uncertainty of the depth ratio (deferred, docs/backlog.md). The row is omitted, with a warning from the step, when the plasmid or a reference replicon has no depth, or when HL is NA.

    What the HL CI covers (statistician review of PR #110; lead decisions 15 and 16). The estimand is the realised dosage-plus-regulation shift of this plasmid against the reference replicons. The TU-block CI covers it conservatively: 0.997–1.000 for ρ = 0–0.8, with detection from covariates or from abundance (Slurm 1860197, 1860273). It is not a CI for the copy number alone. The plasmid's own TU-level expression adds sd ≈ 0.75 log2 around log2(dosage) at ρ = 0.5 (1.7 at ρ = 0.8), and one genome cannot separate that from dosage. No resampling scheme fixes this: circular-block bootstraps reach only 0.85 / 0.71, and a lag-V Wald interval 0.87 / 0.81, which at ρ = 0 is worse than the current CI. The dosage_adjusted row estimates the plasmid's realised regulation under the same conditional reading. Coverage of log2(copy number) is not a gate.     Selection bias at the null (recorded, not gated). The HL compares detected genes only. When detection depends on abundance, each replicon loses its low-abundance genes, and the two replicons lose different shares, so the raw contrast can be biased at the true dosage. The bias is small. With abundance-driven detection (detection = "abundance", default slope, ρ = 0.5, R = 300), the mean HL − log2(20) is −0.087 log2 (MC SE 0.049), and the mean HL − T_all is +0.022. Its sign depends on the detection slope and on how much of each replicon drops out. Under covariate-only detection the bias is +0.006 (SE 0.026). The result is an effect estimate, never a test.
