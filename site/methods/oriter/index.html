    Origin-to-terminus gradient¶

    Issue M5-08 (GIAP_SPEC.md §7.3.5(e), §9.3), module giap.context.oriter. The step plumbing that writes the rows into autocorrelation.tsv and the two genes.tsv columns is M5-09.

    Why the regression is descriptive¶

    Replication from a single origin makes gene dosage decrease from oriC to the terminus during exponential growth, and highly expressed transcription and translation genes sit near oriC because of it. The ori–ter gradient of protein abundance is therefore an evolved property of the gene order, not a deviation from an exchangeable null: no permutation of the genes (or of the values) reproduces "the same genome without a gradient", because the placement of the genes is the effect we would be testing. GIAP consequently reports the slope and an interval, never a p-value: every row carries p_value = NA, q_value = NA and the flag descriptive (§7.3.5(e)). The interval answers "how precisely is this genome's gradient measured", not "could it have arisen by chance".

    The rows therefore enter no §8.1 multiple-testing family. §8.1 lists no ori–ter claim; the autocorrelation BH family is keyed on (contig, statistic, null_model) over lags with a p-value, and these rows carry statistic = oriter_slope, null_model = bootstrap and a NA p, so they can only form a family of their own with nothing in it. M5-09 must keep it that way: p_value NA ⇒ q_value NA, and no ori–ter row is counted in any BH denominator.

    Replichore geometry¶

    The oriC midpoint o comes from Bakta (C15 features, type = oric); the terminus is taken as o + L/2. For a point at coordinate m on a circular replicon of length L, with d = (m − o) mod L:

    condition label position
    right replichore d ≤ L/2 right d / (L/2)
    left replichore d > L/2 left (L − d) / (L/2)

    Positions lie in [0, 1]: 0 at the origin, 1 at the terminus. right is the arm of increasing coordinate from the oriC (lead decision 32); the labels are directions on the circle, not Bakta-annotated leading/lagging strands. The geometry is intrinsic to the circle: rotating every coordinate and the oriC by the same offset leaves both columns unchanged, mirror images across the oriC share a position on opposite arms, and the strand never enters. Midpoints are taken modulo L, so an origin-crossing interval written unwrapped (end > L, §6.5) lands where it belongs.

    Only circular chromosomes are analysed (lead decision 33): the geometry above assumes a circle with one origin, so linear replicons, unknown-kind contigs and plasmids get no row and NA gene columns. Plasmid oriV/oriT calls are not origins of chromosomal replication and are ignored.

    Several oriC calls¶

    Bakta can call more than one oriC (the recorded K-12 MG1655 annotation has two, 42 kb apart). The calls are summarised by their circular mean (the angle of the mean of the unit vectors 2π·mᵢ/L), which handles calls either side of coordinate 0, but only when every pair lies within 0.05·L circular distance; farther apart the module refuses to guess and the replicon is no_oric (lead decision 31). Requiring the maximum pairwise circular distance to be ≤ 0.05·L (not the spread about the mean) is what makes the mean well defined: with 0.05·L < L/3 the calls then necessarily lie inside one arc of that length, so the circular mean is inside the arc and no antipodal cancellation is possible. Both branches log the event context_stats.oriter.multiple_oric with the midpoints, the spread and the decision, so a questionable origin is visible in logs/events.jsonl. For K-12 the two calls differ by 0.009 of a replichore, far below the resolution of a descriptive slope. Without any call — or without a features table at all — the replicon yields one row with value NA and the flags descriptive;no_oric, and its genes get NA ori–ter columns.

    An oriC that is wrong by δ bp shifts every x by 2δ/L and swaps arms for the genes in between. The slope is not invariant to that (only the geometry is), so a genome whose oriC call is doubtful is better read from the multiple_oric event than from the slope.

    Points and fits¶

    The regression is at TU level, so that an operon counts once rather than once per gene: y_t is the mean abundance_log2 of the TU's detected eligible genes with a value (censoring is ignored — the quantity regressed is the observed abundance of what was detected, lead decision 34), and x_t is the replichore position of the TU midpoint. TUs without a value are dropped. Three OLS fits are reported per replicon: the TUs of the right arm, of the left arm and pooled. The slope b is in log2 per replichore, i.e. the fitted terminus minus the fitted origin, and is written to both value and effect. A fit with fewer than three TUs, or with no variance in x, does not exist: its row is NA with descriptive;not_estimable.

    Each arm is a subset of the pooled points, so a non-estimable pooled fit forces both arms to be non-estimable: with fewer than three pooled TUs neither arm can have three, and if the pooled x has no variance then neither subset has any. That is why ℓ̂ is only ever estimated when the pooled fit exists, and the arms can still use it. The invariant is about subsets, not about the circle, so a future change to the per-fit minimum would have to re-check it; block_len_prime raises on ℓ̂ < 1, so a violation fails loudly rather than quietly resampling with block 1.

    The pooled fit regresses on |position|, not on a signed coordinate, so it is the gradient averaged over the two arms, weighted by their TU counts. Read the two arm rows before the pooled one when the arms disagree; a single pooled slope hides an asymmetric origin call or a genuine asymmetry.

    The interval and its block length¶

    Neighbouring TUs are correlated (operons, local expression domains), so an iid bootstrap of the TUs would be too narrow — measurably so: resampling single TUs instead of blocks on the calibration cell below gives mean width 0.78 against 1.11 and coverage 0.785 against 0.943 (R = 200/300). The CI comes from the circular block bootstrap (docs/methods/bootstrap.md) of each fit's own TU sequence — the pooled fit in genome order, an arm ordered from oriC to terminus — resampling index blocks of (x, y) pairs and refitting; the reported interval is the 95 % percentile interval of the stats.bootstrap refitted slopes. Replicates whose resampled x has no variance give a NaN slope and are dropped; n_perm counts the valid ones, and fewer than B/2 of them leaves the CI NA with not_estimable added (B ≥ 1 is assumed, as everywhere else in giap.stats.bootstrap).

    Resampling pairs in blocks, rather than residuals, is deliberate. A TU's y is the mean of however many of its genes were detected, so Var y_t falls with that count and unweighted OLS is heteroscedastic; the pairs bootstrap carries each TU's own noise level with it, so the interval does not assume equal variances even though the point estimate ignores them. The resample also keeps the realised detection pattern: it never re-draws which genes were detected, so the interval is conditional on the detected set (see the caveats).

    The block length follows ADR-0021 decision 1, shared with the island null: ℓ′ = min(20, 2ℓ̂ + 1, T), with ℓ̂ the first lag at which Moran's I of the pooled fit's residuals is no longer significant (choose_block_len, stats.permutations gene-label permutations). ℓ̂ is estimated once per replicon; the clamp to T is applied per fit with that fit's own TU count, because an arm may hold far fewer TUs than the pooled sequence the length was estimated from. An integer context.islands.block_len overrides the rule (clamped to T as well). Those residuals still carry whatever the straight line failed to absorb of the true (cosine-shaped) dosage curve, but that does not drive ℓ̂ in practice: on the calibration cell ℓ̂ averages 3.91 with a 2-log2 cosine planted and 3.87 with no gradient at all, so the AR(1) background, not the leftover curvature, sets the block.

    On an arm the circular wrap joins the TU nearest the terminus to the TU nearest the origin, which are not neighbours on the chromosome; ℓ′/T of the blocks straddle that join (≈ 1 % at 20 TUs and 2,000). The pooled sequence wraps at coordinate 0 between genuine neighbours.

    What is measured¶

    Coverage of the 95 % pooled interval for the OLS slope of the planted curve, on scenarios.oriter_gradient (4,500 genes, ≈ 1,720 TUs with a value, B = 199, the real giap.context.oriter.oriter_rows). The estimand is the OLS slope of the planted cosine over the TUs that enter the fit, which the closed form puts at −12a/π² = −2.432 for a = 2 log2 under a uniform design; the estimator is unbiased for it (mean −2.424 against mean estimand −2.432 at R = 300). Statistician re-measurement for this review, Slurm 1863166 (local/scratch/stat-132-work/); the implementer's recorded run (Slurm 1863081) is the first 50 replicates of the first row and reproduces exactly.

    Validity domain. The interval is nominal to within a few points over the whole range measured: 0.90–0.94 against a nominal 0.95, never grossly wrong, and never conservative. What is claimed is coverage ≥ 0.90 everywhere in the table above, and ≈ 0.94 for giapsim ar1_background at ρ ≤ 0.5 on a ≈ 4,500-gene chromosome (the gate cell); the gate's numeric bound below is the finite-R form of the first claim, not a second one. Unlike the island FWER (ADR-0021 Amendment 1) there is no regime here in which the procedure breaks down — at ρ 0.9 the cap ℓ′ = 20 binds and the interval becomes nearly vacuous instead (width 4.4 for an effect of −2.4), which is a loss of precision, not of validity. No ori–ter row carries uncalibrated (Amendment 1 dropped the flag).

    The shortfall is systematic, not noise: it is the percentile interval's own skewness and the blocks' under-representation of dependence across joins. ℓ′ is shared with the island scan only because ADR-0021 made it so, and the island scan's power trade-off (which is what kept ℓ′ short) does not apply to a slope CI; a longer block, a studentised interval or BCa would likely recover the missing 1–3 points. That is hardening-pass work (backlog), not a merge condition for a descriptive statistic.

    Caveats a reader must apply¶

    The island effect CI is conditional on a selected window (winner's curse). Nothing is selected here — three fixed fits per replicon, no scan, no maximum — so that caveat does not carry over. These do:

    1. The interval is not a test. With no gradient planted at all, the 95 % interval misses the true slope 0 in 8.3 % of replicates (R = 300, above). Reading "the CI excludes 0" as a 5 % test of "this genome has no ori–ter gradient" is wrong twice over: the error rate is not 5 %, and §7.3.5(e) refuses that test on purpose, because gene placement is the effect, not the null.
    2. Conditional on what was detected. y is observed abundance among detected proteins. The bootstrap never re-draws detection, so the interval is for the gradient of the observed abundances of this detected set, not for the complete-data gradient. Under giapsim's abundance-driven detection the coverage of the planted curve is unchanged (0.940, above), but that is a statement about the simulator's selection strength, not a licence: if detectability covaries with position — ribosomal and transcription genes near oriC are short, abundant and easy — the slope is confounded with it and no interval repairs that.
    3. Conditional on the design. The estimand is the OLS slope over the realised TU positions. Slopes are comparable across genomes only to the extent that the oriC call, the TU density and the detected share are; the dosage curve is not a straight line (the planted one is a cosine), so b is a linear summary whose value depends on how the TUs are spread over [0, 1].
    4. No simultaneity. The three rows of a replicon, and the rows of different replicons, are each 95 % intervals, not a 95 % simultaneous set; the pooled row and its two arms use the same TUs and are strongly dependent.
    5. The arms are the weaker rows. They carry half the TUs, 40 % wider intervals and 1–2 points less coverage than the pooled row at every cell measured (0.875–0.945). Only the pooled row is gated.

    Statistician sign-off¶

    Signed off by the statistician (PR #132 review). The review covered the estimand and its closed form, the per-fit block clamp, the percentile interval, the degenerate rules and the measurements above, and settled M5-08 lead decision 8:

    Example 7 is promoted to a gate, on the full (nightly) arm only. The claim is the non-coverage rate of the pooled interval on the gate cell, R = replicates_full.islands = 500, bounded by the house rule level_bound(0.07, R) = 0.0929, i.e. coverage ≥ 0.907, with a non-estimable CI counted as a miss (fail closed) and ADR-0015 two-block confirmation. At the measured 0.943 that gate fails 0.1 % of blocks and ~1e-6 of confirmed nightlies, and catches a regression to 0.88 — what the spec's ℓ̂ produced before ADR-0021 — with probability 0.98. The tolerated non-coverage is 0.07 and not the nominal 0.05 because the measured coverage is 0.943 ± 0.013, not 0.95: a 0.05 tolerance (bound 0.9305) would fail 12–20 % of blocks and 1.6–3.8 % of confirmed nightlies. The fast arm (R = 50) stays recorded: at that R no bound has both a confirmed false-failure rate below 1 % and any power (bound 0.90: 0.4 % false failures, power 0.32 against 0.88). The two arm rows and every other cell of the table stay recorded.
