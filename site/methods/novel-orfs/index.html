    Novel ORFs (M7)¶

    Status: §"Class FDR" written for the statistician's round-2 review of PR #237 (M7-11). Code: src/giap/novel/fdr.py (pure); the novel_orfs step wiring (M7-11b, PR #256) is src/giap/novel/class_call.py and §"Wiring in the novel_orfs step". Spec: GIAP_SPEC.md §7.6.4, §7.9.2, §8.1, §8.2; ADR-0009, ADR-0010; docs/specs/M7-11-novel-class-fdr.md, lead decisions 1–14; docs/specs/M7-11b-class-fdr-wiring.md, lead decisions 1–7.

    Class FDR¶

    Novel peptides have a lower prior than annotated ones, so the global search FDR understates their error (§7.6.4). GIAP therefore estimates the error of a novel-ORF call within its class (intergenic, cds_outframe, antisense, nterm_extension), with stats.alpha.novel_orfs (0.01) as the call threshold.

    Regimes (rule 1, decision 1)¶

    The regime is chosen per ORF from the source of its peptides, database first.

    • picked_decoy: the run has decoy rows whose stripped protein IDs (longest input.decoy_prefixes match removed) are class-tagged makedb IDs GIAPORF_{class}_{contig}_{start1}_{end1}_{p|m}, and the ORF has at least one database (non-denovo) peptide.
    • denovo_subgroup: otherwise, when the ORF has a de novo peptide.
    • unestimated: everything else, for example decoys from a Bakta-protein search (rev_<locus_tag>). The pass-1 rule (≥ 2 distinct peptides) applies, and class_fdr = unestimated.

    Database and de novo q-values are never pooled into one list. Their decoys answer different questions (a search-engine competition and a genome placement), so a pooled list would have no single decoy model.

    Picked class FDR (rule 2; ADR-0009)¶

    Matching. Each target ORF is matched to the makedb six-frame ORF with the same (contig, strand, stop). A makedb ORF is stop to stop, so the codon position just past its 3′ end identifies it: the entry's 0-based end on the plus strand, and 3 nt below the entry's start on the minus strand. For an ORF closed by a stop codon, that position is where Orf.stop (the stop's lowest coordinate) lies. A 3′-open ORF (linear contig end or an X codon) has no closing stop: Orf.stop is its last whole codon, and makedb ends the segment at that codon's end. Its key is therefore Orf.stop + 3 on the plus strand and Orf.stop − 3 on the minus strand (decision 13). When replicon lengths are given, both keys are wrapped modulo the length, so origin-crossing entries match (decisions 8 and 14). Without lengths, an ORF whose entry crosses the origin is unestimated. An nterm_extension ORF with no stop match is paired by locus tag. Its makedb entry covers the upstream part plus the first 30 codons, so its stop lies inside the gene.

    The pairing rules have these consequences: - When the ORF's class and the entry's tag differ, the entry tag wins, because pairing and the class list follow the entry. The ORF is flagged class_mismatch (decision 9). - An ORF whose own class is untagged (pseudogene, sorf, cterm_extension) is unestimated (untagged_class, decisions 2 and 10). - A tagged ORF with no entry is unestimated (no_makedb_pair, decision 7).

    Scores. The score of an entry's target side is its matched ORF's best peptide. Only non-de-novo peptides count, because makedb decoys mimic database-search targets only; adding de novo target evidence with no decoy counterpart would be anti-conservative (decision 10). The score of its decoy side is the best decoy row whose stripped ID is the entry. Within each entry class, picked_fdr keeps the better side per entry, and ties go to the decoy. Entries hit only by their decoy count as decoy wins.

    Unscored rows (decision 11, as M4-24). C9 allows a missing q_value and a missing score. Mapping a missing q to +∞ would rank an unscored decoy below every target and silently give D = 0. In the statistician's all-null attack, 400 of 400 null ORFs then reached q ≤ 0.05. GIAP therefore ranks a class only on a key that every target and decoy row of that class carries: - (q ascending, then score descending) when every q is finite; - otherwise score alone, when every score is finite; - otherwise the class is unestimated with reason decoys_unscored.

    Paired share and rescaling. f is the share of the class's distinct stripped decoy IDs that are makedb entries. The decoy's class is the tag in its ID. Unpaired decoys never enter the list, and each decoy win counts 1/f:

    FDR(t) = (D(t)/f + 1) / T(t), made monotone (ADR-0009, via stats.tda.picked_fdr_rescaled).

    The unit is distinct IDs because decoy wins are counted per entry: the share of lost entries is what the correction needs, while a row count would weight entries by their number of peptides. An entry missing from id_map also loses its target (no_makedb_pair). That loss is symmetric, so 1/f is conservative there. 1/f matters when a decoy is lost but its target stays.

    80 % guard (decision 4). A class with f < 0.80 falls back to unestimated (paired_share_low), by analogy with the §7.3.2 tier guard; changing the guard needs an ADR. Below the guard the cost is power (the ≥ 2-peptide rule), not validity. In the decoy-loss attack (10 % and 20 % of decoy rows re-pointed to unmapped tagged IDs), FDP stays at level.

    A class with targets and no decoys gets q = 1/T, so it needs ≥ 100 target wins before any ORF reaches 0.01 (the B.6 logic).

    De novo subgroup FDR (rule 3; ADR-0010)¶

    Decoy hits come from the mapping work tables (search = decoy). Each peptide is taken at its best decoy cost (minimum n_diff). Each hit takes the M3-14 LocusClassifier class of its mirrored target interval, and is counted once per (peptide, class) at its minimum E (decision 10). A target peptide enters the list of its ORF's class with its E. Per class, peptides are ordered by E:

    FDR_class(e) = (W_class(e) + c) / T_class(e), monotone q (stratum_qvalues),

    where W sums the decoys' own π̂ (a missing π̂ counts as 1), and c is the largest π̂ over the searched peptides, those with a target or decoy hit (decision 5), with a missing π̂ again counting as 1. A class with no decoy hit gets q = c/T. A hit with no E (e_target or e_decoy NaN) raises ValueError naming the peptide (decision 11). Dropping such a decoy would shrink W and be anti-conservative: with 30 % of decoy E missing, FDP was 0.0133 / 0.069 / 0.104 against bounds of 0.0115 / 0.053 / 0.103.

    Why the classes are never pooled¶

    The false share differs strongly between classes. intergenic ORFs are mostly false, while cds_outframe calls next to expressed genes are mostly true. A pooled list lends the clean class's target wins to the noisy class. In the statistician's attack (intergenic 3 % true next to cds_outframe 80 % true, FDP measured inside intergenic), the per-class estimate is at level. A pooled list gives FDP 0.068 / 0.29 / 0.46 at q = 0.01 / 0.05 / 0.10. The de novo attack (80 % of chance hits intergenic, correct peptides 5 % intergenic) is also at level per class.

    ORF q = minimum peptide q (decision 3): anti-conservative¶

    For de novo ORFs, the ORF class q is the minimum of its peptides' subgroup q. That is the best-evidence statistic, but it is anti-conservative at ORF level: a false ORF with k wrong peptides gets k chances to draw a small q. In the review's generator, ORF FDP at q = 0.01 / 0.05 / 0.10 was:

    Wrong peptides per false ORF ORF FDP q + 2 SE
    1–5 0.0185 / 0.078 / 0.113 0.0117 / 0.053 / 0.103
    1–10 0.030 / 0.097 / 0.131 —

    The 1–5 case failed in both confirmation blocks. That generator is a worst case: in real data, chance hits of wrong de novo peptides rarely fall in one ORF. The cases that matter are systematic de novo errors that cluster, such as reversed stretches (§8.5 attack 5) in intergenic space. M7-13 G4 must measure ORF-level FDP per class with false ORFs carrying ≥ 3 peptides, including attack 5. If it fails, an ADR chooses between a picked ORF-level competition on the genome decoy and a per-ORF Bonferroni over peptides (decision 3). A strict xfail in tests/calibration/attacks/test_attack_novel_class_fdr.py flags any change to decision 3.

    Wiring in the novel_orfs step (M7-11b)¶

    Code: src/giap/novel/class_call.py, giap.novel.evidence.class_rules and _call, src/giap/steps/novel.py. Spec: docs/specs/M7-11b-class-fdr-wiring.md, lead decisions 1–7.

    Inputs. The step reads the C9 peptides (picked regime), and for the de novo regime it reads placement_costs, hits_exact and hits_tolerant. All are optional. Without peptides, every ORF is unestimated (no_decoys), which is the pass-1 output byte for byte. One regime is assigned per ORF, with no cascade: a picked-regime ORF whose picked result is unestimated never falls back to its de novo peptides (Q6). Each (regime, class) list is one §8.1 "Novel ORF" family, and α is stats.alpha.novel_orfs everywhere.

    Call rule (rule 6). - Numeric class_fdr = q: called = class rule ∧ q ≤ α. q is compared as the unformatted float. The TSV shows %.6g, and q is never parsed back from the cell. There is no peptide minimum (§7.6.4), but the cds_outframe and antisense class rules still require 2 peptides. - class_fdr = unestimated: called = class rule ∧ n_peptides ≥ min_peptides_without_decoys (§7.6.4 "Neither"), with the flag class_fdr_unestimated. - lost_pick (Q1): the class is estimated, but the ORF's own decoy beat it or tied it. The cell is 1 and the ORF is never called. - Why it must not fall back to the ≥ 2-peptide rule: losing the pick is the competition's evidence that the target looks like chance. Falling back would call exactly the ORFs that the competition rejected. - In the statistician's attack (cds_outframe ORFs of 2–3 peptides, 25 % true, exchangeable false targets and decoys; tests/calibration/attacks/test_attack_class_call_wiring.py), the fallback reading gives FDP 0.98 / 0.31 / 0.35 of called at α = 0.01 / 0.05 / 0.10. The wired rule gives 0.0015 / 0.043 / 0.096, against bounds of 0.012 / 0.058 / 0.112 (R = 50). - Why 1 and not "the q at the decoy's rank": the target never enters the picked list, so it has no q. 1 is the conservative bound, and it keeps the cell numeric. - The FDR claim covers the set {q ≤ α}. The class rule then keeps a subset of it. This preserves control as long as the rule does not prefer false ORFs. The rules (start codon, length, RBS support, ≥ 2 peptides) favour real ORFs. M7-13 G4 measures the called set itself, class_rule_met ∧ q ≤ a, from the side table. - Fallback calls in a makedb run carry no error control. These are the no_makedb_pair, no_target_score and untagged-class ORFs. They are labelled unestimated (§8: never silently trusted), and G4's prediction set ("class q ≤ q") excludes them. M7-13 should therefore also report how many calls in each class came through the fallback.

    Class q floor (rule 8). - No picked q can fall below 1/T_wins, where T_wins is the class's entries won by their target. At any threshold, FDR = (D/f + 1)/T ≥ 1/T ≥ 1/T_wins, and the monotone q is a minimum of these values. - No de novo q can fall below c/T, where T is the class's target peptides, because W ≥ 0. - When the floor is strictly above α, nothing in the class can be called. The run then logs the WARNING novel_orfs.class_q_floor_above_alpha: a class with fewer than about 1/α candidates cannot be called at α. q and α are unchanged (Khoa, 2026-10-22). - T_wins = 100 at α = 0.01 gives a floor equal to α. That raises no warning, because the best ORF can still be called. - A class with no target win (picked) or no target (de novo), and an unestimated class, has no floor and raises no warning. - The warning goes to the manifest warnings list (--strict exits 3), not to a run-flag token (Q4). It mirrors the gene-level q_floor_above_alpha. - The picked floor counts entries won. The warning text quotes n_targets, the ORFs entering the list. When several ORFs share one entry, or many targets lose the pick, the two differ: read q_floor in the event data, not the target count.

    Paired share in v1 (rule 2, Q5). - No run input carries makedb's db_id_map.tsv. makedb_id_map therefore builds the map from every class-tagged ID in protein_ids (targets, and decoys with their prefix stripped). The nterm_extension locus tags come from nterm_entries on the run's own gene model. - So every tagged decoy is an entry, the paired share is f = 1, and the 80 % guard cannot fire from the pipeline (backlog). - This is not anti-conservative. The guard and the 1/f rescaling exist because an unpaired decoy is dropped from the list while targets stay, so D is undercounted. In v1 no tagged decoy is dropped: a decoy whose entry has no target ORF competes alone, and so counts as a decoy win. - In the re-pointed-decoys attack (20 % and 50 % of decoy rows moved to tagged IDs with no target, through makedb_id_map), FDP is 0.0081 / 0.044 / 0.089 at 20 % and 0.0062 / 0.034 / 0.071 at 50 %, against bounds of about 0.012 / 0.053 / 0.104. FDP falls as the loss grows. - Pairing can still be lost on the target side. - A nterm_extension ORF pairs through the locus tag of the run's own nterm_entries, and the other classes pair through the (contig, strand, stop) key. If the run's annotation or genome differs from the one makedb used, the ORF is no_makedb_pair. - Its decoy stays in the list, so the rest of the class becomes more conservative. The ORF itself, however, drops to the uncontrolled fallback above. - A real db_id_map.tsv would not change this, because f counts observed decoys. - No share detects decoy rows that are missing from the input altogether, for example an engine that exports only some of its decoys. On the calibration generator, halving the tagged decoy rows gives FDP 0.017 / 0.096 / 0.20. Input decoys are assumed complete, as for the gene-level picked tier.

    De novo inputs (rule 3, Q11). - denovo_inputs keeps only source = denovo rows of placement_costs: - targets: ORF peptides with a finite e_target; - decoys: those peptides' decoy hit rows at their best decoy cost, carrying e_decoy; - weights: π̂ from pi_denovo; - c: the largest π̂ over the de novo peptides with a target or decoy hit. - Database peptides' decoy hits and their π̂ (NaN, which would count as 1) never enter a de novo list. This is valid, because the list estimates the chance hits of wrong de novo peptides. - The target E is the peptide's best target E over the genome, as M7-11 assumed. The step does not restrict it to the ORF's interval. candidate_rows keeps only peptides with a single novel locus and no hit in an excluding class, so the two usually coincide. They are not guaranteed to. - Decoys of de novo peptides that are not in a de novo ORF still count (for example, banned, multi-locus or picked-regime ORF peptides). That is conservative.

    Calibration of the wired path. tests/calibration/test_class_call_calibration.py runs class_fdr, with its own makedb_id_map and denovo_inputs, plus class_fdr_cell. - In the statistician's mutation probe (PR #256), these mutations fail the criterion: - de novo decoys restricted to ORF peptides (0.0175 / 0.084 / 0.116 against 0.012 / 0.053 / 0.103); - decoy weights halved; - c = 0 (at α = 0.01); - half of the picked decoy rows dropped. - Two mutations pass: - dropping the picked +1, whose effect is too small with T in the hundreds; - pooling the classes, because the generator gives every class the same prior. The M7-11 low_prior_class attack covers that case. - The suite measures numeric cells only. test_attack_class_call_wiring.py adds the called column.

    Diagnostics. novel/novel_class_fdr.parquet has one row per novel_orfs.tsv row, in the same order. - Columns: orf_id, class, fdr_class, regime, q (float, NaN unless estimated and won), class_fdr (the TSV cell), reason, class_mismatch, class_rule_met and called. - G4 calls at level a with class_rule_met ∧ q ≤ a. A lost_pick row has q NaN, so it is never in the set. - One novel_orfs.class_fdr INFO event per (regime, class) list carries the counts, f, c, the floor and n_called. The manifest novel_orfs.classes block holds the same data.

    Statistician (PR #256, M7-11b review, 2026-10-23): wiring, call rule and floor rule reviewed. Verdict recorded on the PR.

    Exact systematic de novo errors (ADR-0050)¶

    The class FDR treats wrong peptides as decoy hits treat them: a wrong read lands on the genome by chance, so a reversed or shuffled genome decoy is as likely to receive it. A de novo read that is wrong but exactly equals the translation of a non-coding six-frame segment breaks that assumption. It places with zero cost, like a true peptide, and no decoy can match it except by chance, so no decoy-based estimate counts it. When several such reads fall in one segment, the ORF also gets several chances at a small q (decision 3). In M7-13 G4-k3 (a stress design: 20 injected false ORFs per class, 3–5 wrong reads each; R = 300) the ORF-level FDP was 0.072–0.075 for cds_outframe and antisense at both q = 0.01 and 0.05, and 0.015–0.017 for intergenic at 0.01. The exact reads account for the excess; error-bearing reads alone leave only a small excess at q = 0.01. The size of the excess scales with how many such errors a run contains, so it is not a prediction for real data. GIAP therefore does not claim class-FDR control against exact systematic de novo errors. q values and calls are unchanged. Calls that this limitation can affect carry denovo_only_unsupported unless chance RBS support exempts them (below): every peptide is de novo, none is a database PSM, and there is no RBS support beyond what the class rule already requires. RBS support at the 0.10 quantile is weak evidence: in M7-13 G4-k3, the chosen start of an injected false ORF that reached q ≤ 0.05 passed it in 12 of 199 cds_outframe rows (0.060) and 15 of 199 antisense rows (0.075) (Slurm 1879351, fast tier, R = 10), so those calls escape the flag; intergenic and nterm_extension are never exempt. The flag is therefore a pointer to an evidence gap, not a second error estimate: flagged calls are not estimated to be false, and unflagged de novo-only calls are not estimated to be correct. In a run with only de novo input nearly every call carries it. Users who need such calls should confirm them by a database search against the giap makedb six-frame database, or by independent evidence.

    Limitations¶

    • c over hit peptides only. ADR-0010 reads "the peptides … searched"; c here is the largest π̂ over peptides with a target or decoy hit (decision 5). This is anti-conservative only when the highest-π̂ peptides have no hit at all: negligible at N in the thousands, visible in B.6-sized lists.
    • Class asymmetry. A de novo target takes its ORF's class (the highest-priority member class), while its decoy analogue takes its own interval's class. A wrong peptide that joins an ORF of another class moves T toward that class, and no D moves with it. The effect is small; M7-13 G4 reports FDP per class, not only per regime.
    • Target E. A target peptide's E is its best E over all its target hits, not its hit inside the ORF. The M7-11b step does not restrict it, and documents this under "De novo inputs" above.
    • regime() of a mixed set of ORFs returns the first of picked / de novo / unestimated held. The M7-11b step uses orf_regimes per ORF. The regime of each ORF is in the side table, and the per-class events hold the list counts. No separate regime-count event is logged.

    C-terminal extensions (M7-16 rule 12, ADR-0058)¶

    A cterm_extension ORF is called when some peptide interval strictly contains the gene's annotated terminal stop codon and extends past it on the 3′ side (spans_annotated_stop); its label is readthrough_candidate. The class is untagged, so its class FDR is unestimated and the call needs ≥ 2 distinct peptides (M7-11 decision 2). A window that ends on the stop codon classifies as cds_inframe and is no extension evidence; it shows up as a stop_codon ALT site instead. A called row is written to annotation_patch.gff3 as the gene from its start to the ORF's stop (Note=cterm_extension, partial=true when the ORF is open at a linear 3′ end), and makedb --patch translates the old stop codon as X.
