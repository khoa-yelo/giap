    Detection tiers and gene-level FDR (M4-12)¶

    Status: M4-24 shared ranking key approved by the statistician 2026-10-23 (PR #238). Earlier: statistician re-review 2026-10-15 (PR #92, after f297600): PEP-only path approved; changes requested on the contaminant-decoy rule (lead decision 11), see Sign-off. Code: src/giap/genetable/detection.py. Spec: GIAP_SPEC.md §2.1 decision 4, §7.3, §7.3.2, §9.3; ADR-0009; docs/specs/M4-12-detection-tiers.md.

    Tier choice (once per run, from the input)¶

    1. decoy_picked: at least one C9 decoy row with protein_ids passes q <= input.q_value_max (NA q passes), and the paired share f = paired / (paired + unpaired) is at least max(mapping.proteins.min_identity, mapping.proteins.min_coverage) (0.80 by default), and the paired decoys share at least one ranking column with the targets (M4-24, see "Shared ranking key"). Decoys above q_value_max are neither paired nor unpaired; that filter applies only when some C9 target row carries q (ADR-0047).
    2. mayu: decoys exist but f is below the cut-off (warning decoy_pairing_low), or an accepted target carries a q-value or a PEP.
    3. two_peptide: C11 has rows but no accepted q or PEP.
    4. protein_list: no peptides at all.

    Protein to gene map¶

    Priority: (1) the ID is a C2 locus_tag; (2) the gene of its C12 rank-1 hits (one gene only); (3) peptide vote: among accepted unique target peptides whose C9 protein_ids contain the ID, the gene with the most distinct peptides (a tie leaves the ID unmapped). Protein groups A;B are split; contaminant-prefixed IDs are never mapped.

    Decoy pairing¶

    The longest matching input.decoy_prefixes entry is stripped from every member of the decoy's protein_ids. A decoy with any stripped member carrying an input.contaminant_prefixes prefix is a contaminant decoy and is neither paired nor unpaired (lead decisions 6, 11). Otherwise the decoy pairs when every member maps, and all map to the same gene.

    PEP-only input (lead decision 5)¶

    When every accepted q-value is missing and PEPs are given, peptide q is the running mean of the sorted distinct accepted PEPs (q_i = mean of the PEPs <= PEP_i), and MAYU uses those q for the thresholds and sum PEP for F(t). Mixed input keeps the given q (decision 11).

    Asymmetry (lead question 1). Decoys pass the same q <= q_value_max filter as targets, but targets must also be placed on a gene and decoys cannot be. False targets that do not place are lost while their decoys stay, so D is too large and the picked estimate errs conservative. In giapsim every false target places (false_source = random_gene), which is the least favourable case for this asymmetry.

    What 1/f assumes (ADR-0009). Decoys are lost at random with respect to score while their target counterparts stay. When loss is symmetric (a reference protein absent from the strain loses its false targets and its decoy alike, as in cross-strain searches), 1/f over-corrects and the estimate is conservative. Loss that depends on score is not corrected. Stress run below: losing the best-scoring 10 % / 20 % of passing decoys gives FDP 1.5x / 2x q at q = 0.01, and the 80 % guard does not catch it. Nothing in the input should make pairing depend on score. If an engine ever does that, a KS check of paired against unpaired decoy scores would be the diagnostic (backlog).

    Picked gene-level FDR (Savitski 2015, ADR-0009)¶

    Per eligible (non-partial) gene: best accepted target and best paired decoy by the key (q ascending, PEP ascending, score descending; NA last), dense-ranked over the union (no -log10 0). The better one is kept; a tie goes to the decoy. With f the paired share, FDR(t) = (D(t)/f + 1) / T(t), made monotone. Decoy winners and genes without accepted peptides get q NA (not_detected). detection_summary.tsv reports f (decoy_paired_share) and 1/f (decoy_win_weight). It also reports q_floor = 1/T, where T is the number of eligible genes won by a target. It is a lower bound on every picked q, from the +1 that finite-sample control needs: q = running min of (D/f + 1)/k ≥ 1/T, whatever f is. It is reached only when no decoy win scores at or above the weakest target win. When q_floor > stats.alpha.identification, i.e. fewer than 1/alpha genes are won by a target, no gene can be detected at that alpha. The run then warns q_floor_above_alpha, and q-values and detection calls are reported unchanged (Khoa, 2026-10-22). The row is NA outside the picked tier: MAYU has no +1 term (its q → 0 as the expected false-peptide count → 0), and the two_peptide and protein_list tiers have no gene q.

    Shared ranking key (M4-24)¶

    A target and a decoy can only be compared on a column both of them carry. The picked tier therefore ranks on S, the shared key. Let C_T be the key columns (q, PEP, score) that at least one accepted target carries. S is the part of C_T that has a value on every paired decoy, kept in key order with the same directions. Columns outside S are ignored on both sides. Before M4-24 a column that the decoys lacked was NA on the decoy side, so every decoy ranked last, D(t) = 0 and every target-won gene got q = 1/T. That was a no-decoy estimate labelled decoy_picked. 1. S = C_T: unchanged, no warning. DIA-NN input (q and PEP on both sides) is in this case. 2. ∅ ≠ S ⊊ C_T: decoy_picked on S, with warning decoy_unscored. One NA value in a decoy column takes that column out of S for every row. No decoy row is dropped, because dropping decoys that lack a value is loss that depends on the input, and 1/f does not correct it. A target with NA in a column of S still ranks last and loses to its decoy. That costs power and is conservative. Shorter keys give more ties, and ties go to the decoy (also conservative). The spec's examples 3–5 show this case: with S = {score}, a decoy that outscores its target wins even though it has no q. 3. S = ∅: the decoys cannot compete. They are treated as absent for the tier choice, with decoy_unscored. The no-decoy chain applies (mayu when an accepted target has q or PEP, else two_peptide or protein_list). The gene table equals that of the same input without its decoy rows. The tier rows still report the decoy counts and f. Two inputs reach this case: decoys with no ranking value at all (giapsim M3-19), and targets with none. 4. q filter (ADR-0047). Decoys with q above q_value_max leave the competition only when some C9 target row carries q. Without that, filtering only the decoys would remove weak decoys whose targets all stay. When q ∈ S and q is monotone in the ranking, the filter changes no target's gene q. It changes f, the 80 % guard and the counts. Why ranking on S still controls the gene FDR: the competition needs decoys that are a null for the ranked column on the side that was selected. Targets are accepted on q, but decoys whose q is NA are not filtered. Selection can only remove false targets, so in expectation D(t)/f over the unselected decoys is at least the false-target count above t, and the estimate errs conservative when the score and q disagree (table below, score + noise). The one input this does not cover is accepted targets whose q is NA while their decoys have a q and are filtered (see Sign-off of M4-24, note 1).

    MAYU tier¶

    giap.stats.mayu.mayu_gene_fdr (see mayu-gene-fdr.md) on eligible genes, with sizes n_observable_peptides, P(t) = distinct accepted peptides, F = sum of PEP when every accepted peptide has one. Otherwise F = t |P(t)|, with warning pep_partial when only some have one. fdr_2pep is reported under its own name and is not the gene FDR. Warning unspecific_search: more than 50 % of distinct accepted peptides (placements ending at the gene's C-terminus excepted) do not end in K/R.

    Unestimated tiers¶

    two_peptide: >= detection.min_peptides_sequences_only distinct accepted peptides -> detected; exactly one -> single_peptide_unestimated (not detected in the primary analysis). protein_list: a C12 rank-1 hit with a gene -> detected. Both carry no gene q. Contaminant evidence counts toward detection in every tier (lead decision 2).

    Reading: 1. MAYU at q = 0.01 is at level, with no margin. The implementer's 0.0104 +- 0.0004 was a +1 SE block. With 3x-6x the replicates the mean is 0.0100 (pi 0.01) and 0.0098 (pi 0.05). The estimate itself runs 4-8 % low (ratio > 1, as in mayu-gene-fdr.md: noisy a_g, bin averaging). The step between gene q values absorbs this, so the realised mean lands on q. This is noise around an unbiased-at-q procedure, not material anti-conservatism. Nightly false-alarm cost (normal approximation, two-block rule): MAYU pi 0.01 at q = 0.01 and R = 300 fails one block with probability ~0.05 and both with ~0.0024. Every other gene_fdr claim, the attack included, is below 1e-3. The suite adds ~0.003 to the ~0.04 nightly budget of ADR-0015. 2. PEP against no PEP. In giapsim q is the running mean of PEP, so sum PEP equals t |P(t)| and the two gated claims are one claim. The M4-09 simulator is the one already in use, so it does not separate them on its own. Once q comes from target-decoy competition (D/T over the emitted decoys), the two differ (0.0096 against 0.0101 at pi 0.01) and both stay at level. An EvidenceConfig mode with an engine-like q is needed before the two can be counted as independent evidence (backlog). 3. Decoy-loss attack (ADR-0009). With 1/f, random loss of 10 % or 20 % keeps the level. Without 1/f, 20 % loss inflates FDP by 1.2-1.25x. The attack catches that mutant with probability ~0.4-0.5 per level in the fast suite (both blocks must fail) and ~1.0 in the full suite. At 10 % loss its power is low: ~0.004 fast and ~0.04 full at q = 0.01. With floor(0.3 n) decoys lost, f = 0.70 < 0.80 holds in every replicate, so the "30 %" claim always runs MAYU. It tests the guard, not 1/f. 4. Levels 0.05 and 0.10 are weak and vacuous. Peptides are capped at q <= 0.01, so the loosest gene FDR reachable is ~0.053 and the q = 0.10 claims sit ~200 SE below the bound. Giving them teeth needs a suite variant with input.q_value_max = 0.10 (backlog). 5. Null structure: false targets and decoys land on genes in proportion to n_observable (§8.4), which is MAYU's own model. Departures from it are covered by the M4-02 attacks (mayu-gene-fdr.md). TU blocks and autocorrelation do not enter gene detection. One test family per run (all eligible genes), as in §8.1. Effect size: the gene q is reported next to M4-11's n_accepted and coverage_frac. The FDR curve rows carry no CI (ci_low/ci_high NA). They are estimates of an expectation, as in mayu-gene-fdr.md.

    Sign-off¶

    Re-review (2026-10-15, after f297600 and lead decision 11): changes requested. 1. Decision 5 (PEP-only q) is implemented and calibrated (table above); approved. 2. Decision 11 as implemented ("ANY stripped ID is a contaminant -> out of both counts") is anti-conservative for decoys of mixed groups rev_P;rev_CON__X: their targets P;CON__X still place on P's gene by sequence, so dropping only the decoy is unaccounted decoy loss.
