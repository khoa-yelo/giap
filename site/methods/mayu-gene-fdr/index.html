    MAYU-style gene-level FDR (closed form)¶

    Implements giap.stats.mayu.mayu_gene_fdr (GIAP_SPEC.md §7.3.2 "MAYU-style closed form (G7)", §8.3, Appendix B.3; issue M4-02). Used by the mayu detection tier (M4-12): when the input has no pairable decoy rows, or when more than 20 % of decoys are unpaired (ADR-0009 keeps this as an upper guard; below it the picked tier rescales decoy wins by 1/f).

    What is estimated¶

    Peptide-level q-values do not control gene-level error (§8.7): false peptides pile up on genes that have no true peptide, and each such hit creates a whole false gene. Given F expected false peptides among the accepted ones, MAYU (Reiter et al. 2009) estimates how many of the genes with ≥ 1 accepted peptide owe their detection to false peptides alone.

    Model¶

    1. Where false peptides land. Each false peptide independently picks one eligible gene, gene g with probability a_g / Σ_h a_h, where a_g = n_observable_peptides (floored at 1). This is "false peptides spread uniformly over the database's observable peptides": a random match is equally likely to be any observable peptide, so a gene with twice the observable peptides takes twice the wrong hits.
    2. Size bins. Genes are split into mayu_size_bins equal-count bins by rank of a_g (ties broken by input order; fewer bins when there are fewer genes). Within bin b every gene gets the bin's mean share w_b = mean(a_g ∈ b) / Σ_h a_h, so Σ_b N_b w_b = 1. Rank bins are deterministic and always non-empty; with many ties (e.g. all sizes equal) they give equal w_b and the one-bin answer.
    3. Per-gene hit probability. With F independent false peptides, a given gene in bin b is missed by all of them with probability (1 − w_b)^F, so p_b = 1 − (1 − w_b)^F (binomial, sampling with replacement). F is an expectation and need not be an integer; the power is evaluated directly so w_b = 1, F = 0 gives p_b = 0.

    4. Fixed point. False genes can only arise among genes with no true peptide. Let M_b be that number in bin b. Then E[false genes] = M_b p_b and E[undetected genes] = N_b − n_b = M_b (1 − p_b). Eliminating M_b: FP_b = (N_b − n_b + FP_b) p_b, i.e.

      FP_b(t) = (N_b − n_b(t)) · p_b / (1 − p_b), FDR(t) = Σ_b FP_b(t) / Σ_b n_b(t)

    (FP_b = N_b − n_b when p_b = 1; FDR clipped to 1; NaN when no gene passes). Under the model this is a ratio of unbiased estimates: E[FP_b] equals the expected number of false genes given M_b. 5. F(t). P(t) = accepted peptides with q ≤ t. With PEPs, F(t) = Σ_{P(t)} PEP (the expected number of false peptides if PEPs are calibrated). Without PEPs, F(t) = t·|P(t)|, the q-value's bound on the false count. When q is the running mean of sorted PEPs the two agree at every threshold (the no_pep attack below is numerically identical to the PEP run). PEPs are used for all peptides or none: all-NaN means "no PEPs"; partly-NaN raises (the tier decides once per run). 6. Gene q. Thresholds are the distinct finite best-peptide q values; q_g = min over t ≥ best_q_g of FDR(t) (monotone_q from giap.stats.tda). Detected at q_g ≤ 0.01. This is one test family per run (all eligible genes), matching §8.1's "Gene detected" row. 7. ≥ 2-peptide variant. The chance that a gene gets ≥ 2 false peptides is approximated by Poisson(λ = F·w_b): p_b⁽²⁾ = 1 − e^(−λ)(1 + λ), plugged into the same FP formula with the same n_b, N_b. Reported as fdr_2pep in the curve and in detection_summary.tsv only; it is not a valid FDR for the set of ≥ 2-peptide genes (n_b still counts ≥ 1-peptide genes), only an indication of how much of the error a two-peptide rule would remove.

    Approximations¶

    • Binomial vs hypergeometric. Reiter's MAYU draws false protein IDs from the database without replacement (hypergeometric within a size bin). Here false peptides choose genes with replacement; several false peptides on one gene are allowed and count once. For F ≪ N_b the two coincide; for large F the binomial form is the exact one for independent random matches.
    • Bin averaging. p_g = 1 − (1 − w_g)^F is concave in w_g, so within a bin the mean of p_g is ≤ p(mean w). The bins also mix genes whose absence probabilities differ (short genes are more often absent). Both effects are small with 10 rank bins (simulation below: estimated / realised loose-threshold FDR within ±3.5 % for log-SD 0.6–1.2 sizes); a single bin overstates FDR by ≈ 22 % when absent genes are shorter than average (one bin: est. 0.0323 vs realised 0.0264).
    • Expectation, not a bound. FDR(t) estimates E[false]/n; it carries no +1 and no finite-sample guarantee. Its calibration rests on the model and on F being right.

    Assumptions and when they fail (direction)¶

    Assumption Fails when Direction
    False hits ∝ a_g wrong hits concentrate on long genes beyond a_g (e.g. ∝ a_g²; long, well-covered proteins attract more near-miss matches) conservative (realised / est. ≈ 0.78): more false peptides land on genes that are present anyway
    wrong hits spread more evenly than a_g (∝ a_g^0.5 or uniform; e.g. semi-specific or unspecific search while a_g counts tryptic peptides) anti-conservative: uniform hits give realised / est. ≈ 1.24 and FDP 0.0122 at q = 0.01; a_g^0.5 ≈ 1.14 (R = 100)
    a_g is the right weight a_g is a noisy proxy of the true hit weight (hits ∝ length, a_g = Poisson(length/25), zeros floored) mildly anti-conservative (≈ 1.05; FDP 0.0102 at q = 0.01, R = 300): genes with a_g overstated by noise are more often detected, so the undetected pool is under-weighted
    a_g ≥ 1 floor genes with no observable peptide conservative (they cannot really be hit)
    F is right PEPs miscalibrated (too optimistic), or q-values anti-conservative upstream (Wen 2025, §8.7) FDR scales roughly linearly with F; an underestimated F is the main real-world risk and is not detectable here (validation: entrapment, §15.5)
    Independent hits false peptides cluster (several wrong peptides from one homologous or contaminant protein onto one gene) conservative: fewer distinct false genes than the independent model predicts
    "No true peptide" = undetected present genes whose true peptides were all rejected get hit by a false peptide counted as false by the estimator, as true by an entrapment truth: conservative
    Many single-peptide genes (one-hit wonders) not a violation: n_b counts genes with ≥ 1 peptide and the model is per gene at level (attack one_hit_wonders); the share of single-peptide genes is what fdr_2pep speaks to

    The anti-conservative cases need a hit distribution flatter than the weights. n_observable_peptides comes from the configured enzyme (§7.3.3); when the input search was semi-specific or unspecific the tier should say so in the manifest (recommendation for M4-12: warn and report fdr with weights a_g ∝ length as a sensitivity row).

    Relation to the picked tier and ADR-0009¶

    Picked target–decoy (Savitski 2015) estimates gene FDR from decoy wins, with a +1 and a finite-sample argument; MAYU estimates it from a hit model and F. Picked is preferred whenever decoys are available. ADR-0009 rescales picked decoy wins by 1/f for unpaired decoys and keeps the > 20 % unpaired fallback to MAYU as an upper guard: beyond that share the paired decoys may no longer be a random sample of all decoys, and MAYU's model-based estimate is the safer one. MAYU's own weak spot (hit distribution flatter than a_g) is independent of the decoy-loss problem, so the fallback does not compound errors. M4-12's gene_fdr suite calibrates both tiers (± PEP) and the decoy-loss cases.

    Simulation evidence¶

    tests/calibration/test_mayu_calibration.py (test-author): under the exact model (two size groups aligned with bins), FP is unbiased, the pooled hit rate matches p_b, and mean FDP ≤ mean FDR + 2 SE.

    Numerical notes¶

    • 1 − (1 − w)^F loses relative precision only for w ≲ 1e-9 (relative error ~1e-7 at w = 1e-9, F = 0.01); real genomes have w ≥ 1/Σa ≈ 1e-6. -expm1(F·log1p(−w)) with a w = 1 guard would be exact; not needed.
    • p⁽²⁾ is clipped to ≤ p (always true mathematically: Poisson P(≥ 2) ≤ Poisson P(≥ 1) ≤ binomial P(≥ 1)).
    • Not validated by the function: PEPs outside [0, 1] (a negative PEP lowers F silently), non-finite sizes (NaN sizes give an all-NaN q), non-integer n_bins. Callers (M4-12) build these from validated tables; input validation would be cheap and is recommended.
    • No effect size or CI: gene q is an error-rate estimate, not a test of an effect. The curve's fp and n_genes (the estimated false and total detected genes) are what detection_summary.tsv should report next to the q threshold; no CI is available for a plug-in model estimate, which is why the calibration suites carry the evidence.
