    Islands: expression islands along the replicon¶

    Issues M5-01 (scores, detrending) and M5-03 (scan engine) add the engine helpers; the island step itself is M5-06 (GIAP_SPEC.md §7.3.5(d)). Each section records its own statistician sign-off; the block-bootstrap null and its calibration are signed off with M5-06/M5-07.

    Report text (M9-16)¶

    The block below is copied into the run report's methods section by scripts/dev/sync_report_snippets.py (plain paragraphs and lists only).

    Islands are runs of adjacent transcription units whose detrended abundance scores are jointly high or low along a replicon. Each window sum is compared with the maximum of a TU-block bootstrap of the same scores, taken over every scanned replicon, so one family-wise error rate covers the whole run; a window is reported when its p-value is at most stats.alpha.context.

    The FWER is claimed for a dependence regime, not for a replicon size. The calibration supports it for TU-level autocorrelation up to rho = 0.5 at every replicon size measured, and up to rho = 0.9 at 4,500 genes; there is no evidence beyond that (rho above 0.95, or dependence longer than the 20-TU bootstrap block cap). It is not claimed where strong TU-level autocorrelation is resolved by Moran's I only to a short lag (block length 7 or less): a high Moran's I with block_len 7 or less is outside the claimed domain. No per-replicon statistic certifies the domain, so nothing is flagged; instead the report lists, per replicon:

    • the estimated correlation length (l-hat), or why it was not estimated;
    • the bootstrap block length used (block_len);
    • the lag-1 Moran's I of the detrended TU scores.

    Gene scores: left-censored Blom¶

    giap.stats.scores.blom_scores(x, censored_mask) (§7.3.5(d) step 1, §8.3). Within a replicon the eligible genes are ranked by log2 abundance. Undetected genes are left-censored: they lie below the detection limit, so their order among themselves is unknown, but their position (below every detected gene) is known. All n₀ of them therefore share the average of ranks 1 … n₀, r = (n₀ + 1)/2, and a detected gene gets n₀ + its average rank among the detected values; a detected gene with a very low abundance still ranks above every undetected one (censoring is "at the lowest rank", not "at its own value"). Ties among detected values and the censored block are averaged separately. The score is the Blom normal score Φ⁻¹((r − 3/8)/(n + 1/4)) (scipy.special.ndtri).

    Properties: scores are invariant under strictly increasing transforms of the values and equivariant under permutation; without ties or censoring the scores of ranks r and n + 1 − r sum to 0; a fully censored replicon scores exactly 0 everywhere (r = (n + 1)/2 maps to Φ⁻¹(0.5)). An uncensored NaN or ±inf raises ValueError: the caller decides who is scored.

    The scores are detrended with the shared LOESS helper (docs/methods/autocorrelation.md, "Detrending") along TU order, span 10 % of TUs.

    Scan and maximum statistic (M5-03)¶

    giap.stats.scan (§7.3.5(d) steps 4–6, §8.3). Windows are lengths w = wmin … min(wmax, cap) with cap = n − 1 on circular replicons (no window covers the whole circle; after detrending such a window has S ≈ 0) and n on linear ones. Circular windows start at every position and wrap. They are ordered by length, then start. S_w = Σ_{t∈w} z_t / √n_w comes from prefix sums. The observed series and the null rows use the same kernel, so a null row equal to the observed series reproduces its maximum exactly.

    Family and FWER. One family is all windows of one replicon, both signs. The statistic is M = max_w |S_w|, and p_w = (1 + #{M_b ≥ |S_w|})/(1 + B) (single-step maxT, Westfall–Young), so high and low islands share one α. Ties within 1e-12·max(1, |S|) count as exceedances (conservative). If the null rows are exchangeable with the observed series under the complete null, P(min_w p_w ≤ α) ≤ α exactly. The excess over α is discreteness only, at most 1/(B + 1). With true islands present, the signal is spread into the null rows (permutation or block bootstrap), which raises M_b. The procedure is then conservative: in simulation with planted high islands of 5–60 TUs the false-island rate was 0–0.033 at α = 0.05 (PR #118 review). A planted low (silenced) island, which the step cannot detect, is not conservative in this sense: it is a low-variance stretch at the censored atom, not extra variability spread into the null rows. It gives 0.034–0.036 at R = 500, against 0.020 on the complete null ("Calibration and power" below), which is within α. Being single-step, it is also conservative for secondary islands. The engine is only as valid as the exchangeability of its null rows: iid permutations are not valid under autocorrelation. The production null is the circular block bootstrap with ℓ′ (ADR-0021), calibrated in M5-06/M5-07. Which family applies where (M5-06 decision 46): the engine used alone, one call per replicon against that replicon's own M_b, controls the FWER per replicon, and across k replicons the genome-wide rate is then up to 1 − (1 − α)^k. The M5-06 island step does not use it that way — it pools one genome-wide M_b over every scanned replicon, so one FWER covers the run ("The null and the genome-wide family" below). A replicon with no window (n < wmin) has max_abs NaN and p NaN, which is never significant. A non-finite |S| gives NaN; a negative finite |S| is a caller error and raises (decision 22).

    Why S/√n_w is not standardised for TU size. z_t is the mean of the gene scores of TU t, so Var z_t falls with the TU's gene count. S_w/√n_w has unit variance only when every TU has unit variance. Windows of single-gene TUs have the largest variance and dominate M_b. Windows over large operons then need a larger true shift to exceed the same threshold, so power is lower in operon-dense regions. This is a power cost, not a size cost, as long as the null rows keep the positional mix of TU sizes (the block bootstrap does; iid permutation does not). TU-size-standardised scores were evaluated and rejected (ADR-0021: FWER 0.18 at ρ = 0).

    Merging and the representative. Significant windows of the same direction that share ≥ 1 TU (cyclically on circular replicons) are linked. Islands are the connected components; touching windows stay separate. A window with S = 0 has no direction and is dropped (it cannot be significant, because M_b ≥ 0 gives p = 1). The best window has the largest |S|, then fewer TUs, then the smaller start. Island-level guarantee: a false island (no window overlapping a true island) contains a falsely rejected window, so P(any false island) ≤ the window FWER. Not controlled: the island's extent. Boundary-straddling windows are non-null and significant, so the union of a cluster usually extends past the true island (27–100 % of replicates in simulation). The reported span is therefore the best window's span (≤ wmax = 20 TUs), not the union. An island longer than 20 TUs is represented by a best window of ≤ 20 TUs and can split into two islands; M5-10's Jaccard inherits both effects. S and the effect of the best window are selected (winner's curse; see ADR-0021 on the effect CI).

    Signed off by the statistician (PR #118 review).

    The island step (M5-06)¶

    giap.context.islands (§7.3.5(d) steps 1–10, §8.1 row "Islands", §9.3 tables/islands.tsv). This section is the claim the island table makes; the two sections above are the pieces it is built from.

    Scores, TU series, detrending¶

    Per replicon: the eligible genes (not partial, p_detect present) are Blom-scored with the undetected ones left-censored ("Gene scores" above); z_t is the mean score of the scored genes of TU t; the series is detrended by LOESS over TU ordinals (not base pairs) with span k = ⌈0.1·T⌉ points and circular padding on circular replicons.

    • Missing-value policy (decision 22). A detected gene whose abundance is NaN — possible only under abundance.missing = exclude — is not scored: it is detected, so censoring it at the bottom would be a lie, and its rank is unknown. It therefore leaves the ranking entirely, which changes every other gene's Blom score slightly; a TU with no scored gene left is dropped from the series. Under abundance.missing = min the same gene is scored at the imputed minimum. The two policies are the §8.6 basic sensitivity axis, so the island membership must not move with the policy for its own reasons — see "What is reported, and what the gene list is" below.
    • The detrend attenuates the signal it is scanning for. A boxcar island of w TUs keeps about 1 − 1.7·w/k of its amplitude while w ≲ k/3 and far less beyond. Measured retained amplitude (circular LOESS, span 10 %):
    T (scored TUs) k w = 2 w = 5 w = 10 w = 20
    2,500 250 0.99 0.97 0.93 0.86
    1,000 100 0.97 0.91 0.83 0.66
    400 40 0.91 0.79 0.58 0.32
    200 20 0.83 0.58 0.32 0.16

    Since w ≤ max_tus = 20, this is a few per cent on a chromosome and a near-total loss of the long windows on a replicon of a few hundred TUs: on small replicons the step effectively scans only its short windows. It is a power cost, not a level cost — the null rows are detrended by the same linear smoother.

    The null and the genome-wide family¶

    Each scanned replicon is resampled by circular_block_bootstrap(detrended, ℓ′, B, rng) with ℓ′ = min(20, 2ℓ̂ + 1, T) per replicon (ADR-0021 decision 1; the rule, the evidence for ℓ′ over ℓ̂ and the join bias live in docs/methods/bootstrap.md "Block length"). The null keeps what the data have: whole TU scores move together, so the TU-size mix and the detectability pattern travel with the scores, and blocks of ℓ′ consecutive TUs keep the local autocorrelation. It is a stationary null: it does not keep variation of the local scale along the replicon, which is the source of both breaches recorded below.

    One family, genome-wide (decisions 21 and 46). For replicate b, M_b is the maximum of |S_w| over the windows of every scanned replicon, taken element-wise across replicons at the same b; the replicons are resampled independently, which is the complete null (replicons are independent under it), and the pairing at index b is arbitrary but valid. p_w = (1 + #{M_b ≥ |S_w|})/(1 + B) and a window is reported when p_w ≤ stats.alpha.context. So one FWER of 0.05 covers the whole run, matching the single §8.1 row, instead of 1 − (1 − α)^k over k replicons. The price is paid by the smaller replicons: a 60-TU plasmid raises the common threshold a little and loses power to the chromosome's maximum, which is the correct direction for a run-level claim. A replicon that is not scanned (below) contributes nothing to M_b, consumes no draws and cannot carry an island.

    Validity domain (ADR-0021 Amendment 1)¶

    The FWER is claimed for a dependence regime, not for a replicon size. It is claimed for giapsim ar1_background at ρ ≤ 0.5 at every size measured (557 / 1,001 / 2,501 scored TUs: 0.020 / 0.031 / 0.037 at α = 0.05, R = 1,000, bound 0.0638), for the 4,500-gene chromosome at ρ = 0.9 (0.024, where ℓ̂ ≈ 8 makes ℓ′ = 17 span the 20-TU windows) and for the §8.5 attacks that stay in that regime.

    It is not claimed where strong TU-level autocorrelation is resolved by Moran's I only to a short lag (ℓ′ ≤ 7): ρ = 0.9 gives 0.113 at 557 TUs and 0.095 at 1,001 TUs, and 1,000 single-gene TUs give 0.068 / 0.110 / 0.208 / 0.327 at ρ = 0.7 / 0.8 / 0.9 / 0.95. The scored-TU count decides nothing on its own (400 TUs at ρ 0.5: 0.005; 1,000 TUs at ρ 0.9: 0.095–0.208; 2,500 TUs at ρ 0.9: 0.024); it enters only through the power of the ℓ̂ test, together with ρ, the TU-size distribution and the censored share — the same ρ = 0.9 replicon gives ℓ̂ = 9 and 0.007 uncensored, ℓ̂ = 2 and 0.208 at 55 % detected.

    Two mechanisms, both measured at 1,000 TUs and ρ = 0.9 with the block forced:

    1. Autocovariance beyond the block. Uncensored, Î₁ = 0.78: FWER 0.576 at block 5 against 0.016 at block 19. The bootstrap breaks the dependence at every join, so a block shorter than the correlation length makes the null rows too tame and M_b too small.
    2. Local-scale heterogeneity at matched variance. With 45 % censored the overall variance ratio is ≈ 1.0 and the FWER is still 0.158 at block 5; the block-SD ratio q90/q10 is 1.73 at ρ = 0.9 against 1.24 at ρ = 0. A stationary null gives every window the same scale, so the windows that sit in a locally noisy stretch are tested against too narrow a null. This is the same mechanism as the ori–ter gradient attack (decision 49): left censoring compresses the low half of the Blom scores against the censored atom while the high half spreads, so a gradient makes the detrended series heteroscedastic (local-SD ratio 1.74 against 1.35 without it) and removing censoring removes the inflation (0.066 → 0.011).

    A long block fixes both and costs all the power, which ADR-0021 decision 1 rejected. Local standardisation (dividing by a LOESS fit of |residual|) was measured and rejected: it reduces the inflation (ρ 0.8: 0.106 → 0.032; ρ 0.9: 0.188 → 0.084, still a breach) and absorbs the signal (power on a 20-TU × +2 SD island at ρ 0.5: 0.103 → 0.003). A scale-aware null is on the backlog.

    No flag, and why not (decision 48). Nothing is flagged uncalibrated: no per-replicon statistic certifies the domain, and selecting on one is adverse. ℓ̂, ℓ′, the lag-1 Moran's I, a Bartlett variance ratio of the window sums and a local-scale ratio all fail as keep/flag rules — at ρ = 0.9 and 557 TUs the overall rate is 0.113 but 0.220 among the replicates that look calmest (ℓ̂ ≤ 1, flat local scale), because a small ℓ̂ is exactly what makes the block too short, and the same rule flags 94 % of the perfectly calibrated 2,501-TU ρ = 0.5 cell. Instead the §9.4 run report carries ℓ̂, ℓ′ (block_len) and the lag-1 Moran's I of the detrended TU scores per replicon, so a reader can see where their data sit against the paragraphs above. Reading them: ℓ̂ is reported as 0 when it was not estimated — the replicon was not scanned, or context.islands.block_len fixed it — and block_len is 0 only for a replicon that was not scanned. ℓ̂ = 0 is a sentinel, never a measurement: the estimator is bounded to [1, 20], so a report must render it as "not estimated" and never as the number 0, which would read as "no autocorrelation" — the false reassurance the amendment refuses. The lag-1 Moran's I is computed from the data either way, so a fixed block does not blind the reader. With block_len fixed the calibration above does not apply at all: it is evidence about ℓ′, and the FWER claim is conditional on the default rule.

    The low direction is a structural zero at normal detection rates¶

    Left censoring is not symmetric (decisions 47 and 50). Every undetected gene shares the censored Blom score c = Φ⁻¹(q/2) with q the undetected share, so a fully silenced window of w TUs cannot go below −√w·|c|: about 3.4 at q = 0.45 and w = 20, against a genome-wide critical value of about 4.1–4.9 across the settings measured. low_direction_ceiling(q, w) returns this bound. Measured on a ρ = 0.5 null with 10 / 20 / 40 consecutive TUs silenced entirely: ceiling 3.42 / 3.38 / 3.35, M_b 95th percentile 4.92 / 4.57 / 4.49, best low p = 1.000, no low island at any α. The high direction has no such cap — the top Blom score is about +3.3 at these sizes — which is why planted low islands are missed on giapsim at the default detection rate while high ones are found.

    The bound is exact for the raw TU scores; the detrended statistic differs from it by √w times the window's mean trend, so it can sit slightly below the ceiling when the window lies on a locally high trend (measured −2.77 detrended against −2.42 raw at 10 silenced TUs) — far below the critical value either way. The procedure is two-sided and costs the high direction almost nothing, but a null result in the low direction is not evidence of no silenced island unless the censored share is small (roughly below a quarter undetected). M5-07 records low-island power separately and labels it a structural zero with the ceiling and the critical value printed beside it; the run report prints the ceiling against the 95th percentile of M_b per replicon.

    What is reported, and what the gene list is¶

    Each cluster of merged significant same-direction windows is reported by its best window, never by the union (decision 42, "Merging and the representative" above): tu_first/tu_last, n_tus, unwrapped start/end (end > length when the window crosses the origin), scan_s, block_len, p_value (FWER), q_value NA, method = scan_max_stat, null_model = circular_block_bootstrap.

    genes, n_genes and detected_frac cover all eligible genes of the window's TUs, including a detected gene that was dropped from the scores for want of an abundance. The asymmetry with the scoring is deliberate: the statistic is an inference about the scored series, while the gene list is a description of the locus, and it is the gene list that the §8.6 sensitivity Jaccard compares across abundance.missing ∈ {min, exclude}. If membership followed the scored set, that comparison would mostly measure the missing-value policy instead of the island's extent, and detected_frac would stop being comparable to the replicon-level detected fraction and to the §7.3.5(c) region tests, which also count detection over eligible genes. Two consequences to keep in mind: detected_frac·n_genes can exceed the number of genes behind mean_abundance_log2 (which averages only detected genes that have a value), and a TU with no scored gene at all is absent from the series, so the span start … end can contain eligible genes that genes does not list.

    The effect and its CI: a selected interval¶

    effect is the mean detrended score of the best window's TUs — identically scan_s/√n_tus, the test statistic on a per-TU scale — with a 95 % percentile tu_block_bootstrap CI over that window's TUs (decision 26). Read it as a descriptive size, not as a test. Four caveats, all in the anti-conservative direction:

    • Winner's curse. The window was chosen as the genome-wide maximum of |S|, so both the point estimate and the interval are conditional on that selection: effect is biased away from zero, the CI under-covers the underlying island mean, and it essentially never contains zero — so it carries no independent evidence that the island is real. The p_value is the only test. On a 600-gene null with the threshold opened to α = 0.9, the three reported windows have effects +2.35, +2.33, +1.67 with CIs [1.88, 2.83], [1.70, 2.96], [1.44, 1.99] — every one excluding zero — at p = 0.70, 0.86, 0.13.
    • Within-window dependence is ignored. The CI resamples TUs independently inside the window; with positive autocorrelation that understates the variance and the interval is too narrow.
    • Scale. The units are detrended Blom scores, not log2 fold change, and the detrend has already attenuated the island by the factor tabulated above.
    • Few units. Windows of 2–9 TUs carry the M4-16 flag ci_few_units: a percentile bootstrap over so few units is coarse (at 2 TUs the interval is essentially the two values).

    The realised coverage of this CI is not yet measured (backlog).

    Degenerate cases¶

    Situation Behaviour

    The plasmid is not always scanned. The 60-gene plasmid has 21–46 scored TUs (median 34). With T ≤ 30 the LOESS span ⌈0.1·T⌉ is below 4 points, so the replicon is not scanned (few_values, decision 40). That happened in 93 / 89 / 98 of 500 replicates at ρ = 0 / 0.5 / 0.9, about 19 %. The two-replicon family was therefore exercised in 402–411 replicates per cell, with FWER 7/407, 9/411 and 11/402 at α = 0.05. An unscanned plasmid cannot carry an island and adds nothing to M_b; the genome-wide-family claim rests on the 81 % of replicates where it was scanned.

    Power (recorded, not gated; §7.3.6). scripts/dev/island_power.py, Slurm 1868246 (commit 20e5273), R = 100 per cell. Layout: the 4,500-gene island_power scenario at ρ = 0.5, island starting at chromosome TU 625, stats.bootstrap = stats.permutations = 1000, α = 0.05. The layout carries no regions: the scenario has no region effect and the island step reads no region, so this changes the random stream, not the distribution of anything the step sees. Power is the share of replicates with a same-direction island sharing at least one TU with the planted island; J is the TU Jaccard of the best such island's best window with the planted TUs, averaged over hits (unstable below 5 hits); false is the share of replicates with an island that shares no TU with the planted one (an island counts as false when its best window misses the planted TUs, even if its cluster touches them, which biases this rate upward). Null FWER of this layout: 0.020 [0.011, 0.036] (gate cell ρ = 0.5).

    The planning target of ≥ 0.8 is not met under giapsim defaults: 0.20 at 20 TUs × 2 SD. When detection follows abundance (0.65), a high island also raises detection, and the left-censored Blom score turns that into larger scores. Pooled false-island rates with a planted island: high cells 18/900 = 0.020 (covariates_only) and 16/900 = 0.018 (abundance); low cells 36/900 = 0.040 and 24/900 = 0.027. At R = 500 the low 20-TU cells give 0.034 (−1 SD) and 0.036 (−2 SD): within α but above the complete-null 0.020. All of these are high-direction islands, mostly more than 300 TUs from the planted one (see "Scan and maximum statistic").

    Several strong islands mask each other (MVP-04 gate 13, Slurm 1872831, 1872881). The block bootstrap resamples the observed series, planted islands included, so the null maxima M_b contain copies of the islands. When the islands are few and short relative to ℓ′, the copies are often intact and M_b sits at the islands' own S. On the K-12 evidence layout (RegulonDB TUs, ρ = 0.5, q ≈ 0.38) three 8-TU high islands at +3 SD have median S 4.7–6.7 (range 3.5–7.3), raise ℓ̂ from a median of 4 to 5 (ℓ′ 7–19, at least 9 in 21 of 22 runs, longer than an 8-TU island), and lift the 95th percentile of M_b from 5.0–5.7 (no islands) to 6.3–7.4. They were recovered in 3 of 33 island-seeds with truth detection (Wilson [0.03, 0.24]; 1 of 33 with GIAP-like detection). A single 20-TU island at +3 SD also raises the 95th percentile of M_b (6.3–9.5), but its S of 7.5–10.3 stays above it: it was recovered in 21 of 22 seeds ([0.78, 0.99]). The one miss had ℓ̂ = 12, ℓ′ = 20, so an intact copy fits in a block. The three 8-TU low islands of the same layout had S −1.6 to −3.3 against a ceiling of 2.2–2.5 and were never recovered (0/330 across all variants, [0, 0.012]). This is the structural zero above. The FWER is unaffected: no spurious island appeared in 220 runs ([0, 0.017]). The effect is a power loss specific to the stationary maxT null, and it is conservative. A missing call among several similar islands is therefore not evidence that they are absent. MVP-04 gates one 20-TU high island for this reason and reports its low islands as exempt with their ceiling. A null that masks the top windows before estimating ℓ̂ is on the backlog.
