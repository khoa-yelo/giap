    Genome decoys (M3-03)¶

    What the decoy has to estimate¶

    A placement is wrong when a wrong peptide (a misidentified spectrum or a de novo error) happens to occur in the six-frame translation. For one wrong peptide the number of such chance hits depends only on the search space: the set of distinct stop- and X-free L-mers of the six frames (each peptide counts once at its best cost, §7.2.5 step 1, so multiplicity of a word does not matter), and the peptide law. The decoy search space must give a wrong peptide the same hit probability as the target space, while giving a correct peptide (which is in the target by construction) only that same chance probability. Then D (peptides with a decoy hit at cost m) estimates the number of wrong peptides with a chance target hit at cost m, and π̂_wrong (D + 1)/T is the stratum FDR (M3-10).

    Formally, for a peptide law Q and a space with distinct words W, P(hit) = Σ_{w ∈ W} Q(w) (exact search; for ≤ m differences replace W by its m-neighbourhood). The decoy is valid for Q when Σ_{W_D} Q = Σ_{W_T} Q.

    The two constructions¶

    Both act on the I→L-folded uint8 frames of build_sixframe, frame by frame, and return frames of the same contig, strand, offset and length (build_decoys).

    reverse (database input) shuffle (de novo input)
    Operation frame string reversed inside each stop-free segment, non-X residues cut into consecutive windows of 50 from the segment start (last window shorter); each window permuted uniformly (Fisher–Yates), seeded for_step("sixframe_index", seed)
    * and X mirrored (position n−1−i) fixed in place
    Frame composition identical identical
    Per-window composition identical (mirrored windows) identical (exactly, per 50-aa window)
    Stop-free segment lengths and X positions same multiset, mirrored identical
    Number of L-mer positions without */X identical identical
    Local order kept, reversed (dipeptide xy → yx) destroyed below 50 aa; composition gradient above 50 aa kept
    Repeats / low-complexity runs kept (a repeat stays a repeat) homopolymer runs kept; periodic repeats and multi-copy elements destroyed
    Mirror map (§7.6.4, mirror_aa) [n − end, n − start) identity

    Same search-space size. The decoy frames have the same frames, the same length, the same * and X geometry (identical or mirrored) and therefore exactly the same number of candidate L-mer positions for every L (verified for L = 7, 8 in every replicate of the attack suite). The I→L fold is applied before decoying, so neither space can match I/L differently. The circular extension (first 120 nt appended, §7.2.1) is decoyed with its frame: for reverse the duplicated words stay duplicated, so distinct-word counts agree exactly; for shuffle the extension is shuffled independently of the origin region, which adds at most 40 distinct decoy words per frame per circular replicon (≈ 2 × 10⁻⁵ of a 5-Mb space, conservative, negligible).

    Why reverse for database input¶

    • Exact under any i.i.d. peptide law. If Q(w) = Π Q₁(wⱼ) then Q(rev w) = Q(w), and W_D = rev(W_T) word for word, so Σ_{W_D} Q = Σ_{W_T} Q exactly — for every genome, including repeat-rich and low-complexity genomes, and for every difference budget (the m-neighbourhood of rev w is the reverse of the m-neighbourhood of w for substitutions; adjacent swaps and the mass-equivalence operations are closed under reversal because the operation tables list both orders). Positional laws that are not reversal-symmetric (tryptic C-terminal K/R) still agree in expectation when the genome's L-mers are reversal-exchangeable; the attack suite confirms it on giapsim genomes.
    • It keeps everything structural. Composition per window, repeats, low-complexity runs and the stop geometry are kept, so the decoy reproduces the de-duplication that repeats impose on the target (see the low-complexity results below: shuffle over-counts by 40–70 % there, reverse is exact).
    • Deterministic and seed-free, and its own inverse (mirror_aa is an involution).
    • It is also the standard choice of proteomic search engines, so database peptides are not systematically "close to" reversed sequence (input decoy rows are never mapped, M3-17).

    Why shuffle for de novo input (§8.5 attack 5)¶

    De novo errors frequently reverse a short stretch of a correct peptide (b/y-ion ambiguity). A peptide with a reversed internal stretch, or a fully reversed one, matches the reversed frame near the true locus far more often than chance, so a reversed decoy would inflate D with correct-locus peptides and make the de novo FDR uselessly conservative and the de novo precision estimator (§7.2.5, r_bℓ) biased. A window shuffle breaks all order below 50 aa, so such peptides hit the decoy only at chance rate, while the 50-aa windows keep local composition (codon bias, hydrophobic stretches, AT-rich non-coding frames), which is what per-position match probability depends on.

    Window size. 50 aa is a compromise: windows must be much longer than the longest tolerant peptide window that matters for chance hits (≤ 40 aa) so that a peptide-length stretch is a near-random draw from its window, and short enough to keep composition gradients (genes vs. intergenic, GC skew, transmembrane segments). Windows restart at each stop and skip X, so a window never mixes two stop-free segments. With window 1 the shuffle is the identity (unit-tested); with an unbounded window (whole segment) the attack results were indistinguishable at 100 kb (mean D/T expected-hit ratio 1.003 vs. 1.000 at L = 7; exploratory, 20 genomes).

    What each decoy is a null for¶

    Matching reverse shuffle
    Exact (Route 1) exact null for any i.i.d. (and any reversal-symmetric) wrong-peptide law, any genome null for i.i.d. wrong peptides on genomes without heavy repeats; conservative with repeats
    Tolerant, ≤ m differences (Route 3) same, because the five cost-1 operations are closed under reversal same as exact, with the m-neighbourhood
    Frameshift splits (M3-08) decoy splits by mirrored adjacency (M3-08) identity map keeps frame adjacency

    Neither decoy is a null for wrong peptides that share sequence order with the genome's own proteins (see "Limitations").

    Evidence¶

    Implementation review (M3-03, src/giap/mapping/decoys.py).

    • The shuffle swaps slot l of a window with a uniform slot in [0, l] in increasing l (Durstenfeld's forward variant of Fisher–Yates), which is uniform over the l! orders by induction; swaps never leave the window (j = s − local[s] + draw), so per-window multisets, * and X positions are preserved exactly (property-tested, tests/property/test_decoys_props.py).
    • Windows restart at * but not at X (X skipped), as the issue specifies.
    • Determinism: one generator for all frames in index order, one draw per shuffled residue (rng.integers(0, local + 1)); reverse draws nothing. Seed-identical runs are byte-identical. Note: the draws are not rng.permutation as the issue text says; this is statistically equivalent, but the stream is fixed by this implementation and must not change silently (golden tables, §6.6).

    Attack suite (tests/calibration/attacks/test_attack_genome_decoys.py). Fast suite: R = 50 giapsim genomes of 100 kb (seeds 20261014 + i); full suite R = 300. For each genome the exact expected hit probability of an i.i.d. peptide drawn from the genome's six-frame residue frequencies is computed on the distinct L-mers of the target and of each decoy (no peptide sampling noise), and three sampled peptide sets are searched.

The test-author's unit test (one 3-residue window, 600 seeds) is adequate as a smoke test (it catches the classic "swap with any slot" bias already at n = 3) but tests neither independence between windows nor the X/* geometry; the attack suite covers those.

    Exploratory runs, 1-Mb synthetic genomes, 4 × 10⁵ sampled 7-mers each:

    Chance hits and the ranking score (M3-09)¶

    The model¶

    A wrong peptide of length L is modelled as L residues drawn independently from the genome's six-frame composition f, and every searchable target window as L residues drawn independently from the same f, independently of the peptide. Then one position matches with probability s = Σ_a f_a², a window is within ≤ m substitutions with probability Σ_{j≤m} C(L, j)(1 − s)^j s^(L−j) (j mismatching positions out of L, each mismatching with probability 1 − s), and the expected number of chance windows is

    E(L, m) = N Σ_{j≤m} C(L, j)(1 − s)^j s^(L−j) (§7.2.5, B.2; expected_hits_uniform).

    The per-peptide ranking score E_i (expected_hits) conditions on the peptide's residues: position i mismatches a random target residue with probability 1 − f_{a_i}, the mismatch count is Poisson-binomial, and E_i = N · P(≤ m mismatches). Averaged over i.i.d.-f peptides, E_i equals E(L, m) exactly (positions are independent and E_f[1 − f_a] = 1 − s); the calibration test confirms it by simulation at L = 8.

    Composition f (residue_freqs): counts of the 19 residue codes over the target frames, I and L merged (the frames are I→L folded before anything else, so neither target nor decoy can match I/L differently); * and X are excluded from f. Query letters outside the 19 (U, O, B, Z, J, X) get f = 0: they never match, so they always count as a mismatch.

    N (n_positions) is the number of six-frame codon positions whose first nucleotide lies in the replicon: for a circular replicon one per nucleotide and strand (≈ 2 × genome length), the 120-nt circular extension excluded, but every position near the origin still starts a full window that reads through the extension (origin-crossing windows are searchable and counted once); for a linear contig the last ≤ L − 1 positions of each frame have no full window but are counted (negligible). As the spec defines it, N counts all positions, including * and X and windows that contain them. Those windows can never match, so the number of searchable windows is W_L < N (next section).

    Substitutions only. E counts substitution neighbours. The tolerant matcher also accepts adjacent swaps and mass-equivalent blocks at cost 1, so the true cost-≤ m neighbourhood is larger than E assumes (lead default, M3-09 Q1). This does not matter for validity: E is a ranking score, not an FDR estimate. Target and decoy E are computed by the same function from the peptide sequence and the same genome f (decoys keep the target composition exactly, see Genome decoys), so E is exchangeable between a wrong peptide's target and decoy hits; the decoy count D (which uses all operations) calibrates the FDR. An error in E costs power (a worse ordering), never validity. E also counts hit positions with multiplicity, whereas a peptide counts once in the strata: P(≥ 1 hit) ≤ E, ≈ E when E ≪ 1, which is the range that is accepted.

    Reproduction of B.2¶

    expected_hits_uniform(L, m, 0.069, 1e7) against exact rational arithmetic: relative error ≤ 1.3 × 10⁻¹⁵ in all 21 cells; every cell rounds to the B.2 table (e.g. (7, 0) 0.0744635, (8, 1) 0.559742, (9, 2) 2.36692, (10, 1) 0.00332505, (12, 2) 0.00141835, (14, 1) 1.05295 × 10⁻⁷; L = 6: 1.08, 88.4, 3.04 × 10³).     L simulated (± SE) E(L, 1) ratio B.2 ≤ 1
    8 0.561 ± 0.006 0.5597 1.002 0.56
    9 0.0440 ± 0.0007 0.0434 1.015 0.043

    When the independence assumption fails¶

    Attack tests/calibration/attacks/test_attack_chance_hits.py (statistician). For a peptide drawn i.i.d. from the genome's own f, the exact expected number of hit windows is Σ_{searchable windows t} P(≤ m mismatches | t), mismatch probability 1 − f(t_j) at position j (closed form for m ≤ 2; no peptide sampling, cross-checked against sampled exact hits).     1. Stops and X (factor W_L / N ≈ (1 − p_stop)^L, here 0.56–0.91; p_stop ≈ 3/64 for GC 50 %): N counts positions that cannot start a match. With i.i.d. codons this is the only error (cond = 1 at any composition, including a strongly skewed GC 75 % genome: the model handles composition skew through f).
    2. Heterogeneous composition and dipeptide structure. Coding, antisense and intergenic frames have different residue laws g_k; for an i.i.d.-f peptide E[Π f(t_j)] = Σ_k w_k (Σ_a f_a g_k(a))^L ≥ s^L by Jensen, and codon/dipeptide structure inside genes adds to it. E under-states the conditional count by 2–7 % (growing with L, shrinking with m). On realistic genomes the stop factor dominates, so E stays conservative overall (total 0.64–0.76).
    3. Low complexity. Simple-sequence repeats put long runs of a few abundant residues into the target (poly-A, poly-P, (AR)n, …); windows there have Π f(t_j) ≫ s^L. E under-states the absolute count, strongly and increasingly with L (×1.9 at L = 8, ×15 at L = 12, exact matches). By construction the excess comes from peptides that are themselves rich in the repeat residues (an exact or 1-substitution match to a repeat window needs a near-repeat peptide): those hit far more often than their E_i says, while typical peptides are barely affected. This is the same structure that made the shuffled decoy anti-conservative for periodic wrong peptides (Genome decoys › Limitations, D/T 0.88) and that the reverse decoy preserves exactly.

    Consequence. Since E is only a ranking score, none of these errors affects FDR validity as long as the decoy is a valid null (reverse: exact for any i.i.d. law on any genome; shuffle: see Genome decoys). They affect (a) power, through the ordering: the stop factor varies with L, so pooling lengths within a stratum (G3) ranks long peptides slightly too pessimistically relative to short ones (≈ 3–5 % per residue against a factor ≈ 1/s ≈ 14 per residue in E itself: immaterial); low-complexity peptides are ranked too optimistically, which is the case for the low_complexity placement flag recommended to M3-10; (b) any absolute reading of E (a reported "expected chance hits" column, or the §7.2.5 table read as a property of a real genome): E over-states real genomes by 1.3–1.8× and under-states low-complexity peptides by up to an order of magnitude. If E is ever reported as an absolute expectation, use W_L instead of N and flag low-complexity peptides.

    Numerical accuracy of the Poisson-binomial¶

    giap.stats.poisson_binomial (shared with M4-02) computes the pmf by the standard DP \(\mathrm{pmf}_{i+1}[j] = \mathrm{pmf}_i[j](1 - p_i) + \mathrm{pmf}_i[j - 1]\, p_i\), O(n²) time, O(n) memory. Every step is a convex combination of non-negative numbers, so there is no cancellation: each entry has relative error ≤ ≈ 2n·u (u = 1.1 × 10⁻¹⁶) whatever its size, and tiny tails stay exact to relative precision rather than to absolute 10⁻¹⁶. The cdf and sf are summed directly from the pmf (never 1 − the other), as §8.3 requires. Checks (review, 2026-10-14):

    • brute-force enumeration of all 2ⁿ outcomes, n = 0–12, 260 random p vectors including p^20-skewed ones: max relative pmf error 2.5 × 10⁻¹⁵; cdf and sf for every k ∈ [−1, n + 1] within 10⁻¹⁴ absolute;
    • exact rational arithmetic, n = 60, p ∈ [0, 0.1]: max relative error 6.0 × 10⁻¹⁶ over the whole pmf, including the extreme tail P(X = 60) = 4.75 × 10⁻⁸³;
    • n = 5,000 (M4-02 scale): Σ pmf − 1 = 4 × 10⁻¹⁶, 36 ms;
    • E at L = 40, m = 0 stays positive (7 × 10⁻⁴⁵ for a uniform-f poly-A query at N = 10⁷).

    Limits (outside the peptide range, documented only): entries below ≈ 10⁻³⁰⁸ underflow to 0 (n ≳ 230 at p = 0.05 for P(X = n)); expected_hits_uniform uses exact integer binomial coefficients and raises OverflowError for L ≳ 1,000 with m ≈ L/2. The vectorised in-place update reads its right-hand side before assigning, so there is no aliasing.

    • Origin off-by-two (minor, requested fix). _core_residues keeps a minus-strand codon when its first (5′-on-minus, highest-coordinate) nucleotide is < replicon length. The two minus-strand codons whose 5′ nucleotide is replicon position 0 or 1 lie wholly in the extension in the frame coordinates and are dropped, although they are unique origin-crossing codons, so a circular replicon gets N = 2·length − 2 instead of 2·length (checked at length 9, 10, 11, 300 and 100 kb).     • N includes */X positions as the spec defines it; documented above with its direction (conservative ×0.56–0.91), not a defect.

    π̂_wrong in the stratum FDR (M3-10)¶

    Scope: how a π̂_wrong that varies between peptides enters FDR_m(e) (§7.2.5 step 3). In practice this is de novo input in stratum 0, where π̂ is per score bin (step 4, M3-11). Database input in stratum 0 (one π̂ for all) and every stratum m ≥ 1 (π̂ = 1) have a constant π̂, and the rule below reduces exactly to the spec formula there. This section replaces lead decision M3-10 Q2 ("mean π̂ of the targets with E ≤ e"), which is anti-conservative.

    Model and assumptions¶

    Fix a stratum m and a decoy type. Every mapped query peptide j of that decoy type is searched against target and decoy (lead decision M3-10 Q1). Let π_j = P(j wrong | its score bin) and π̂_j its estimate.

    • A1 (decoy hit ⇔ chance target hit of the same peptide). For a wrong peptide j, (target hit at cost m, its E) and (decoy hit at cost m, its E) have the same law: p_j(e) = P(hit with E ≤ e) for both (Genome decoys; E is computed by the same function for both, M3-09).
    • A2 (decoy hits do not depend on correctness). The decoy hit of j has the same law p_j(e) whether j is correct or wrong: a correct peptide's decoy hits are chance hits too.
    • A3 (π̂ is a peptide-level label). π̂_j depends on the peptide's score bin only, not on its hits; it is the same for the peptide's target and decoy hit.

    p_j(e) may vary freely between peptides (length, composition) and may be correlated with π_j.

    Derivation¶

    The number of false targets with E ≤ e is V(e) = Σ_j 1{j wrong} 1{chance target hit of j, E ≤ e}, so by A1 E[V(e)] = Σ_j π_j p_j(e). Candidate estimators of V(e) and their expectations:

    Estimator of V(e) Expectation Unbiased when
    Q2 as written: π̄_T(e) · D(e), π̄_T(e) = mean π̂ of targets with E ≤ e π̄_T(e) Σ_j p_j(e) π̄_T(e) = Σ π_j p_j / Σ p_j; fails in general
    (a) π̄_S · D(e), π̄_S = mean π̂ over all searched peptides π̄_S Σ_j p_j(e) Cov_j(π_j, p_j(e)) = 0
    (b) W(e) = Σ_{decoy hits j with E ≤ e} π̂_j Σ_j π_j p_j(e) (A2, A3) always (given A1–A3 and π̂ = π)

    Why Q2 fails: a correct peptide always has its target hit, a wrong one only by chance, so the targets with small E are mostly correct peptides from the good (low-π) bins and π̄_T(e) is far below the decoy-hit-weighted π̄ that D(e) needs; D counts chance hits of all searched peptides, most of which come from the bad bins. The bias is largest at the top of the list, i.e. at the operating point α = 0.01. Why (a) is not enough: chance-hit rates vary by a factor ≈ 14 per residue of length (M3-09), and low-score de novo bins plausibly hold shorter peptides, so Cov(π, p) > 0 is the realistic case and (a) is then anti-conservative by Σπp / (π̄ Σp).

    The "+1" (§7.2.5, B.6) guards the top of the list against D = 0 by pre-counting one decoy. With weights, one more decoy hit can weigh up to max π̂, so the pre-count must be c = max π̂. Using the mean π̂ instead (c = π̄_S) left a small excess on B.6-sized lists with very unequal bins (mean FDP 0.0113 ± 0.0004 at q = 0.01, N = 150, π = 0.02 / 0.9, R = 3,000).

    Signed rule¶

    Within stratum m and decoy type, one list ordered by E (unchanged), at every target E = e:

    FDR_m(e) = (W_m(e) + c) / T_m(e), W_m(e) = Σ over peptides j with a decoy hit at cost m and E_D,j ≤ e of π̂_j, c = max π̂ over all searched peptides of that decoy type in the stratum's input,

    and q_i = min over e ≥ E_i of FDR_m(e), clipped to 1; ties as before (a decoy tied with a target counts). Definitions:

    • π̂_j is the peptide's own π̂ by the step-4 route, whether it contributes a target, a decoy or both: database m = 0 the one pi_wrong_database value; de novo m = 0 its pi_denovo (1 if missing); m ≥ 1 always 1. A decoy-only peptide carries its own π̂.
    • "Searched peptides of that decoy type" = every peptide of that source in the costs table that enters the decoy search, i.e. all rows of the source except same_db exact peptides in stratum 0 (they leave both stratum-0 lists). If the set is empty, c = 1. Any de novo peptide without π̂ makes c = 1.
    • Constant π̂ = π: W = π D, c = π, FDR = π (D + 1)/T, identical to the spec and to tda_qvalues(−E_T, −E_D, π, plus_one=True).
    • Monotonicity: q is non-decreasing in E within the stratum; adding a decoy hit, raising any π̂_j of a decoy, or raising c never lowers a q.
    • mapping_fdr rows (per decoy type × length class × m, no threshold): decoys = D (count, as before), fdr_stratum = (W + c)/T with W summed over the class's decoy hits and the stratum's c (NA when T = 0), pi_wrong = (W + c)/(D + 1), the effective π̂, so fdr_stratum = pi_wrong (D + 1)/T still holds and pi_wrong equals the constant π̂ for database input and m ≥ 1.

    Suggested interface (implementer's choice, the semantics above bind): stratum_qvalues(e_target, e_decoy, pi_decoy, pi_plus), pi_decoy one weight per decoy, pi_plus = c (scalar in (0, 1]).

    Alternative considered: separate lists per π̂ value (score bin)¶

    Running stratum 0 separately per score bin (the Q2 alternative) applies the spec formula with a constant π̂ in each list. It is also valid in every scenario above (it needs A1–A3 only within a bin) and has more power at α = 0.01 on large inputs with unequal bins (N = 4,000: 1,336 vs 1,063 accepted with 10 bins, 1,969 vs 1,069 with 2 bins), less at q ≥ 0.05 and on small inputs (10 bins N = 300 at 0.01: 55 vs 17 for (b), 21 vs 0 at N = 150). It splits a stratum into several lists, so q is no longer monotone in E within a stratum (§7.2.5 step 3 and the M3-10 acceptance criterion), and mapping_fdr would need a bin dimension. It is therefore a spec change for the lead (ADR), not the default; if adopted, the attack above applies unchanged.

    Forwarded¶

    • Database input, mean PEP. The spec's single π̂ = mean PEP is estimator (a): unbiased only if PEP is uncorrelated with the chance-hit rate. Short peptides have both higher PEP and many more chance hits, so mean PEP can under-state the false-placement share (10-bin correlated scenario: 0.0126 at q = 0.01; database PEPs are small, so the absolute effect is bounded by the PEPs).     Strata and ranking (M3-10)¶

    • Search. Every mapped query peptide (non-decoy, q ≤ input.q_value_max or q missing) is searched against the decoy of its own source (database → reverse, de novo → shuffle; both built for mixed input) exactly as against the target (exact for all, tolerant for all ≥ 10 aa; lead decision M3-10 Q1). Each peptide counts once at its best target cost m_T and, independently, once at its best decoy cost m_D (separate search, not competition: a peptide may be a target in stratum 0 and a decoy in stratum 1). Decoy hits are de-duplicated on their mirrored target interval, never placed and never written to peptide_hits.tsv.
    • Lists. One list per decoy type × m ∈ {0, 1, 2}: T = peptides with m_T = m, D = peptides with m_D = m, both ranked by E ascending (M3-09; target and decoy E from the same function and the same genome f). Length classes are pooled inside a list (G3): E already carries the length (≈ ×14 per residue), so pooling only orders long against short peptides by their chance rate; per-class lists would have small T and a +c that dominates (B.6). The per length class × m rows of mapping_fdr.tsv are descriptive: T and D of the class, fdr_stratum = (W_class + c)/T_class with the stratum's c, no threshold; they are not the q-values.
    • FDR and q. FDR_m(e) = (W_m(e) + c)/T_m(e) at every target E (rule and derivation in "π̂_wrong in the stratum FDR"); q_i = min over e ≥ E_i; ties: all entries with E ≤ e count, so a decoy tied with a target counts against it and tied targets get one q; clipped to [0, 1] by M4-01's monotone_q, so with constant π̂ the q-values equal tda_qvalues(−E_T, −E_D, π̂) (unit- and property-tested). Accept at q ≤ stats.alpha.placement (0.01, inclusive).
    • Families (§8.1). Each list is one FDR family: "placed (exact)" = stratum 0, "placed with ≥ 1 difference" = strata 1 and 2 with π̂ = 1, separately per decoy type. Each accepted set has FDR ≤ α; their union has marginal FDR E[V]/E[R] ≤ α exactly and FDR ≤ α up to the weighting of the ratios (each list pays its own +c, which makes the union slightly conservative). A peptide accepted in stratum m is accepted at all its loci of cost m: multi-locus ambiguity is uniqueness (M3-14), not FDR.
    • Effect size. The per-peptide effect measure next to placement_q is the ranking score E (expected chance windows, conservative ×1.3–1.8 on real genomes, see M3-09) and m_T; the list quantities T, D, W, c are in mapping_fdr.tsv. A target-decoy q-value has no CI of its own; its uncertainty is the +c pre-count (B.6).

    B.6. Stratum 1, π̂ = 1 (c = 1): 150 targets before the first decoy give FDR = (0 + 1)/150 = 0.0067, all 150 accepted at 0.01; 50 targets and no decoys give 1/50 = 0.02, none accepted. With per-peptide de novo π̂ the floor is c/T with c = max π̂, so a de novo stratum-0 list needs T ≥ c/α targets before the first decoy (90 at c = 0.9): small de novo inputs are conservative by design (attack two_bins_small: FDP 0.0063 at 0.01).

    Same-strain input (M3-10)¶

    §7.2.5 step 5: database peptides from a search against this genome's own proteins (search_db = same) with an exact target hit get placement_q = 0 and flag same_db, and leave both stratum-0 lists. Rationale: the engine's own target-decoy already controls the identification error against exactly these sequences, and an exact hit of an own-protein peptide is its source locus; the remaining question is uniqueness (M3-14).     Cross-strain input and run collapse (ADR-0019, M3-25)¶

    Status: signed off by the statistician (M3-25 review of #124, 2026-10-20; ADR-0019 decisions 1–5). Decision and evidence: docs/adr/ADR-0019-stratum0-pi-hat-exact-share.md (accepted by Khoa 2026-10-20; Slurm 1860123, 1860138, 1860180). This section supersedes the mean-PEP row of "π̂_wrong sources" for database input in stratum 0 and resolves the item forwarded from ADR-0010 (π̂ correlated with the chance-hit rate).

    Why mean PEP fails. Two defects, both measured in M3-21:

    1. Cross-strain input (search_db = other). Some correct identifications report the database strain's sequence across a strain difference. They have no exact true locus, so an exact chance hit elsewhere is a false stratum-0 placement, while their PEP measures identification error only and is small. Mean PEP counts only the identification errors among the peptides without an exact origin and under-estimates the false placements by about (π_wrong + δ)/π_wrong, δ = the spanning share of the correct identifications.
    2. Run collapse. §5.3 collapses a peptide's runs with pep = min. The mean of min-collapsed PEPs is not a peptide-level wrong share: with 3 runs M3-21 measured π̂ ≈ 0.0026 against a true wrong share ≈ 0.024. No PSM-level aggregation (min, mean, max, product) is calibrated in general, so placement stops reading PEP instead of changing §5.3's aggregation, which ranking, detection and evidence still use.

    The rule. Population P: all database rows except same_db exact peptides (unchanged from M3-10) and except contaminants (M3-25 decision 7 — they are not genome-derived, so they carry no information about the chance-hit rate). Per length class ℓ of mapping.length_classes:

    • n_ℓ = peptides of P in ℓ; k_ℓ = those with no exact target hit (m_T ≠ 0 or no hit at all); r_ℓ = share of n_ℓ with an exact decoy hit;
    • U_ℓ = one-sided 95 % Clopper–Pearson upper bound of k_ℓ/n_ℓ (1 when k_ℓ = n_ℓ), mirroring step 4's "1 − lower 95 % bound";
    • π̂_ℓ = 1 if r_ℓ = 1, else min(1, U_ℓ/(1 − r_ℓ)).

    This is the database analogue of the de novo precision estimator with c_l = 1: a peptide with an exact origin always matches, one without matches at the decoy rate. Every peptide of P carries its class's π̂ into ADR-0010's FDR_0(e) = (W_0(e) + c)/T_0(e), with c = max π̂_ℓ over the classes with n_ℓ > 0. A peptide outside every class weighs 1 and does not enter c; an empty class has no weight. Edge cases: r = 0 ges π̂ = U_ℓ, and m < r (fewer exact target than exact decoy matches) clips to 1. m ≥ 1 is unchanged (π̂ = 1). Why per class and not pooled: the no-origin share correlates with the chance-hit rate, which changes by ≈ ×14 per residue of length; an oracle that knows the true pooled no-origin share still fails the short-peptide and padding scenarios (2.19×, 2.02×).

    Evidence (failing cells of 18 list-level scenarios, δ × π × q; ADR-0019 Evidence): mean PEP fails 6–14 cells per scenario, a pooled exact share 0–13, the per-class rule 0 everywhere, including PEP deflated 10× (the run collapse) and the length-correlated inputs. Power at δ = 0 relative to mean PEP is 0.984–1.0 at N = 4,000 and 0.85–0.97 at N = 300 (π̂ = 1 instead gives 0.43–0.84 and 0.25). At genome scale the estimated/true false count moves from 0.03–0.46 under mean PEP to 0.96–2.4 under this rule; acceptance at q = 0.01 is unchanged, small or binding lists lose up to ~15 % power.

    Limitation and the run warning. If the upstream pipeline kept only genome-matching peptides, k is ~0 by construction and no genome-based rule can be calibrated — mean PEP included (FDP 0.061 at q = 0.01, δ = 0.2). placement therefore raises the run warning input_prefiltered when k falls below the 0.001 quantile of Binomial(n, mean PEP × (1 − r)) over P pooled; this is the one place the row-level mean PEP is still read, and it is read directly; the old pi_wrong_database helper (whose max-q and 1.0 fallbacks would misfire here) was deleted with M3-25 decision 11. The detector inherits PEP's weakness in one direction: a deflated PEP (the §5.3 min over runs, defect 2 above, or an engine that under-reports) lowers the expected count n × mean PEP × (1 − r) towards the realised k, so the test loses power and a genuinely pre-filtered input can pass unwarned. It never fires spuriously for that reason — too small an expectation only hides the warning — and the π̂ it guards does not read PEP at all. An inflated PEP is the conservative direction.

    Reporting. mapping_fdr.tsv columns are unchanged, with pi_wrong = (W + c)/(D + 1) now reflecting the class π̂. Read the per-class π̂ from the class table, not from mapping_fdr: a class with no decoy hit has W = 0 and D = 0, so its pi_wrong row is c, the largest class estimate, not that class's π̂_ℓ. The class table is the placement step's stratum0_classes output (mapping/stratum0_classes.parquet, columns length_class, n, k, r, upper, pi_wrong), an optional input of context_stats, which turns it into detection_summary.tsv QC rows stratum0_unmapped_share = k/n (the §7.5.1 genome-inconsistent rate of this population), one for the run and one per length class, each per-class row carrying that class's upper as ci_high. It sits beside genome_inconsistent_rate, which measures another population (C9 other, non-contaminant, over accepted exact placements) and is unchanged.

    Annotated-stop read-through (M7-16a, ADR-0058)¶

    Code: src/giap/mapping/readthrough.py (sites, cells, mirrored cells), tolerant_dp._verify_core (the consumable-stop case), tolerant.Search(marked), frameshift._FSearch(marked) and nonfs_overlap, steps.mapping.sixframe_index (index format 2, event mapping.stop_sites). Spec: docs/specs/M7-16-stop-codon-readthrough.md; review: statistician, 2026-10-24 (local/stat/m7-16-review.md).

    1. The rule. Route 3 (tolerant) may consume a target * only at the annotated stop codon of a CDS or sORF: the last codon of the feature, on its strand, in its frame. Pseudogenes are excluded, and a feature whose last codon is not a table-11 stop (for example a partial gene at a contig edge) is skipped and counted (not_a_stop). The codon is marked at every copy in the origin-extended frames of a circular replicon. The only move over a marked cell is a substitution of one query residue (any letter, U and X included) at cost 1, recorded as substitution; no match, swap, block or near-block reads it, and every other * and every X stays unconsumable. The exact route and the frameshift sides never consume a stop; the frameshift precedence check does see read-through alignments. Peptides shorter than 10 aa never reach route 3 (mapping.tolerant.min_len), so a stop-spanning peptide of 7–9 aa is never placed; at 10–11 aa (k = 1) the stop is the only allowed difference.

    2. Counts are exactly symmetric. Each decoy frame carries the target frame's number of marked cells: reverse maps cell p of an n-residue frame to n − 1 − p, shuffle keeps p (stops keep their positions). Every mirrored cell holds *, so the stop-free run lengths on each side are the same, origin copies included. mirror_cells raises if a mirrored cell is not a stop, draws no randomness, and the decoy frames are byte-identical to index format 1 for the same seed.

    3. Context mirroring and the measured ratios. Under any i.i.d. peptide law the reverse decoy is exact (read-through D_rt/T_rt = 1 to 1e-16). The local context is mirrored, not kept: gene | * | 3′ flank becomes reversed flank | * | reversed gene. Under a law that is not reversal-symmetric (tryptic, C-terminal K/R) the per-genome read-through D_rt/T_rt was 0.52–1.89 (reverse) and 0.58–1.74 (shuffle) on three giapsim 1-Mb genomes, and 0.81–1.15 (reverse) and 1.14–1.42 (shuffle) on Nissle. The net effect on the total, (D_rt − T_rt)/T_regular, was at worst −9.4 × 10⁻⁴ (giapsim) and −1.3 × 10⁻⁴ (Nissle): exact expected chance mass, Slurm jobs 1889319 (Nissle) and 1889320 (giapsim), script local/stat/m7-16/rt_mass.py. Job 1889319 used a local Bakta run with all four replicons linear (4,686 sites).     4. The added mass is an approximation, not a bound. A window consumes a marked cell only if it contains it, with the other L − 1 residues within m − 1 differences. Relative to E(L, m) of §7.2.5 the added chance mass is approximately G/(N(1 − s)) at m = 1 and 2G/(N(1 − s)) at m = 2 (G consumable cells, N six-frame positions, s the residue-match probability), independent of L and scaling with the gene density G/N. Measured realised shares of the target chance mass: 0.04–0.08 % on Nissle and 0.05–0.20 % on giapsim 1 Mb (i.i.d. and tryptic laws; the top of the range is tryptic at m = 2). On another genome the share is approximately, not at most, the formula's value for its G/N.

    5. The read-through ratio is not a subset FDR. D_rt/T_rt does not estimate the false discovery rate of the read-through placements. The list FDR (per decoy type and stratum) does not control that subset, and no output, table or report may present the ratio as one (R3).

    6. Expected false read-through placements per run are about α · T_acc(m ≥ 1) · ρ, where T_acc(m ≥ 1) is the number of accepted target placements in strata 1–2 and ρ ≈ 10⁻³ is the read-through share of the chance mass. That is ≪ 1 for any realistic run, but it is not a per-site guarantee.

By Appendix B.6 a stratum-1 list with fewer than about 100 targets ahead of its first decoy accepts none, so cterm_extension has low power on small inputs by design.     16b reporting: the readthrough flag and the placement-kind counts¶

    A peptide_hits.tsv row whose accepted alignment consumes an annotated terminal stop cell carries the flag readthrough (and mappings.gff3 the attribute readthrough_codon), computed from the sites the index was searched with. detection_summary.tsv counts distinct peptides by placement kind, tolerant diff type, placement flag and unmapped reason (section qc, metric n_peptides). These are counts, not rates or FDRs: n is the population the count comes from, never a denominator to report. The flagged rows are a subset of a list accepted at q, and the list FDR does not control that subset (items 5–6); no output describes the readthrough rows as accepted at q on their own.
