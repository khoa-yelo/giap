    Free entrapment: the FDP of A1's identifications¶

    Implements validation/pilot/entrapment.py (GIAP_SPEC.md §15.5 metric 8, §8.7; issue M10-07, docs/specs/M10-07-free-entrapment.md with its binding statistician decisions). Validation only: metric 8 gates nothing (§15.6) and is in no §8.1 testing family.

    Source¶

    Wen B, Freestone J, Riffle M, MacCoss MJ, Noble WS, Keich U. "Assessment of false discovery rate control in tandem mass spectrometry analysis using entrapment." Nature Methods 22 (2025), doi:10.1038/s41592-025-02719-x (open access, CC BY 4.0). N_T and N_E are the numbers of original target and entrapment discoveries, and r is "the effective ratio of the entrapment to original target database size".

    • Equation (1), the combined method. FDP̂(T ∪ E) = N_E (1 + 1/r) / (N_T + N_E). Under an assumption analogous to the equal-chance assumption of target–decoy competition, the paper proves (Supplementary Note 2.2) that it is "an estimated upper bound, that is, on average it overestimates the true FDP". This is fdp_combined, §15.5's formula unchanged.
    • Equation (2), the lower bound. N_E / (N_T + N_E). If every entrapment discovery is false, it is a lower bound on the FDP (Supplementary Note 2.1). This is fdp_lower.
    • Why the sample method (3) is not used. N_E (1/r) / N_T estimates the FDP among the original targets only; the paper calls it "inherently flawed", so it is not computed.
    • The paired method needs one shuffled partner per target peptide (r = 1). A free entrapment has no such pairs, so it does not apply.

    The statement about (1) is about expectation: a single estimate can fall below the realised FDP. The report therefore says "estimated upper bound", never "upper bound".

    Mapping onto the pilot¶

    A1 searched UniProt K-12 plus contaminants. Against Nissle spectra its peptides split into the original targets T, the K-12 peptides present in Nissle's translation (shared), and the entrapment E, the K-12-only peptides (k12_only). The entrapment is free: it is already in A1's database, and DIA-NN competed and filtered T and E together without knowing which is which. (It does use protein information in its precursor q-values, which breaks the symmetry in a known direction; see Bias.) So the combined method's quantity is the FDP of A1's accepted set after removing contaminant-sequence and not_in_universe discoveries. r is computed in silico: r = |k12_only| / |shared|.

    The universe and "present"¶

    • Digest. D(F) = the distinct trypsin_p products of 7–30 aa with ≤ 1 missed cleavage (initiator M kept, giap.mapping.digest), I → L, with every product holding a residue outside the 20 standard letters dropped (non_standard). The universe is D(K-12) \ D(contaminants): a peptide a contaminant also yields could be a true contaminant identification, so it is removed on both sides.
    • Present. One function, applied unchanged to the in-silico universe and to the discoveries, with one index. Rule 1 (exact): an exact match in Nissle's six-frame translation (match_exact; I → L, both strands, the origin overlap of circular replicons), or (strain_db) a substring of a Bakta protein after I → L, which covers N-terminal products of non-ATG starts. Rule 2 (mass_equivalent): a tolerant target row (match_tolerant, GIAP's mapping.tolerant defaults, ≥ 10 aa) whose diff_types is non-empty and only block, the exact-composition exchanges of §7.2.4. near_block, swap, substitution and frameshift rows do not make a peptide present. Using one function on both sides keeps every asymmetry of the rules (for example that 7–9-aa peptides can only be present through rule 1) the same in r and in N_T, N_E.
    • Distinctness. I/L-collapsed on both sides and at both levels. Two K-12 library entries that differ only by I/L give one collapsed element two chances of a false match; this is rare.

    The searchable space¶

    Precursors carry carbamidomethyl on every C and 0 or 1 oxidised M (each oxidised position its own form), at charge 1–4 with 300 ≤ m/z ≤ 1800, m/z = (M + z · 1.007276) / z and M the residue masses plus 18.010565. At peptide level these bounds remove nothing: a 7–30-aa peptide weighs between 7 G + H₂O and 30 W + H₂O, carbamidomethyl-C and oxidised M lie inside the G–W residue range, and charges 1 and 4 together already cover that whole mass range. The bounds are still applied (they are parameters), and at precursor level they decide how many charge states a peptide has. r_prec = Σ_{k12_only} n_prec / Σ_shared n_prec is taken from these configured bounds, not from DIA-NN's library.

    Levels and the assessment rule¶

    q_value is DIA-NN's experiment-wide precursor Global.Q.Value, so the set of distinct accepted precursors (norm(modified_peptide), charge), pooled over the wild-type runs, is the only unit whose FDR the search claims to control at α = 0.01.

    What DIA-NN 2.3.1 has already filtered. The DIA-NN documentation for 2.3.1 (README at 2fb4df2, "GUI settings reference" and "Main output reference") says that the precursor FDR setting (--qvalue) is "the precursor q-value filtering to be applied to the main output report", and, of the recommended main-report filters, only "Q.Value at 0.01" is "done by DIA-NN automatically"; the global, library and protein-group q-values (Global.Q.Value, Lib.Q.Value, PG.Q.Value, Global.PG.Q.Value, Lib.PG.Q.Value) are listed as filters for the user to apply, because "the main report is designed to be filtered". The matrices, which the pilot does not use, are filtered at 1 % with global protein-group q-values. A1 sets --qvalue 0.01 and no flag that changes these defaults (validation/pilot/diann_A1.cfg). The accepted set is therefore the precursors with run-level Q.Value ≤ 0.01 (DIA-NN) and Global.Q.Value ≤ 0.01 (GIAP, Q_ACCEPT). No FDR claim attaches to that intersection itself: a subset of an FDR-controlled list is not FDR-controlled in general. The run-level filter removes only rows that are low-scoring in their run, so in practice it lowers the false share, and the pooled set stays the closest unit to one with a stated FDR. This is why α is the reference there and only there. No protein-level filter is applied by either. A1 also runs without --no-refine-q, so the precursor q-values use protein information (see Bias and the next paragraph).

    The assessment is issued only at precursor level:

    • evidence_of_failure if fdp_lower > α (checked first);
    • consistent_with_control if fdp_combined ≤ α (inclusive);
    • inconclusive otherwise; not_evaluable when there are no discoveries or no shared peptides.

    Both comparisons are exact rationals (fractions.Fraction, α = 1/100): fdp_combined is computed as N_E (k + s) / (k (N_T + N_E)) with k = |k12_only| and s = |shared|, which equals equation (1) because 1 + 1/r = (k + s)/k. These are the paper's three readings (Fig. 1b).

    What the labels do and do not say. The labels are readings of point estimates, not tests, and carry no error rate:

    • consistent_with_control. It does not mean that the FDP is at most α at any stated confidence. In the attack's equal-chance scenario A, with a mean true FDP of 1.6 % (above α), about 15 % of lists read consistent_with_control, because fdp_combined is noisy. In scenario F, a search whose true FDR is about 0.5 α, about 11 % of lists read inconclusive. ucb95 ≤ α is the stronger reading, and holds only under its model.
    • The A1 settings. The upper-bound property behind this label needs equal chance. DIA-NN's documentation calls --no-refine-q "essential" for validating its precursor q-values with classic entrapment, and A1 runs without it. So for A1, consistent_with_control has less support than the paper's reading.
    • evidence_of_failure. It needs only that every entrapment discovery is false. It is therefore unaffected by equal-chance violations, but not by the genome and contamination caveats.

    The peptide level is §15.5's number but is not assessed (not_assessed): DIA-NN claims no FDR for the distinct peptides derived from its precursor list, and false precursors spread over distinct peptides while true ones cluster, so a peptide-level FDP above α is not evidence that the search failed. Per-run blocks are descriptive and not assessed either, because the rows were selected by the global q-value together with the run-level one, and no FDR claim attaches to a run's intersection. Estimates are reported unclipped and flagged above_one when equation (1) exceeds 1, which small counts allow.

    Uncertainty¶

    • fdp_lower is a realised bound, not an estimate. If every entrapment discovery is false, the realised FDP of this list is at least fdp_lower, with no sampling error. The condition is that the genome is complete and is the sample's strain, and that no K-12 material is in the sample. The report calls it a realised lower bound, conditional on these caveats.
    • ucb95, a one-sided 95 % upper confidence bound for the FDP. Model: the equal chance assumption in its plainest form, with independent false matches. Given M false discoveries among N = N_T + N_E, N_E ~ Binomial(M, π) with π = k/(k + s) = r/(1 + r); then N_E/π estimates M without bias, and fdp_combined = N_E/(π N). The bound inverts the exact one-sided test: M_max = max{M ∈ {N_E, …, N} : BinomCDF(N_E; M, π) > 0.05} and ucb95 = M_max / N. It bounds the realised FDP M/N, which lies on the grid {0, 1/N, …, 1}, so it is at most 1 and can sit below an unclipped fdp_combined (above one, or π close to 1); it is never below min(fdp_combined, 1) by a full grid step 1/N. It is not valid under clustered (protein-level) errors, and no two-sided interval is given. At precursor level π comes from the precursor sums.
    • A protein-block bootstrap was rejected: the discoveries are one realised list, and the resampling unit for false matches is not the protein of the discovered peptides.

    Bias¶

    Source Direction
    Near-homolog entrapment: every K-12-only peptide near a Nissle variant has its neighbour's signal in the sample, so neighbour-driven errors concentrate on E (§15.5 "errs high") high
    7–9-aa variant peptides are not tolerantly matched, so they stay in E high
    The tolerant route reports only minimal-cost loci; a block-only locus behind a cheaper non-block locus is not seen high, negligible
    An incomplete genome, a different strain, or K-12 material in the sample (carryover, lab contamination) makes some K-12-only discoveries true; the lower bound then stops being a bound high
    Six-frame chance hits move short K-12-only peptides into shared lower N_E (a more conservative lower bound); negligible for equation (1)
    E and T differ in length and charge composition, so equal chance per precursor mis-weights the peptide-level r (sensitivity.r_prec_weighted) either
    False matches attracted to T, e.g. interference from abundant co-eluting shared proteins (equal-chance violation in the low direction) low, unquantified
    A protein-level filter (PG.Q.Value) would remove K-12-only proteins first; neither DIA-NN's main report nor GIAP applies one (DIA-NN 2.3.1 docs, above), and adding one is a lead finding low
    DIA-NN's precursor q-values use protein information unless --no-refine-q is set, which the docs call "essential if the goal is validation of DIA-NN's precursor-level q-values using classic entrapment"; A1 does not set it. False matches are favoured on proteins with many true identifications. In A1's database those are mostly K-12 proteins with an expressed Nissle ortholog, whose peptides are mostly shared and whose few K-12-only peptides are the variant peptides. K-12-specific proteins, almost all E, and unexpressed proteins are penalised. The E share among favoured elements is below π, so false matches go to T more often than equal chance assumes (the attack's scenario D shows this direction). This is an equal-chance violation; fdp_lower is unaffected low, unquantified
    K-12-derived constructs in engineered strains excluded: only wild-type runs are accepted

    Sensitivities, pre-registered and descriptive (no assessment): present_broad widens rule 2 to rows of block, swap and near_block only; r_prec_weighted uses r_prec in equation (1); far restricts E to K-12-only peptides of ≥ 10 aa with no tolerant target row of any type, and T to shared peptides of ≥ 10 aa, and reports the split of k12_only and N_E into lt10, near_homolog and far.

    Simulation evidence¶

    tests/calibration/attacks/test_attack_free_entrapment.py (statistician; seed seeds.calibration_base + 707) simulates a protein-grouped universe with r = 0.25 and checks: the mean of fdp_combined is at least the mean true FDP under equal chance (edge and uniform), and with whole-protein clustered errors; the excess grows with homolog attraction λ ∈ {1, 2, 4}; attraction to T biases low; fdp_lower never exceeds the true FDP; no list with true FDP ≤ α is labelled evidence_of_failure; and ucb95 covers at ≥ 95 % under equal chance. Two teeth checks show the tests can fail: fdp_lower used as an estimate fails the mean check, and a median bound fails the coverage check.

    Recorded, not gated (R = 2,000):

    • ucb95 coverage. About 0.72 under whole-protein clustered errors (E) and about 0.75 under attraction to T (D). This is why its assumes string excludes them.
    • Labels in scenario A (mean true FDP 1.6 %): 15 % of lists read consistent_with_control.
    • Labels in scenario F (true FDR about 0.5 α): 89 % read consistent_with_control, 11 % inconclusive, and none evidence_of_failure.
