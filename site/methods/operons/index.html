    Operons: the within-operon distribution and the missed-operon decoy FDR¶

    Issue M6-01 (GIAP_SPEC.md §7.4.2–§7.4.4, §8.1–§8.3), modules giap.stats.tdist and giap.stats.decoy_fdr, with the lead decisions of docs/specs/M6-01-lead-decisions.md. The pair table is M6-04, the discordance tests M6-05, the missed-operon step M6-06 and the operon-level calibration gate M6-09. This note covers the engine and the discordance step (M6-05); the other step sections come later.

    The within-operon distribution¶

    Model. Δ = log2 abundance(downstream) − log2 abundance(upstream) over OP pairs whose two genes are both detected (imputed abundances are excluded by the caller) is modelled as a Student-t with free location μ, scale σ and degrees of freedom ν ∈ [2, 50] (fit_student_t, default df_bounds = (2, 50)). μ is free because operon polarity makes the downstream gene systematically lower; a heavy tail is allowed because operon partners legitimately differ 1–10-fold (§7.4).

    Fit. Maximum likelihood, Nelder–Mead on (μ, log σ, ν) from μ₀ = median, σ₀ = 1.4826 · MAD (floored at 10⁻⁶), ν₀ = 8, maxiter 2,000. The ν bound is passed to scipy's bounded Nelder–Mead rather than through the logit reparameterisation the issue text names; the two have the same optimum. A simplex pressed against a bound stops just short of it (ν = 49.9997), so after the fit both bounds are scored at the fitted (μ, σ) and the best of the three ν is kept (_snap_to_bound). That step can only lower the negative log-likelihood, so it cannot move the fit away from the optimum; a ν on a bound is a legitimate optimum and is returned with converged = True. converged is otherwise scipy's own flag (False when the optimiser stops on iterations); M6-05 marks the tests unestimated when it is False.

    Two-sided p. p = 2 · min(F, 1 − F), F = F_t((Δ − μ)/σ; ν) (student_t_two_sided): exactly 1 at Δ = μ, asymmetric about 0 whenever μ ≠ 0, NaN in → NaN out, all-NaN under an unestimated fit.

    Abundance-dependent scale (M6-13, ADR-0055 option (a)). Proteomics noise grows at low abundance. One σ for every OP pair is therefore too narrow for low-abundance pairs and too wide for high-abundance ones. Attack D-1 measured the consequence through the step: the pooled level failed at α = 0.05 and 0.10, and the lowest-abundance quartile ran at about 3× nominal (Slurm 1884493). The scale is therefore modelled as σ_i = σ₀ · exp(γ z_i), where z_i is the pair's mean log2 abundance, standardised over the fit pairs (fit_student_t_scaled). - μ, σ₀, γ and ν are fitted jointly by maximum likelihood, by Nelder–Mead from the constant fit, with ν ∈ [2, 50] and γ ∈ [−2, 2]. - γ = 0 is the constant model above, and the p of a pair is 2 · sf_t(|Δ − μ̂| / σ̂_i; ν̂) (scaled_t_two_sided). It applies from the same 20 usable pairs as the constant model. At n = 20–50 it was measured at level, and more conservative than the constant fit (statistician, M6-13 S-5). - The fitted σ̂_i does not depend on how z is centred or scaled. γ is reported per SD of pair-mean log2 abundance, and per log2 unit.

    Tested pairs outside the fit's abundance range (imputed pairs) are evaluated at the nearest end of that range, not extrapolated.

    The constant model is used instead, and the run log says why, when: - the pair means vary by less than 0.1 log2 (SD). This is a relevance guard, not an identifiability one: over so narrow a range no noise curve changes σ appreciably; - the four-parameter fit fails to converge, collapses, or fits worse than the constant one.

    There is no model-selection step: when the four-parameter fit is available it is used. The likelihood ratio against γ = 0 is reported, not acted on.

    γ̂ describes the spread of Δ among pairs whose two genes were both detected, given their observed mean abundance. It is not the instrument's noise curve, for two reasons. - Detection truncation can narrow the spread at low abundance (in the simulator the effect is not measurable: γ̂ is 0.001 under the null). - m carries the same measurement noise as Δ, which attenuates γ̂.

    The conditional spread is the right null for the test, which is conditional on detection.

    What the level claim covers. The claim is the pooled per-pair level. m = a + Δ/2 contains the response, so even under homoscedastic noise the pairs in the outer quartiles of m are enriched for large |Δ − μ| (1.05–1.17× nominal at α = 0.01, for the constant and the log-linear model alike). Within quartiles of an abundance that is independent of Δ (the upstream gene's), the null is at level.

    The one-detected P(miss) still averages over the raw within-operon ratios. Under abundance-dependent noise that sample is too narrow for a low-abundance partner (open, M6-13 Q1).

    The log-linear form is an assumption. Attack D-1b checks a step and a variance floor, and D-1c checks per-gene measurement noise.

    The claim covers the pooled level under smooth, monotone abundance-dependent noise. - Under the variance floor and under per-gene measurement noise, the top-abundance quartile still runs at up to about 1.7× nominal at α = 0.01. The constant model runs at about 3×, in the lowest quartile. - A discontinuous σ (D-1b-step) fails even the pooled level. No smooth σ(z) removes that, and it is outside the claim (ADR-0055 Amendment 1, accepted by Khoa 2026-10-24). In the full tier it is a strict xfail, confirmed in both blocks at α = 0.05 and 0.10 (above).

    What the p means. M6-05 fits the t on the same OP pairs it then tests, so its p is a plug-in p: the parameters absorb part of each pair's own deviation. With a fit on 400 draws the plug-in rejection rate is 0.0102 / 0.0506 / 0.1007 at α = 0.01 / 0.05 / 0.10 (R = 1,000, recorded in local/logs/calibration/discordance_plugin.jsonl), so the plug-in effect is negligible at that size; it grows as the number of OP pairs falls and is gated at the operon level in M6-09. The fit is the null model for OP pairs only: it assumes the OP pairs are exchangeable draws from one t, so a genome whose OP pairs mix two very different populations (e.g. highly polar long operons and short ones) violates it and the BH family of M6-05 inherits that.

    Degenerate cases. Fewer than 5 finite values → StudentT(nan, nan, nan, n, False). When more than ν_min/(ν_min + 1) = 2/3 of the values are tied (all equal, or a dominant exact tie, which quantised abundances can produce), the likelihood is unbounded as σ → 0. The issue requires converged = False there. The review of PR #140 found the fit returning σ ≈ 10⁻³⁰⁵ with converged = True (50 equal values; 40 tied of 50), which would have given p = 0 for every untied OP pair. Fixed in 7ad17fe (M6-01 decision 13): a scale below 10⁻⁸ · max(1, range) is a collapse and the fit is returned unestimated, so M6-05 tests nothing.

    Not yet provided (pass 2). A CI for (μ, σ, ν) (profile likelihood), so the reported fit carries its uncertainty. The per-pair effect M6-05 reports is described under Discordance below.

    Discordance (M6-05, M6-05H)¶

    Module giap.operons.discordance (GIAP_SPEC.md §7.4.3, §8.1 row "Operon discordance"; lead decisions in docs/specs/M6-05-discordance.md and M6-index decision 1).

    Test. The t of the previous section, with the abundance-dependent scale (M6-13; sigma_model = "loglinear", the default; "constant" is the M6-05 null), is fitted to Δ and the pair-mean log2 abundance m over the usable OP pairs: both genes eligible (not partial), both detected, both log2 abundances finite, neither imputed. Fewer than 20 usable pairs, or a constant fit with converged = False, leaves every OP pair test = none with too_few_op_pairs and unestimated, and no family is formed. A failed four-parameter fit is not that case: it falls back to the constant null with a WARNING. Otherwise every eligible both-detected OP pair with a finite Δ is tested: p = 2 · min(F, 1 − F), F = F_t((Δ − μ̂) / σ̂_i; ν̂). Pairs whose abundance is imputed are tested but not fitted (spec §7.4.2, example 5); their z is clamped to the fit range. The run-log event operons.discordance_fit records the null in force (γ̂ per SD and per log2, the fallback reason, the LR). The fit and the test use the same pairs, so p is a plug-in p (below).

    Effect and CI. effect = Δ − μ̂ in log2 units: how far the pair sits from the typical within-operon ratio. No CI is reported for it (ci_low, ci_high NA): a single pair has no sampling replicate, and the t prediction interval is a statement about the null, not about the effect (M6-05 lead decision 2). The claim GIAP makes is "discordant at q ≤ 0.1", and that claim carries its q.

    One detected (M6-05H). When the operons step has C11 peptide hits and M6-02's model in force is usable, an OP pair with exactly one seen gene (at least k_min of its observable peptides assigned in C11, M6-02 lead decision 9) and no both-detected t test is one_detected_pmiss. The unseen gene u may still carry a detected call or an imputed value; the event is about peptides seen, which is what the detection-limit model conditions on. stat = the seen partner v's log2 abundance a_v, p_value = P(miss) of u under the out-of-fold model of v's TU (v's abundance enters the score, so v must not have trained it; the full model when v has no TU), averaged over the oriented ratios r of the fit pairs (Δ when u is downstream, −Δ when upstream), method = detection_limit, null_model = p_detect_model. A gene with no observable peptide (P(miss) NaN), no peptide hits or an unusable model leaves the pair none with no_detection_limit_model; without a usable model the event falls back to detected_a xor detected_b. A row whose partner has no abundance is none with no flag. A replicate model fitted with detectability.covariates cannot be scored until the step joins the M4-10 covariates, so its one-detected rows are none with pmiss_covariates_missing and the step logs a WARNING.

    Family. One BH family per run: every OP pair with test = both_detected_t. M6-index decision 1 keeps P(miss) out of the family: a one_detected_pmiss row has q_value NA and no extra flag. That departs from §7.4.3's "both kinds together", which is ADR-0056 (accepted by Khoa, 2026-10-24). P(miss) is not uniform under any null, so BH over the mixture has no justification.

    Relative scale. With abundance_scale = relative (MaxLFQ, summed intensity, unknown; §7.3.4) Δ is not a ratio of copy numbers, because each protein's normalisation constant enters it. A pair that is "unusual" on that scale is not a stoichiometry statement. Every abundance test is therefore none with relative_scale and unestimated, as the spec requires. This is the right behaviour, not merely a conservative one.

The small-p tail is the one BH uses. The in-sample point pulls the fit toward itself, and that cancels the downward bias of the MLE scale at small n. The held-out p is the anti-conservative one: 0.029 at α = 0.01 for n = 20. So leave-one-out would make the level worse, not better, and it is not recommended.
    • True discordant pairs in the fit make the null pairs conservative. They lower ν̂ toward the bound 2 and widen the tails, so contamination never inflates the level.
    • Family-wise under the complete null. The share of replicates with any BH call at q ≤ 0.1 is 0.0015 at n = 20, 0.0065 at n = 50 and 0.0725 at n = 500 (ν = 5); the bound is 0.1 + 2 SE.
    • TU-chained Δ. Consecutive pairs of one TU share a gene, so their Δ are negatively correlated. With TUs of 2–6 genes the in-sample rates are 0.0103 / 0.0495 / 0.0993 at n = 500 and the any-call share is 0.0725. That passes.
    • Abundance-dependent noise (Δ SD × exp(−0.5 · z_abundance)) passes the pooled criterion (0.0070 / 0.0522 / 0.1076 at n = 500) but not within the lowest-abundance quartile (0.024 / 0.152 / 0.271), at 2.4–3× nominal. One t for all pairs puts null calls on noisy, low-abundance pairs. This is attack D-1 for M6-09 (below).
    • Power is close to zero at realistic effects. With ν free, the fit absorbs discordant pairs into a heavy tail. BH power at q ≤ 0.1 by n and π₁:
    • s = 2 log2 (4-fold): 0.000–0.003 at every n and every π₁ ∈ {0.05, 0.10, 0.20}.
    • s = 4 log2 (16-fold, 8 null scale units), π₁ = 0.10: 0.12 at n = 20, 0.005 at n = 50, 0.000 at n = 500.
    • s = 4 log2, π₁ = 0.01 (5 of 500 pairs): still 0.000, because ν̂ falls to 3.3.

    Power falls as n grows, because BH needs ever smaller p while ν̂ stays near the bound. This is a property of the specified method (§7.4.2 fits ν by ML on all OP pairs, discordant ones included), not of the code.

    What M6-05 can claim. Discordance calls control the per-pair level and the family-wise false call rate on null OP pairs. Their FDR is controlled at q ≤ 0.1, conservatively. The module will rarely make a call: a near-empty operon_flags.tsv is the expected output, not evidence of concordance. Like the missed-operon family (M6-01 decision 12), M6-09 gates level and FDR and claims no power. A method change that would restore power needs an ADR draft for Khoa. Options include: ν fixed (e.g. 4) or bounded below at 3–4; a robust or trimmed fit of the bulk; a two-component null+outlier mixture; σ modelled as a function of mean abundance, which also answers attack D-1.

    Minimum of 20 usable pairs. At n = 20, ν is not identified: ν̂ sits on a bound in 56 % (ν = 5) and 72 % (ν = 30) of replicates. The p is still at level, for the cancellation reason above, and is conservative in the BH tail (rate 0.0017 at α = 0.01). At n = 20 a single BH call needs p ≤ 0.005. Sizes below 20 were not measured. So 20 is defensible as a floor for level. It is not a floor for useful power, and that is stated rather than gated.

    Attacks owed to M6-09 (tests/calibration/attacks/, §8.5):

    • D-1. Δ variance that depends on abundance, with level checked per abundance quartile as well as pooled. The pooled check passes; the per-quartile check fails, as measured above. A failing attack blocks the gate, so M6-09 needs either the ADR fix or an ADR that scopes the claim to the pooled level.
    • D-2. TU-chained Δ (shared genes), level and any-call share. Passes as measured above.
    • D-3. Contamination masking: report power at π₁ ∈ {0.01, 0.05, 0.10} and s ∈ {2, 4}. This is a power record only; it is not gated.

    Decoy FDR for missed operons¶

    Targets and decoys. Targets are NO pairs (same strand, UniOP p below threshold) with both genes detected, scored s = −|Δ| (higher = more co-abundant). Decoys are CV (convergent) and SW (sandwich, → ← →) pairs with both genes detected, which cannot share a transcript. Each pair carries a distance bin b from bin_index (half-open, operons.distance_bins_bp, default [−∞, 0, 50, 150, 300, ∞) → 5 bins; NaN distance → excluded everywhere).

    Estimator (reweighted_fdr):

    FDR(t) = π₀ · Σ_b N_NO,b · (D_b(t) + 1)/(N_decoy,b + 1) / T(t)

    with N_NO,b all targets in bin b, D_b(t) the decoys of bin b with s ≥ t and T(t) the targets with s ≥ t. (D_b + 1)/(N_decoy,b + 1) is the per-bin null tail probability with the +1 correction, and N_NO,b times it is the expected number of null targets of bin b above t if every target were null; the sum is therefore a π₀ = 1 (Storey-type) decoy estimate of the false count, reweighted to the targets' distance mix. A bin with targets and no decoys contributes N_NO,b, the maximally conservative value. π₀ is operons.pi0 = 1 (π̂₀ estimation is pass-2 work, needs an ADR).

    Which decoy set. The FDR is computed with each set and the element-wise maximum is taken first, then made monotone with giap.stats.tda.monotone_q (q at a score = the smallest FDR over thresholds loose enough to accept it), then clipped to [0, 1] (max_decoy_fdr). Maximising first gives q ≥ each set's own monotone q, so q is at least as conservative as the more conservative set at every threshold; the reverse order can report a q below one of the two decoy FDRs (lead decision 1). which names the set that gave the larger raw FDR (first key on a tie, which is forced at the loosest threshold where every set gives π₀); it is "" for an excluded target. A set with no usable decoy at all is skipped; if none survives, q is NaN.

    Exchangeability assumption. Within a distance bin, |Δ| of a null NO pair and |Δ| of a decoy pair have the same distribution. Distance is binned because co-abundance falls with distance (shared local chromatin/supercoiling effects, read-through) and CV/SW distances differ from NO distances (an SW distance is the sum of two gaps). The assumption is untestable on one genome; CV pairs can look less alike than same-strand neighbours (transcriptional interference), which is conservative and is why the larger FDR is reported; anything that makes decoys look more alike than null NO pairs (e.g. an abundance or detection mix that differs between the classes when the noise of Δ depends on abundance) is anti-conservative and is an M6-09 attack.

    Local fdr (local_fdr, DecoyFdr.lfdr), for ranking only, never for calls: lfdr(s) = min(1, π₀ f₀(|Δ|)/f_NO(|Δ|)), f₀ the Gaussian KDE of the decoy |Δ| with per-decoy weights N_NO,b/N_decoy,b (normalised), f_NO the unweighted KDE of the target |Δ|, both reflected at 0, each with scipy's gaussian_kde(bw_method="silverman") bandwidth, h = (3n/4)^(−1/5) · sd, computed from its own unweighted sample (so the weights move the density, not the smoothing). Over decoy sets the larger lfdr wins (np.fmax, so a set whose KDE is undefined does not mask a defined one); the set giving the larger lfdr need not be the one in which. Fewer than 5 values in either sample → NaN.

    Numerical evaluation. The KDE sums are evaluated by linear binning on an h/40 grid, one convolution with the kernel truncated at 10 h, and linear interpolation. Measured against the exact reflected kernel sum (statistician review, n = 50 and 2,000, half-normal, half-t₂ and an outlier at 40): maximum relative error 1.1 × 10⁻³ – 2.9 × 10⁻³ where the density exceeds 10⁻⁶ of its maximum, ∫₀^∞ f = 1.000000, slope at 0 ≈ 0 (the reflection is correct). The KDE's own sampling error is O(n^(−2/5)), i.e. several per cent even at n = 2,000, so the approximation is immaterial for an lfdr used as a ranking. Beyond 10 h from every decoy, f₀ is exactly 0 and lfdr = 0, where the exact KDE gives a value of order e^(−50); also immaterial.

    Known asymmetry. A bin with targets but no decoys contributes the conservative N_NO,b to the FDR but weight 0 to f₀, whose mass is then spread over the other bins; if that bin's null is narrower than the others, its targets get a too-small lfdr. Harmless while lfdr only ranks; must be fixed (lfdr NaN, or 1, for those targets) before lfdr is ever thresholded.

    Power: what this family can and cannot find¶

    Measured by the implementer (PR #140) and re-derived and re-measured independently in the statistician review. Conclusion: at q ≤ 0.1 this family has no power for co-abundance effects in the realistic range. The cause is the score, not the +1, the bins or π₀. The calls it makes are correct (FDR control holds); it rarely makes any.

    The identifiability bound. Let the target |Δ| have density π₀ f₀ + (1 − π₀) f₁ (f₁ = missed operons) and the decoys f₀. In the large-sample limit FDR(τ) at the threshold |Δ| ≤ τ is π₀,used · F₀(τ)/F(τ). When f₁/f₀ decreases in |Δ| (true when both are centred scale families and f₁ is the narrower), F₀/F increases in τ, so its infimum is the limit τ → 0:

    min over τ of FDR = π₀,used / (π₀ + (1 − π₀) · L),    L = f₁(0)/f₀(0)   (= σ₀/σ₁ for normals)

    and the true FDP of even the very best-scoring calls is at least π₀ / (π₀ + (1 − π₀) L). A call at q ≤ α is possible only if L ≥ (π₀,used/α − π₀)/(1 − π₀); with π₀,used = 1 and α = 0.1 that is L ≥ 46 for 20 % missed operons and L ≥ 23.5 for 40 %. Even an oracle with the true π₀ needs L ≥ 9π₀/(1 − π₀) = 36 and 13.5. In words: a missed operon has to be ~25–50 times more concentrated near Δ = 0 than an unrelated neighbour pair before |Δ| alone can single it out at 10 % FDR, because the null |Δ| also has its mode at 0. The M6-09 power grid's planted effects of 0.25–1.0 SD correspond to L ≤ 4 and are predicted to give zero calls whatever the decoy counts, bins, +1 or π₀.

    The +1 per bin (secondary). At the top of the list, before any decoy passes, FDR ≥ π₀ Σ_b N_NO,b/(N_decoy,b + 1)/k for k calls: one phantom decoy per bin, ≈ B · N_target/N_decoy in total for balanced bins, versus N_target/N_decoy for single-bin target–decoy. With B = 5 and equal-size sets about 50 calls must sit above every decoy before q can reach 0.1. The sum is dominated by the bin with the fewest decoys per target; on a real genome the overlapping-gap bin (< 0 bp) is common for NO pairs and rare for SW pairs, so one sparse bin can hold q near 1 on its own. This floor binds only when the bound above allows calls at all (planted spread ≲ 0.05 null SD).

    5. Fewer or coarser bins (config operons.distance_bins_bp, but a default change still needs an ADR): shrinks the floor by B; weakens the distance-confounding protection that attack A tests.
    6. A higher level for this family (e.g. 0.25, exploratory): cannot cross the bound either (0.36 at 20 %, σ₁ = 0.1).
    7. More decoys: CV and SW counts are fixed by the genome; adding other decoy classes (e.g. more distant opposite-strand pairs) gives more decoys but they mimic NO pairs less well. Shrinks only the +1 floor.

    The missed-operon step (M6-06)¶

    giap.operons.missed.missed_operons(pairs, cfg, *, genes=None) applies the engine above to the M6-04 pair table.

    • Targets. NO pairs whose two genes are eligible (not partial), both detected, with finite abundance_log2 and Δ. Imputed abundances and rna_between pairs are kept (lead decision 2), since §7.4.4 excludes imputation only from the t fit.
    • Decoys. CV and SW pairs with both genes detected and finite abundances. The code applies partial to targets only. In a production gene table a partial gene is never detected (detection_status = partial_excluded), so both sides are filtered the same way in practice.
    • Configuration. operons.decoys selects the sets; an empty list is a config error. A configured set with no usable pair is dropped with a log line.
    • Binning. Both sides are binned by intergenic_bp over operons.distance_bins_bp, and operons.pi0 is passed through. A bin with targets and no decoys of a surviving set is named in a warning: it contributes its whole target count, which can push every q to 1 (spec example 7).

    Output rows. One row per NO pair. A scored target has:

    • test = missed_operon_decoy, stat = −|Δ|, effect = |Δ|;
    • q_value from max_decoy_fdr, and lfdr (ranking only);
    • which (the deciding decoy set), method = decoy_fdr, null_model = <sets>_reweighted;
    • p_value, ci_low, ci_high and n_perm NA (lead decision 1: a decoy FDR has no p, and a pseudo-p would invite a second BH).

    bin_counts (N_NO, N_cv, N_sw per bin) goes to the run report so a reader can see the reweighting. Nothing is scored, and every NO row is flagged, in three cases:

    • the scale is relative (relative_scale);
    • there are fewer than 20 scored NO pairs (too_few_no_pairs);
    • no decoy set survives (too_few_no_pairs).

    The claim and its error estimate. The claim is "co-abundant at q ≤ 0.1", and its error estimate is the decoy q (§8.1). There is no CI for |Δ| of a single pair, so ci_low and ci_high are NA; the row must say so with ci_unestimated, as in M6-05. The lfdr is not a claim. In a bin with no decoys it is too small (see Known asymmetry), so it must not be shown as an error estimate.

    π₀. The default operons.pi0 = 1 is the conservative choice. The NO class contains the missed operons, so the true null share is below 1. A configured π₀ < 1 multiplies every q and lfdr by π₀ with no estimate behind it. Until the manifest records the π₀ used (M6-08, backlog), such a q cannot be read correctly from pairs.tsv alone.

    Power. As stated in the power section above, the step controls the FDR and will make almost no calls at realistic effects. Its unit example 6 makes calls only because the planted pairs have |Δ| ≤ 10⁻³ null SD. On a real genome an empty q_value ≤ 0.1 list is the expected result. It is not evidence against missed operons, and the lfdr ranking is the useful output.

    Level under structured nulls, and the imputation point mass (statistician review of PR #148)¶

    Simulation setup.

    • Classes and bins. Every pair is null, so any call is false. The five distance bins have NO/CV/SW mixes that deliberately differ: NO 0.15/0.35/0.30/0.15/0.05, CV 0.05/0.15/0.30/0.30/0.20, SW 0.05/0.10/0.25/0.30/0.30.
    • Sizes. An E. coli-like genome (700 NO, 450 CV, 300 SW) and a large one (2,000 of each). R = 500 replicates.
    • Δ. Δ ~ N(0, 1).
    • Imputation. Each gene is filled at rate r, as under the default abundance.missing = median. A pair with both genes filled has Δ = 0 exactly, because the fill is one shared value. A pair with one gene filled has Δ = observed − fill.
    • Co-imputation. "Co-imputation ρ" makes the partner of a filled NO gene also filled with extra probability ρ. This models same-strand neighbours that lose quantification together, such as tandem paralogs that share their peptides.

    What the table shows.

    • Continuous nulls stay at level. That includes NO and decoy pairs whose abundance mix, and so their Δ noise, differ. This is the identifiability bound at work: with a continuous null, a false call needs the null NO pairs to be about 10 times tighter than the decoys.
    • Imputation removes that protection. Pairs with both genes filled sit at Δ = 0 exactly, a point mass with L = f₁(0)/f₀(0) = ∞. Ties do go to the decoys, so only the excess of tied targets over the reweighted tied decoys counts. Even so, once the number of tied targets T₀ exceeds about (1/α) · [reweighted tied decoys + Σ_b N_NO,b/(N_decoy,b + 1)], every one of them is called. In the ρ = 1 row that is ≈ 200 false calls, and the per-bin +1 floor is the only margin. That floor is about 7–8 for the large bins and 11–19 for the E. coli-like bins, so the risk grows with genome size.
    • When the attack is harmless. With equal per-gene imputation rates in every class it is harmless. It needs filled genes to pair up preferentially in NO pairs.
    • Excluding imputed pairs fixes it. Excluding every pair with an imputed gene, from targets and decoys alike, restores the level and costs nothing measurable, because the family has no power at realistic effects anyway.

    • the abundance_imputed state must reach every pair row and every call (lead decision 2 assumes it does);
    • the §8.6 exclude sensitivity rerun is the built-in check: a call that vanishes under exclude rested on an imputed value.

    • M-2. Abundance and detection mix differing between NO and decoys, the last two rows. Gated.
    • M-3. The M6-01 attacks: AR(1) abundance (attack A), and detection confounded with distance (attack B).

    K-12 cut-off calibration (M6-12)¶

    Issue M6-12 (GIAP_SPEC.md §15.4 steps 3–5, §7.1.3, §7.1.5, §7.4.6), spec docs/specs/M6-12-uniop-terminator-cutoffs.md with its lead decisions of 2026-10-23. Module giap.stats.cutoffs holds the pure functions (auroc, threshold_curve, choose_cutoff, fit_distance_model). validation/calibrate_cutoffs.py is the driver: it does the re-map, directons, windows, matching, the bootstrap and the writers. The truth is RegulonDB 14.5.0 on K-12 MG1655 (U00096.3, circular, 4,641,652 bp).

    There is no hypothesis test in M6-12. Every output is an estimate (AUROC, precision, recall, d₀, s) with a 95 % CI, or a cut-off chosen by a fixed rule. No p or q value is produced, so there is no §8.1 family and nothing to correct. The M6 gate clause is "UniOP-alone AUROC recorded" (§13 row M6). It has no threshold, so no test is run on it.

    Notation: L is the labelled pairs (label ≠ ambiguous) that have a UniOP p. y = 1 for positive and 0 for negative. P = Σy and N = |L| − P. An item is called at threshold t iff score ≥ t; this is the pipeline's comparison (classify, build_tus, the flag code).

    Directons: the resampling unit¶

    A directon is a maximal run of consecutive genes on one strand, in genome order. It is computed on the whole gene model (CDS, sORF, pseudogene; all 4,373 genes), before ambiguous pairs are dropped. On a circular replicon, the last and first runs are one directon when they share a strand. Each same-strand adjacent pair belongs to exactly one directon, the one holding both of its genes. n_directons counts the directons that hold at least one pair of L, because those are the resampling units.

    Why directons, and not pairs or TUs: - A RegulonDB TU is a run of consecutive same-strand genes, so every TU lies inside one directon. The dependence between pairs comes from shared TUs (one operon gives several positive pairs, and its boundary gives a negative). That dependence is therefore within a directon, never across directons. - TUs themselves cannot be the unit. A negative pair joins two TUs, and overlapping TUs (alternative promoters) give pairs several TU IDs, so a pair has no unique TU. - Directons are the finest unit that contains all the TU dependence. Coarser units, such as genomic windows, would add little and lose clusters. - Across directons, the remaining dependence (UniOP's genome-wide features; RegulonDB's study bias towards well-studied regions) is not modelled. It is small next to the within-TU dependence. The directon CI is therefore the right CI, but it is not a guaranteed upper bound on the variance.

    The resampler is giap.stats.bootstrap.tu_block_bootstrap(df, tu_col="directon_id", stat_fn, B=2000, rng, level=0.95), without strata. Each replicate draws n_directons directons with replacement and keeps all their L rows. Replicates have variable size and a variable P/N mix. That is intended: the class mix is part of the sampling variability of a single genome's truth set.

    The single-pair comparison line (lead decision 2) is the same function with tu_col = "row_id", a column of unique pair IDs, so each pair is its own cluster. It is reported as *_ci_iid, for comparison only.

    UniOP AUROC¶

    • Estimate. The Mann–Whitney AUROC = (1/(P·N)) Σ_{i∈pos} Σ_{j∈neg} ([p_i > p_j] + ½[p_i = p_j]).
    • Computation. Use average ranks: AUROC = (R₊ − P(P+1)/2)/(P·N), where R₊ is the sum of the average ranks (scipy.stats.rankdata, method average) of the positives over all of L. Average ranks give each tie exactly ½. UniOP's output is heavily tied (153 distinct values over 2,739 pairs), so this matters.
    • Undefined case. P = 0 or N = 0 makes the AUROC undefined. auroc returns NaN.
    • In a bootstrap replicate, that NaN is dropped. With fewer than B/2 valid replicates the CI is (NaN, NaN), written as null.
    • On the full data, P = 0 or N = 0 is a ValueError (the "< 500 labelled pairs" guard).
    • CI. The 95 % percentile CI of the directon bootstrap: linear quantiles at 0.025 and 0.975 of the valid replicates (percentile_ci).
    • Reported. The estimate, the CI, P, N and n_directons.

    Distance AUROC. The distance-model AUROC is the same statistic on −d, with the same CI. It does not depend on the fitted d₀ and s, because the model is monotone in d. It is the quality of the fallback that runs when UniOP fails.

    Recommended (R4): report the paired difference AUROC(UniOP) − AUROC(−d), with its directon CI. It is one more stat_fn that computes both AUROCs on the same replicate. On K-12 the two AUROCs are close (see the design check), and only the paired CI can say whether UniOP adds anything over distance.

    The cut-off rule (lead decision 1: the stable reading)¶

    Candidates and counts. The candidates are the distinct observed scores, sorted in descending order: u₁ > u₂ > … > u_m. For each u_k: - TP_k = #{y = 1, score ≥ u_k} and FP_k = #{y = 0, score ≥ u_k}; - precision_k = TP_k/(TP_k + FP_k) and recall_k = TP_k/P; - F1_k = 2TP_k/(2TP_k + FP_k + (P − TP_k)) = 2TP_k/(TP_k + FP_k + P).

    These are cumulative counts. All items tied at u_k enter together, at the exact value u_k.

    The precision test is in integers. With the target 0.90 = 9/10, "precision_k ≥ 0.90" is 10·TP_k ≥ 9·(TP_k + FP_k). For a general target a/b in lowest terms, it is b·TP_k ≥ a·(TP_k + FP_k). A precision of exactly 0.9 counts as reached. No floating-point division is used.

    Rule (stable reading). - Let k* be the largest k such that every j ≤ k passes the test. This is the last candidate before the first failing one in descending order. - If k* exists, t* = u_{k*} and rule = precision_0.90. - If u₁ already fails, no t qualifies. Then t* = the u_k of maximum F1, and among equal F1 values the smallest u_k (the largest k); rule = max_f1. - F1 values are compared exactly. F1_k > F1_j iff TP_k·(TP_j + FP_j + P) > TP_j·(TP_k + FP_k + P), in Python integers or Fraction.

    Properties the tests rely on. 1. Every candidate at or above t* has precision ≥ 0.90. This is what "stable" means: a low-threshold spike in a non-monotone curve cannot set the cut-off. 2. The stable t* is ≥ the literal t*, the smallest u_k with precision ≥ 0.90 (the literal qualifying set contains the stable one). The ADR prints the literal value when it differs. - In spec example 4 the literal value is 0.90 and the stable value is 0.92. Under lead decision 1, example 4's expected t* is 0.92, with rule = precision_0.90, precision 1.000 and recall 8/9. 3. The stable rule depends on the first candidate. If the top tie group has precision < 0.90 (for example 4 positives and 1 negative tied at the maximum), no t qualifies and max-F1 fires, even if lower thresholds reach 0.90. This follows lead decision 1 as written. It needs its own unit test. - On K-12 the top UniOP group is 252 positives and 5 negatives. In 2,000 directon replicates the precision rule fired every time. 4. All scores equal: one candidate, AUROC = 0.5. t* is that value under either branch. 5. F1 = 0 at every candidate can only happen if P = 0, which the guard already rejects.

    Recorded value. - t* is an observed score, written as Python's shortest round-trip repr of the float, and never rounded. The pipeline then compares the same parsed float with ≥, so every item tied at t* stays called. - TerminatorNet's candidates near 1.0 differ only in the 12th to 16th decimal (the design check found t* = 0.9999999999961204). Any rounding would move items across the cut-off. - threshold_curve returns (u_k, TP_k, FP_k) as integers. Precision, recall and F1 are derived for the TSV only.

    Precision and recall at t*, and the selection effect¶

    UniOP. - Precision(t*) and recall(t*) get 95 % directon-bootstrap CIs with t held fixed. Each replicate counts TP, FP and P at the same t; t* is not re-selected. - That CI is a CI for "the precision of the threshold t*", not for "the precision achieved by the rule". - Holding t* fixed ignores the selection. The rule chose t* because the observed precision there is ≥ 0.90, so the point estimate is biased upwards, and the fixed-t CI does not cover the variability of the choice. - The ADR must say this in those words.

    How large the selection effect is (design check on the K-12 inputs; preliminary, not the record): - In-sample precision at t*: 0.9017. - 10-fold directon cross-validation of the whole rule, choosing t on 9 folds and counting TP/FP on the held-out fold, pooled, 50 repeats: 0.9008 (SD over repeats 0.001). - The out-of-bag precision of a bootstrap-selected t: 0.9025. - So the optimism is about 0.001, against a fixed-t CI half-width of about 0.017. The selection effect is real but negligible here, because the precision curve is shallow around t*. - t* itself is not stable: 13 % of directon replicates re-select the same candidate, and the 95 % range of re-selected t* is [0.458, 0.610]. t* is known only to about ±0.08; the cut-off's precision is what is stable.

    Recommended (R1, non-blocking, cheap): add precision_cv, the pooled directon 10-fold CV precision of the rule (folds from the same rng, drawn after the bootstrap streams below), and threshold_boot_q, the 2.5 / 50 / 97.5 % quantiles of the re-selected t* over the AUROC replicates. Together these make the selection statement quantitative.

    What "precision 0.90" means. The 0.90 is the share of true operon pairs among all pairs called at t*: a cumulative precision. It is not the reliability of a pair near the cut-off. In the design check, the pairs with p ∈ [0.53, 0.65) were operon pairs 50 % of the time. The ADR should say this, because users read pair_threshold as a per-pair confidence.

    TerminatorNet. - Precision(t*) and recall(t*) get Wilson 95 % intervals, as the spec says. Precision is over eligible calls; recall is over eligible truth. - The Wilson interval treats calls as independent. Calls cluster: one window can hold several calls, and up to 3 matched calls fall in one window. So Wilson is slightly anti-conservative. - In the design check, a bootstrap over windows (each gene's downstream window is a cluster) gave [0.194, 0.258], against Wilson's [0.197, 0.257]: about 6 % wider. That is too small to block on. - Recommended (R2): report the window-cluster interval beside Wilson, or state the 6 %. - recall_all and recall_by_type get Wilson intervals over their own denominators.

    The d₀ / s refit¶

    Model. p(operon | d) = 1/(1 + exp((d − d₀)/s)), which is y ~ Bernoulli(σ(β₀ + β₁d)) with d₀ = −β₀/β₁ and s = −1/β₁. The fit uses all of L, whatever its UniOP p, with d = intergenic_distance(a, b, replicon) (negative for overlaps, across the origin on a circular replicon). The fit is unpenalised maximum likelihood.

    Algorithm. This replaces the spec's plain Newton from (2.5, −0.05); see B1. 1. Separation check first (exact, one covariate). The 1-D logistic MLE exists iff the classes overlap in d both ways: - max{d : y = 1} > min{d : y = 0}, and - max{d : y = 0} > min{d : y = 1}.

    If the first fails, the data are (quasi-)separated with operons at short distance, and β₁ → −∞. If the second fails, the separation runs the other way. Either is a failure, reported before any iteration. 2. Start at β = (logit(P/|L|), 0). This is the intercept-only MLE, which lies inside the region where Newton is well behaved. 3. Damped Newton (IRLS) on the log-likelihood ℓ, computed stably with logaddexp. - Step δ = H⁻¹g. - Halve the step until ℓ(β + hδ) ≥ ℓ(β), with at most 30 halvings. Because ℓ is concave, the ascent is monotone. - Stop when max|hδ| < 1e-10, after at most 100 iterations. 4. Failure is any of the following: - the separation check fails; - no convergence in 100 iterations; - a singular Hessian (LinAlgError), which must be caught and reported, not raised; - a non-finite β; - β₁ ≥ 0; - |β₁| > 1 (s < 1 bp). This is a backstop: under quasi-separation the damped Newton can "converge" on saturated weights, at β₁ ≈ −10.6 in the example-5 separated data.

    A failure gives converged: false and keeps d₀ = 50, s = 20 with calibrated: false.

    CI. The same directon bootstrap refits each replicate. A failed replicate is NaN and is dropped (fewer than B/2 valid gives null). The CIs are the 95 % percentile CIs of d₀ and of s, taken separately. On K-12 no replicate can separate: the largest positive d is 559, and the smallest negative d is −59. On synthetic data with known d₀ and s (part A tests, R = 300, B = 200) these percentile CIs cover at about 0.92–0.93, slightly below 0.95 (0.89 for d₀ at B = 100). Coverage is recorded, not gated.

    Fit quality. - The log-loss of the refit and of the placeholder (50, 20) on L are both in-sample. The refit's gain is therefore optimistic by about 2/|L| nats (two parameters), which is negligible at |L| = 2,739. They are reported without a CI. - The linear-logit model misfits K-12. It is too flat between 0 and 150 bp, and too confident beyond 300 bp, where about 10 % of the labelled pairs are still operon pairs. The model family is fixed by §7.1.3. The ADR should show the binned table (observed vs fitted share by distance bin) from the run.

    TerminatorNet¶

    Window eligibility. - Each gene of the model has one downstream window, _window(g, neighbour, replicon, 50): - it starts at g's 3′ end, moved 50 bp back into g; - it ends at the nearest end of g's genome-order neighbour on either strand, in the direction of transcription; - it is unrolled across the origin on a circular replicon, and is 300 bp past the stop on a linear contig end; - lo ≥ hi gives no window. - A call on strand s is eligible iff its whole interval lies in some window of a strand-s gene, for a shift in {0, ±length}. - Containment, rather than overlap, keeps calls that cross into the next gene out of the denominator. - Eligible calls are those in the §15.4 sense, "calls in intergenic regions downstream of genes". Only eligible calls enter precision. The design check found 2,447 of 8,294 calls eligible.

    The match. - An eligible call c matches when a RegulonDB terminator r on the same contig and strand has an interval gap max(0, r.start − c.end, c.start − r.end) ≤ 50 bp, for a shift in {0, ±length}. Overlap is a gap of 0. - An opposite-strand terminator never matches. - Several calls may match one terminator, and each counts (§15.4 "share of calls"). - Truth eligibility, the recall denominator, uses the same containment rule on the RegulonDB intervals.

    The rule. - Rule 2 runs on (confidence, matched) over the eligible calls. F1 in the max-F1 branch is therefore call-level: its recall is TP/(all matched eligible calls), not the truth-level recall that is reported. - The pure function sees only (score, y). Truth-level F1 would need the call-to-terminator assignment. That choice is the lead's; the call-level F1 is the specified default.

    Why precision is a lower bound, and when it is not. - RegulonDB 14.5.0 lists 375 terminators on K-12, far fewer than the genome's intrinsic terminators, and includes Rho-dependent ones that TerminatorNet does not model. - An unmatched call can therefore be a real terminator RegulonDB lacks. This pushes the observed precision below the true precision. - But a false call can also land within 50 bp of a listed terminator by coincidence, because both concentrate just past stop codons. This pushes the observed precision above the true precision. - So the observed precision is a lower bound only when missing terminators outnumber coincidences. This is likely, but it is not guaranteed (B2). - The honest bound: let c be the probability that a false eligible call matches by coincidence. Then obs ≤ true + (1 − true)·c, so true ≥ (obs − c)/(1 − c). This bound holds whatever RegulonDB is missing. - c is estimated with a placement null (B2). The ADR states both: the observed precision, which is a lower bound to the extent that RegulonDB is incomplete, and the coincidence-corrected bound.

    The coincidence null (B2). - R = 200 placements. Each placement moves every eligible call to a uniformly chosen same-strand window that is long enough to hold it. - The call keeps its length, its strand and its offset from the window's 3′-side start (lo for +, hi for −). Keeping the offset preserves the "just past the stop" position distribution that makes coincidences likely. - The window is drawn uniformly among the same-strand windows that fit, from the same rng stream, after the bootstrap streams. - Each placed call is matched with the same 50-bp gap rule. - c(t) is the mean match rate over placements among calls with confidence ≥ t. The confidence travels with the call. - Reported: coincidence_rate at t*, and precision_lower_bound = max(0, (obs − c)/(1 − c)). - In the design check, a cruder uniform-in-window placement gave c ≈ 0.06–0.075 overall. The overall observed match rate was 0.120, so roughly half of all matches could be coincidental. At t* (obs 0.226) the corrected bound would be about 0.17.

    Recall is low by construction. Rho-dependent terminators are in the truth, and TerminatorNet does not call them. recall_by_type makes this visible. Recall is not a property of the cut-off alone.

    What the rule did on K-12 (design check, preliminary): - No t reaches 0.90 against RegulonDB. The best precision over thresholds with ≥ 20 calls is 0.229. So the TerminatorNet cut-off is a max-F1 cut-off, under either reading of the rule. - t* = 0.9999999999961204, with 727 calls at or above it, precision 0.226 and F1 0.318. - The F1 curve is flat between 0.9 and 1.0 (0.310–0.318). Re-selection in window replicates picks the same candidate 12 % of the time, with a 95 % range of [0.9957, 1.0]. - terminatornet_yes will therefore mean "saturated network output". Its 12th decimal is noise. - The run records it exactly, which is correct for K-12. - On another genome, or another TerminatorNet build, the float pattern near 1.0 can differ. Recommended (R3, a question for the lead): record the F1 flatness in the ADR, and consider a one-standard-error rule. That rule takes the smallest t whose F1 is within one bootstrap SE of the maximum. It would replace a noise-level argmax with a stable one, but it changes the §15.4 text, so it is Khoa's call.

    Implementation note for PR-B. parse_terminatornet reads plain text (Path.read_text). The recording is terms.tsv.gz, so the driver must decompress it first, or the parser must learn gzip in a separate change. As the spec section 4 call is written, it fails with UnicodeDecodeError.

    Bootstrap streams and reproducibility¶

    The run has one generator, rng = giap.rng.for_step("m6_12_cutoffs", 20261014) (the config default seed). Each tu_block_bootstrap call draws one (B, n_directons) block, B = 2,000. The draw order is fixed, and the tests pin it.

    1. UniOP AUROC.
    2. UniOP precision at t*.
    3. UniOP recall at t*.
    4. Distance AUROC.
    5. AUROC difference (R4, if adopted).
    6. d₀.
    7. s.
    8. The iid comparison lines, in the same order as 1–7, with tu_col="row_id".
    9. The TerminatorNet coincidence placements (B2).
    10. The CV folds (R1, if adopted).

    d₀ and s use separate draws (two refits per replicate). That is statistically fine: each CI is a marginal CI. It costs about 4,000 IRLS fits, which takes seconds. The same inputs and seed give a byte-identical cutoffs.json apart from date.

    Limitations, and what transfers to other genomes¶

    • One genome, one truth set. Every number is about K-12 MG1655 against RegulonDB 14.5.0. The CIs are over resampled directons of this genome. They describe the uncertainty of K-12's numbers, not the genome-to-genome variation users will meet. No CI here covers transfer.
    • Precision depends on prevalence. Precision at t* is π·TPR/(π·TPR + (1 − π)·FPR), where π is the share of operon pairs among the scored pairs. K-12's labelled π is 0.632, with TPR 0.848 and FPR 0.159 at t*. At the same t, a genome with π = 0.5 gets precision ≈ 0.84, and one with π = 0.4 gets ≈ 0.78.
    • The labelled set also excludes 10.7 % ambiguous pairs, and only covers genes RegulonDB has studied. Its π is not even K-12's all-pairs π.
    • pair_threshold is a K-12-tuned cut-off. Its 0.90 is not a guarantee elsewhere. AUROC is prevalence-free, but it is not genome-free (UniOP's features depend on the genome's phylogenetic neighbourhood).
    • d₀ and s transfer worse than AUROC. β₀ absorbs K-12's prevalence. On a genome with different intergenic-length statistics or operon density, the fallback's probabilities are miscalibrated, even though its ranking (AUROC of −d) may hold.
    • Truth labels are not perfect.
    • A "negative" pair (different TUs, none containing both) can be an operon pair under conditions RegulonDB has not sampled.
    • A "positive" can come from a weakly supported TU.
    • Label noise of either kind biases both AUROC and precision towards 0.5 and the base rate. So the UniOP figures are conservative with respect to this noise. They are not conservative with respect to study bias, because well-studied operons are probably the easy ones.
    • Possible training overlap. If UniOP's development or training used K-12 operons, the AUROC is partly in-sample for the tool. This was not verified here. The ADR should state UniOP's published training data, or say that it is unknown.
    • TerminatorNet. Precision is against an incomplete truth, with coincidences, as above. The cut-off is max-F1 on call-level F1, sits at saturated outputs, and is unstable in its decimals. The 0.90 target is unreachable against RegulonDB. So terminatornet_yes carries no precision guarantee on any genome, and the ADR must not describe it as a 90 % cut-off.
    • Selection. Both cut-offs are chosen and evaluated on the same data. This is negligible for UniOP precision (≈ 0.001, see above). For TerminatorNet it is unquantified.

    Statistician review of the design (PR-A, 2026-10-23)¶
