    Detection-limit model (P(detect | abundance) and P(miss))¶

    Implements giap.operons.detection_limit (GIAP_SPEC.md §7.4.3 "One detected, one not", §7.3.3 conventions, §8.7; issue M6-02, docs/specs/M6-02-detection-limit-model.md). Consumer: M6-05H.

    Status: method reviewed by the statistician before implementation (2026-10-21). The estimator below replaces the naive logistic fit that the spec's wording suggests; the model, its features, its training rows and its outputs are the spec's.

    Why the naive fit is wrong¶

    Both training sets in §7.4.3 are selected on the response:

    • the peptide model is trained on the observable peptides of detected genes, and a gene is detected exactly when at least k_min of those peptides were seen;
    • the replicate model is trained on genes seen in some but not all runs, i.e. with 1 ≤ S ≤ R − 1 per-run detections out of R.

    An ordinary logistic regression on such rows estimates P(seen | features, gene selected), not P(seen | features). At low abundance a detected gene has, almost by definition, one peptide seen out of several, so the per-peptide rate looks flat in abundance: the abundance coefficient is attenuated and the intercept is pushed up. The fitted P_det is then too high at the low end, which is exactly where the undetected partner lives, so P(miss) is too small (anti-conservative).

    At R = 2 the replicate training set is degenerate: every retained gene has S = 1 of 2, the response is 1/2 for every gene whatever its abundance, and the logistic MLE is the constant 1/2 (slope exactly 0). No estimator can recover the slope from those rows: P(S = 1 | 1 ≤ S ≤ 1) = 1.

    Estimator: conditional (truncated) likelihood¶

    Same model, same rows, the likelihood conditioned on the selection event (the conditional likelihood of capture–recapture with covariates; Huggins 1989, Biometrika 76:133–140).

    Peptide model. For a training gene g with observable peptides j, linear predictor η_gj = β₀ + β_a·a_g + β_L·L_j + β_G·GRAVY_j, p_gj = σ(η_gj), and P_det,g = P(X_g ≥ k_min) the Poisson-binomial survival over g's peptides (the quantity p_detect already returns):

    ℓ_c(β) = Σ_g [ Σ_j ( o_gj·η_gj − log(1 + e^η_gj) ) − log P_det,g(β) ]

    Score: Σ_g Σ_j ( o_gj − P(O_gj = 1 | X_g ≥ k_min) )·x_gj, with P(O_gj = 1 | X_g ≥ k) = p_gj · P(X_g,−j ≥ k − 1) / P(X_g ≥ k); for k_min = 1 this is p_gj / P_det,g.

    • The conditioning event must be the event P_det models: training genes = genes with finite abundance_log2 and at least k_min distinct observable peptides seen in C11 (any route, any uniqueness, Q2). A detected gene whose evidence lies only outside the observable set (missed cleavages, length outside 7–30) contradicts the conditioning and is not a training gene; count it in the detection summary. For the detected genes of the spec that satisfy the event this is the spec's training set.
    • Genes with exactly k_min observable peptides contribute 0 (all seen given detection); they are still counted in n_fit, which stays "rows of the training genes".
    • Fit unpenalised (Newton or BFGS with the analytic score; centre and scale the features for the optimiser, report coef on the raw scale). Do not use a cross-validated ridge on the naive log loss: shrinking β_a towards 0 is the anti-conservative direction. If the optimiser fails to converge or the data are separated, usable = False (spec degenerate row "separated logistic"). A fixed, negligible ridge purely for numerical safety is acceptable only if the calibration criteria below still pass.
    • Cross-fitting, folds and model_for_tu are unchanged (GroupKFold by TU, folds from giap.rng.for_step("operons.detection_limit", seed)).

    Replicate model. Per-run detection probability q_g = σ(γ·z_g) on the gene's mean abundance_log2 plus detectability.covariates; S_g of R runs. Training genes as in the spec (1 ≤ S_g ≤ R − 1); the likelihood is the doubly truncated binomial

    ℓ_c(γ) = Σ_g [ S_g log q_g + (R − S_g) log(1 − q_g) − log(1 − (1 − q_g)^R − q_g^R) ]

    (the binomial coefficient is constant). It is identified for R ≥ 3. At R = 2 the replicate model is not fitted and usable = False with reason replicate_two_runs: under the spec's training set its MLE is the constant model, which spec item 6 already declares unusable; the code must decide this from R, not from a numerically small slope. When runs differ per gene (a run in which the gene could not be quantified), R is the gene's own number of runs.

    p_detect for the replicate model is the per-run probability q (spec item 4). The partner is undetected in the pooled table, i.e. in all R runs, so 1 − q overstates P(miss) relative to (1 − q)^R under run independence: the spec-literal choice is conservative. Changing it is an ADR (see Open decisions).

    Calibration criteria¶

    The slope alone has no teeth here: unlike M4-13 (trained on the population it scores, so a logistic MLE with an intercept is calibrated in the large by its score equation), this model is trained on a selected subset and scored on everyone. Proposed for DL1 (Khoa's decision; the tolerance file is protected): mean CITL over R within ±0.2 in addition to the slope in [0.8, 1.2]. The conditional fit gives |mean CITL| ≤ 0.03; the naive fit −0.89 to −1.36. Until a key exists, DL1 records the CITL.

    P(miss) is a probability, not a p-value¶

    M6-index decision 1: no q-value, no BH family. For a pair whose detected partner has abundance a_v, P(u undetected | a_v, H0) = P(miss); so P(P(miss) ≤ α and u undetected) ≤ α per opportunity if P(miss) is correctly estimated — the level is valid over opportunities, not conditionally on the pair being in the one-detected arm. An underestimated P(miss) breaks this: a pair with true P(miss) 0.2 estimated at 0.04 contributes 0.2 to the level at α = 0.05.

    Owed to M6-09 (attack C)¶

    • Report the level with both denominators: per opportunity (v detected) and per one-detected pair. Only the first is a validity property; a 2 × α trigger on the second can fire under a correct model (or stay silent under a biased one, as above at σ = 0.6).
    • Record the calibration in the large of P(miss): mean P(miss) over opportunities against the observed undetected share (the conditional fit matches to 0.01; the naive fit is 0.10 low).
    • Run the arm with k_min = 2 (two_peptide) and with a replicate-only input at R = 3 and R = 2 (the latter must give no_detection_limit_model).
    • Measurement error with selection in abundance_log2 of low-abundance detected genes (estimated from one or two observed peptides, biased upwards) is not in these simulations; it pushes the same direction and needs an M6-03 generator in which abundance is estimated, not given.

    Open decisions (ADRs for Khoa)¶

    1. Replicate model at R = 2: accept usable = False (inside the spec), or widen the training set to every gene seen in at least one run with the zero-truncated binomial likelihood, which is identified at R = 2 (slope 0.498 above). The second changes the spec's training set.
    2. Replicate p_detect: per-run q (spec, conservative) or pooled 1 − (1 − q)^R (correct under run independence, anti-conservative if detections are over-dispersed across runs).
    3. The DL1 CITL tolerance (above).

    Statistical review of the implementation (PR #157, head cf164a5, 2026-10-21)¶

    Simulations ran the code under review: Slurm jobs 1865238 and 1865261. The scripts and output are in $GIAP_ROOT/stat/m6-02/review/ (review_sim.py, review_sel.py, out/). R = 100 replicates per arm, 60 for the selection arm. The values below are means, with the Monte Carlo SE in brackets.

    Verdict: blocked on one item (B1). Everything else matches this note.

    Matches the note¶

    • Likelihoods. The peptide conditional likelihood and its score are correct, including the leave-one-out factor P(X_−j ≥ k − 1) for k_min = 2. The same holds for the doubly truncated binomial and its score. Since cf164a5, _newton returns only at a stationary point with −H positive definite.

    0.6 1 0.502 (0.002) 0.998 +0.006 (0.008) 0.302 0.304 0.004 0.011
    0.6 2 0.504 (0.002) 0.992 +0.015 (0.014) 0.434 0.443 0.003 0.009
    1.5 1 0.503 (0.002) 1.000 +0.012 (0.009) 0.305 0.313 0.006 0.016
    1.5 2 0.504 (0.002) 0.999 +0.017 (0.014) 0.420 0.459 0.005 0.014

    At σ = 1.5 with k_min = 2, P(miss) falls 0.04 below the observed share. This gap comes from the r sample, which is selected on both-detected pairs; the model itself has CITL +0.017. It belongs to M6-09 attack C, not to M6-02. - Cross-fitting is honest. The folds are a seeded partition of the training TUs. fold_coef[j] never saw fold j, and a TU that trained nothing gets the full model, which never saw it either. An undetected partner is never a training gene in either model. M6-05H must call model_for_tu with the TU of the detected partner v. That is the only gene of the pair that can have trained. - Replicate model at R ≥ 3. The slope is 0.504 (0.004) at R = 3 and 0.499 (0.003) at R = 4, against a truth of 0.5. At R = 2 the model is decided from the run count. With the per-run q, P(miss) is very conservative: mean 0.45 against an observed undetected share of 0.15, and level 0 at every α. This is as the note predicted. - Calibration tests. The DL1 slope gate has no teeth against the naive fit. The teeth are elsewhere: - DL2 (halved slopes); - the every-commit β_a recovery test (±0.08; the naive fit gives 0.35); - the slow unit CITL test (|CITL| ≤ 0.1 on an independent genome; the naive fit gives −0.89); - the k_min = 2 recovery test (±0.04; the k = 1 score gives 0.44).

    These tests are adequate for the failure this note predicted. They cannot detect B1, because in simulate_peptides detected is exactly X_obs ≥ k_min.

    B1 (blocking): the training genes are selected on detected, not on the conditioning event¶

    fit_peptide_model keeps called & finite(abundance) and then sum(o) >= k. The likelihood conditions on X_obs ≥ k_min. In the mayu and decoy_picked tiers, detected is a gene-level q ≤ α. That is a strict subset of the conditioning event: single-hit genes fail it at random. Genes that fail are missing from the training set, yet the likelihood treats them as possible. Because they are low-abundance genes with few peptides, the bias that this note's estimator was chosen to remove comes back, in the same direction (anti-conservative).

    Attack arm (review_sel.py): the pair design above with σ = 0.6. Every gene with ≥ 2 peptides seen is called, and a gene with one peptide seen is called with probability π₁.

    The fix is in the implementation and the tests, which are not mine to edit:

    • Training genes = finite abundance_log2 and ≥ k_min distinct observable peptides seen in C11, whatever detected says. This is decision 5's wording and this note's; spec item 2's "detected" is the special case. M4-11 gives finite abundances to undetected genes that have accepted peptides.
    • The test-author adds a case in which a gene with ≥ k_min observable peptides seen but detected = False contributes its rows to n_fit, and a recovery test with π₁ < 1.

    If the lead reads spec item 2 as binding, this needs a Khoa ADR. Until one exists, it stays blocking.

    B1 re-check (bac9046, Slurm 1865546, R = 60). B1 is fixed and the block is lifted. The selection arm against bac9046 reproduces the "training = X ≥ 1" rows to every printed digit:

    Non-blocking¶

    • N1: the event P(miss) models. Even with B1 fixed, P_det models X_obs ≥ k_min, not detected. Against the event "u not called", P(miss) is too small in the large: CITL −0.84 (π₁ = 0.5) and −1.39 (π₁ = 0.2). The per-opportunity level still held in these arms: at most 0.023 at α = 0.05 and 0.050 at α = 0.10. M6-05H must define the one-detected arm by u having < k_min observable peptides seen, or ADR the gap. This is owed to Khoa (M6-05H) and to M6-09 attack C.
    • N2: stray run label. With 5 % of the run labels dropped to "":
    • 2 real runs give R = 3 and the replicate model is fitted instead of replicate_two_runs;
    • with 3 real runs the slope is 0.473 (0.003) against 0.5.

    P(miss) stays conservative under the per-run q: level 0. A C16 validation item, not blocking. - N3: unstored covariate median. At scoring, a NaN covariate gives NaN, so the pair is not tested. This costs power, not validity. Store the median. If M6-05H passes cov=None, every replicate P(miss) is NaN, so M6-05H must pass the M4-10 covariates. - N4: replicate model under two_peptide. A run counts as seen with ≥ 1 peptide, while detection needs ≥ 2 distinct peptides pooled. P(miss) is then 0.41 against an observed share of 0.46 (R = 3, 1–4 peptides per gene), still level ≤ 0.003 at α = 0.10 because 1 − q is so conservative. Record it in the summary; it only matters if the pooled 1 − (1 − q)^R ADR is accepted. - N5: imputed abundances. A detected gene with an imputed abundance_log2 (no intensity) trains with a fill value. Excluding abundance_imputed rows from training would be cleaner; the current behaviour is measurement error in the direction this note already lists. - N6: the DL1 docstring still describes the naive oracle as "recorded, not gated" with intercept −0.97. It should describe the conditional fit. - The other backlog nits are numerical or test gaps, not statistical issues: NaN tu_id pooled into one fold group, rng consumption order, I/L, a constant feature giving a singular H.

    ADRs owed to Khoa¶

    1. The DL1 CITL tolerance of ±0.2 (tolerances.yaml is protected). The measured |CITL| is ≤ 0.02 at k_min = 1 and 2. A conditional-fit CITL record should also be added at k_min = 2 (DL1 runs only k_min = 1).
    2. If B1's fix is read as a change to spec item 2: the training set is the conditioning event, not detected.
    3. The one-detected event for M6-05H (N1).
    4. The two existing open decisions above: R = 2 zero-truncation, and the pooled replicate p_detect.
