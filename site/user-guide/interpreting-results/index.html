    Interpreting results¶

    Every claim GIAP makes carries one of four labels:

    • a q-value;
    • an estimated false discovery rate (FDR);
    • a family-wise error rate (FWER);
    • the label unestimated.

    That is the reporting rule. It does not mean every method is calibrated in every situation. Some methods ship in 1.0 with a known calibration failure. Their rows carry a flag, and their p- and q-values are not discovery claims. They are listed in Methods shipped flagged uncalibrated below. Read that section before you report any function, determinant, island, operon or variant result.

    This page explains what each label means in GIAP, and what a detection does and does not tell you. The statistical methods behind each estimate are documented one note per method under Methods.

    q-values, FDR, FWER and unestimated¶

    FDR. The false discovery rate is the expected share of false claims among the claims accepted at a threshold. GIAP estimates it in two ways:

    • from decoys, which are matched false hypotheses:
    • reversed or shuffled genome translations, for placement;
    • convergent and → ← → sandwich pairs, for missed operons;
    • from p-values, with Benjamini–Hochberg (BH) adjustment within a pre-declared family of tests.

    q-value. The q-value of a claim is the smallest FDR at which that claim would be accepted. Accepting every row with q_value ≤ 0.05 gives a list with an estimated FDR of at most 5 %. A q-value is a property of the list, not a probability that one row is wrong.

    BH and decoy q-values control the FDR within one family only. Nothing is controlled across families. If you scan several tables, expect roughly α × (number of discoveries) false ones in each family (multiple testing).

    FWER. The family-wise error rate is the probability of at least one false claim in the whole family. GIAP uses FWER for abundance islands. One family covers the whole run: each window is compared with the maximum statistic over the windows of every scanned replicon. The null comes from a circular TU-block bootstrap of the detrended TU scores. The FWER claim is conditional on a dependence regime (see islands below). It is not an unconditional guarantee.

    unestimated. GIAP labels a claim unestimated when the input cannot support an error estimate, for example:

    • gene detection from peptide sequences alone;
    • a test whose null has too few units.

    The claim is still reported, so you can inspect it. Do not report it as error-controlled. Flags such as ci_unestimated, class_fdr_unestimated, not_estimable, ora_uncalibrated and null_uncalibrated say which part is missing or uncalibrated (see the flag reference).

    Families and defaults. BH applies within the families fixed in code. Here are the main claims, with the default threshold of each (stats.alpha.* in the configuration reference):

    Claim Error control Default
    Peptide placed at a locus placement q-value from genome decoys: one list per decoy type × stratum (0, 1, 2 differences), length classes pooled 0.01
    Gene detected gene q-value (tiers decoy_picked, mayu) or unestimated 0.01
    Novel ORF FDR per class 0.01
    ALT call the 0.01 is the placement-q filter on supporting peptides (stats.alpha.variants); the class error rate is reported, never thresholded, else unestimated (see below) 0.01
    Region effect BH within each (region type, statistic) family 0.05
    Autocorrelation BH across lags 0.05
    Abundance island FWER, run-wide over replicons; conditional on the dependence regime 0.05
    Operon discordance BH over the both-detected pairs (both_detected_t); pooled level under smooth, monotone σ(abundance) only 0.1
    Missed operon decoy FDR 0.1
    Category detection (ORA) BH per ontology and direction; every row ora_uncalibrated: not a discovery claim 0.05
    GSEA BH per collection; conditional on detection, with no p_detect adjustment 0.05
    Determinant model permutation p-value; every null p null_uncalibrated: not a discovery claim 0.05

    Every p-value and q-value is reported, not only the significant ones. Every test row has the columns effect, ci_low, ci_high, method and null_model. Where no CI is estimated, ci_low and ci_high are NA and the row carries ci_unestimated. This is the case for:

    • every GSEA row (NES has no CI, ADR-0027);
    • every ORA row (effect = k − E[K | N], with no CI);
    • the autocorrelation lag rows;
    • the operon discordance rows;
    • the P(miss) rows, whose effect is also NA.

    The island CI is a different case. It is descriptive and conditional on selection: the window was chosen as the run-wide maximum. The CI therefore under-covers, essentially never contains zero, and its coverage has not been measured. The island's p_value is the only test.

    ALT calls. The class error rate estimates the share of erroneous reads among ALT-supporting de novo reads whose spectrum a database search identifies confidently. It is not the false-ALT rate among all ALT reads. It is biased upwards, up to a degenerate value of 1, when the reference search could not contain the ALT peptide. It is biased downwards by spectrum-quality selection. Read a rate of 1 from a reference-proteome search as "not measured" (variants).

    Methods shipped flagged uncalibrated¶

    These results are reported, but their p- and q-values are not error-controlled. Do not report them as discoveries.

    • Detection enrichment (ORA): ora_uncalibrated on every row. The detection-probability recalibration is anti-conservative for categories selected on TU size or detection probability (ADR-0035, ADR-0037; function). A skipped fit (tu_recal_skipped) is a second reason for the flag.
    • Determinant model null: null_uncalibrated on every null p. The permutation p is valid only when TUs are exchangeable. It fails under autocorrelation along the genome, positional covariates and detection selection (ADR-0034).
    • Operon discordance outside smooth noise. The discordance level holds under smooth, monotone σ(abundance). With a step change in noise at some abundance (the D-1b-step attack), it is exceeded, and no row is flagged (ADR-0055 Amendment 1).
    • Islands at strong, short-resolved autocorrelation. The FWER breaches its level at TU-level ρ = 0.9 when the bootstrap block is short. No row is flagged (see the next section).
    • Variant-call precision (gate M7). The simulated precision gate G1 and the de novo error attacks A6 are strict expected failures. Gate M7 is not recorded as passed. Mass-equivalent and swap ALTs stay high with the flag mass_equivalent (ADR-0049).

    Islands: the FWER holds in a stated regime¶

    The island FWER is claimed for these cases (islands › Validity domain, ADR-0021 Amendment 1):

    • TU-level autocorrelation ρ ≤ 0.5;
    • ρ = 0.9 on a chromosome of about 4,500 genes, where the block length is long enough.

    It is breached at ρ = 0.9 when the block length ℓ′ is 7 or less (0.095 to 0.208 at α = 0.05). It does not apply at all when context.islands.block_len is fixed. No per-row flag marks a breach, because no statistic reliably detects one. The report instead prints, per replicon:

    • the estimated dependence length ℓ̂ (0 means "not estimated", never "no autocorrelation");
    • the block length block_len;
    • the lag-1 Moran's I of the detrended TU scores.

    Use these to judge where your data sit.

    Operons: discordance and P(miss)¶

    • Discordance (test = both_detected_t in pairs.tsv) is one BH family over the pairs with both genes detected. It holds level only under smooth, monotone σ(abundance), and it has little power at realistic effect sizes (operons).
    • P(miss) (test = one_detected_pmiss) is the model's probability that this partner would be missed if it were co-expressed. It is not a significance level. These rows have q_value NA, sit outside the BH family, and never make a discordance call (ADR-0056). P(miss) is anti-conservative when detectability varies between proteins in ways the model does not see. Thresholding it at 0.05 flagged 0.198 of one-detected rows in a simulated null with such variation, where about 0.05 was expected.

    GSEA is conditional on detection¶

    Abundance GSEA ranks detected genes only. Its results are conditional on detection, with no p_detect adjustment (ADR-0026): when a set's genes are harder to detect, its detected members are selected for high abundance, and the set can look enriched without any true difference. Islands and operon discordance likewise carry no p_detect adjustment.

    • "No significant set" never means "no enrichment".
    • detection_confounded and genomic_cluster are screens, not guarantees: an unflagged row is not certified (function GSEA › Limits).
    • In a collection that has flagged rows, no FDR claim is made for the unflagged rows. Flagged rows enter the same BH family, and their effect on the other rows has not been re-measured for the R engine.

    Detection tiers¶

    How GIAP decides that a gene is detected depends on what the input contains. The tier is chosen once per run and recorded in the manifest and in genes.tsv (detection_tier). It is the first of the four below that the input allows:

    Tier When Detected means Gene error
    decoy_picked decoy rows with protein_ids exist, at least 80 % of them pair with a gene, and the decoys share a ranking column (q-value, PEP or score) with the targets gene q ≤ 0.01 by picked target–decoy competition over genes gene q-value
    mayu otherwise, the accepted peptides carry q-values or PEPs; also chosen when decoys exist but pair too rarely (warning decoy_pairing_low) gene q ≤ 0.01 by a MAYU-style estimate of false genes gene q-value
    two_peptide otherwise, peptide sequences only at least 2 distinct accepted peptides unestimated
    protein_list proteins only, no peptides a mapped input protein unestimated (inherited from the search engine)

    Decoys without a ranking column cannot be picked. Preflight warns about them, and the tier falls back.

    Under two_peptide, a gene with only one peptide gets detection_status = single_peptide_unestimated. It counts as not detected in the primary analysis and as detected in the sensitivity rerun. Partial genes at linear contig ends are partial_excluded. detected_2pep (at least 2 distinct accepted peptides) is reported in every tier.

    To get the strongest tier, keep the search engine's decoy rows (see Inputs). Details: detection tiers and MAYU gene FDR.

    Absence of detection is not absence of expression¶

    GIAP says "detected", never "expressed". A gene can go undetected for reasons unrelated to whether it is made. The protein may be:

    • short, with few observable peptides;
    • membrane-bound, or hard to ionise;
    • below the instrument's detection limit;
    • absent from the search database.

    To account for this, GIAP fits a detectability model. It predicts each gene's probability of detection (p_detect) from protein covariates, with cross-fitting. Region O − E tests and ORA compare observed detections with the sum of p_detect, never with raw counts. A region "detected less than expected" is detected less than its proteins' detectability predicts. That is a statement about detection, not about transcription or translation. GSEA, islands and operon discordance work on abundances and have no p_detect adjustment (see above).

    detectability_constant. The model is replaced by a constant in any of these cases:

    • fewer than 50 detected eligible genes;
    • fewer than 50 undetected eligible genes;
    • fewer TUs than cross-fitting folds.

    p_detect is then the overall detection rate for every gene, and every gene carries detectability_constant. The detection tests still run, but without any adjustment for detectability, and ORA uses ω = 1. Treat their results as unadjusted comparisons. "No gene detected" is only an extreme case of this, where the detection-dependent tests are not_estimable.

    Placement FDR is not search-engine FDR¶

    Two error rates apply to a peptide, and they answer different questions.

    • The search engine's FDR (the input q_value, pep) asks: is this spectrum matched to the right peptide sequence? GIAP inherits it and does not re-estimate it. Engine precursor q-values may themselves under-control FDR, and they do not control gene-level error (GIAP_SPEC.md §8.7). This is why gene detection uses the gene-level tiers. GIAP's pilot validation measures the realised false discovery proportion by entrapment.
    • GIAP's placement FDR (placement_q in peptide_hits.tsv) asks: given the peptide sequence, is it placed on the right locus of this genome? A short peptide can match a genome window by chance, and more so when mismatches are allowed. GIAP estimates this rate by searching the same peptides against decoy translations of the genome (reversed frames, or shuffled frames for de novo input). There is one list per decoy type and per stratum of 0, 1 or 2 differences, with length classes pooled.

    A peptide with a search q-value of 0.001 can still have a placement q-value of 1, for example when it matches the genome only with two substitutions. A peptide counts as evidence for a gene only when all of these hold:

    • it passes the search threshold (q_value ≤ input.q_value_max, when a q-value exists);
    • its placement is accepted (placement_q ≤ stats.alpha.placement, or same_db);
    • its locus class assigns it to the gene;
    • its uniqueness is unique or multi_identical;
    • under --genome-relation same, it is an exact hit.

    Peptides rejected by placement FDR are in mapping/unmapped.tsv with reason = rejected_placement_fdr.

    mapping/mapping_fdr.tsv lists the targets, decoys, π̂_wrong and FDR per length class and number of differences. Its per-length-class rows are descriptive. They are not the q-values: the q-values come from one pooled list per decoy type and stratum. Details: placement FDR › Strata and ranking.

    Same-strain input. Peptides searched against this genome's own proteins (search_db = same) skip the decoy step for exact hits: placement_q = 0 and the flag same_db. Tolerant hits still go through it.

    Kinds of placement¶

    Each row of mapping/peptide_hits.tsv is one peptide at one accepted locus. The columns route, n_diff, diff_types and flags say how the peptide was placed.

    Kind How to recognise it What it means
    Exact route = exact, n_diff = 0, empty diff_types the peptide occurs verbatim in a six-frame translation (I and L treated as equal)
    Tolerant route = tolerant, n_diff 1 or 2, diff_types from substitution, swap, block, near_block the peptide matches with up to 1 difference at 10–11 aa, or up to 2 at 12–40 aa; peptides shorter than 10 aa are never placed this way
    Frameshift route = tolerant, diff_types contains frameshift a ±1-nt frameshift: the prefix lies in one frame and the suffix in the adjacent frame, with at least 4 aa on each side

    The difference types count one each:

    • substitution: one residue differs;
    • swap: two adjacent residues swapped;
    • block: a mass-equivalent exchange, such as N for GG or Q for AG;
    • near_block: a near-equal mass exchange, such as W for AD or EG;
    • frameshift: the frame shift itself.

    Tolerant placements are judged at π̂_wrong = 1, which is conservative. They carry the flag strain_difference when --genome-relation related, and genes with such evidence carry tolerant_evidence.

    In mapping/mappings.gff3, a frameshift placement has two match_part children, one per frame. It also has the attribute frameshift_reread (the base read twice) or frameshift_skipped (the base skipped), giving the 1-based position of that base. In mappings.bed, the two parts are BED12 blocks.

    Flags on placements (flags column; full list in the flag reference):

    • readthrough: the tolerant alignment substituted a residue for the annotated stop codon at the end of a CDS or sORF (cost 1, recorded as a substitution). This is evidence of stop-codon read-through, or a C-terminal extension (locus class cterm_extension). mappings.gff3 gives the codon's 1-based position as readthrough_codon. Only annotated terminal stops can be read this way; every other stop codon stays unmatchable. Read-through rows are part of the ordinary placement FDR of their stratum. That FDR does not control the read-through subset on its own.
    • sec_site and pyl_site mark peptides that consume a recoded TGA (selenocysteine, U) or TAG (pyrrolysine, O) codon. They are specified in M7-16 (part 16c) but are not in the 1.0 peptide_hits schema. A 1.0 run does not write them.
    • same_db: an exact hit from same-strain input, accepted without the decoy step.
    • low_complexity: the peptide has at most 3 distinct residues, with I and L counted as one. For such peptides, the chance-hit score E can underestimate chance hits by 1.9× to 15×. The decoys keep the q-value valid, and the flag changes nothing in it (ADR-0011).
    • origin_spanning: the placement crosses the origin of a circular replicon.
    • multi, multi_identical: the peptide has accepted loci in more than one locus class, or in identical gene copies. uniqueness (unique, multi_identical, shared) says whether the peptide supports one gene.
    • engine_protein_mismatch: with same-strain input, the gene GIAP chose is not among the engine's protein IDs.
    • nterm_extension_evidence: the peptide lies upstream of the annotated start, in frame.

    Empty results¶

    A table with only a header means the step ran and found nothing at its threshold. For example, a header-only tables/novel_orfs.tsv means no novel ORF or start-site candidate passed its class FDR. It is a result, not a failure. Also check:

    • that the input allowed the test;
    • the flags on the rows and the run, such as class_fdr_unestimated.

    Likewise, an empty islands.tsv or operon_flags.tsv means no window or pair passed. If no gene is detected, the detection-dependent tests are not_estimable.

    An empty result is not evidence of absence. Several tests have little or no power by construction:

    • Low islands. At normal detection rates, the low direction is a structural zero. Even a fully undetected window of 20 TUs cannot reach the critical value. No low island is therefore not evidence that no silenced region exists, unless fewer than about a quarter of genes are undetected (islands).
    • Missed operons. With the shipped decoys (convergent and sandwich pairs), this test made no call in the simulation gate or in any attack. It is near-powerless by construction, so an empty list says nothing about missed operons (operons).
    • Operon discordance. Power is near zero at realistic effect sizes, so few or no flagged pairs is the expected outcome even when discordant pairs exist.
    • GSEA. "No significant set" never means "no enrichment" (see GSEA is conditional on detection).

    A file that is missing usually means its module was switched off. run_manifest.json lists switched-off modules under modules_off, and they write no files. A missing tool layer (for example, geNOMAD without its database) appears as the run flag layer_missing:<layer> in logs/events.jsonl, and the tests that need it are skipped or marked. The plots of a switched-off module or missing layer are skipped with a reason (module_off:<module>, layer_missing:<layer>).

    Wording¶

    GIAP's own text follows one rule: "detected", never "expressed". The report, the plot titles and the column names all use it. Every number in the report that comes from a statistic carries its error estimate or unestimated. Please keep the same wording when you write about your results.
